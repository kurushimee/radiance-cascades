# Supporting techniques

Methods that RC work uses without owning: sampling patterns, direction maps, resamplers and denoisers, GPU data structures, ray traversal, transport math, formats, shading models, metrics and the frameworks implementations are written in. Each entry gives the definition, the formula or algorithm with its usual parameters, the primary reference, and the notes that use it. Methods that are central to RC are summarized here and covered in full in the topic that owns them: direction maps for cascades in [cascade layout and merging](cascade_layout_and_merging.md), jump flooding and screen-space tracing in [scene representation and tracing](scene_representation_and_tracing.md), visibility bitmasks in [related screen-space GI](related_screen_space_gi.md). Background GI methods (irradiance volumes, light propagation volumes, light-field probes, voxel cone tracing, PoE1's screen-space GI) are in [related probe GI](related_probe_gi.md) and [related screen-space GI](related_screen_space_gi.md).

A reference marked "secondary" was read only through a textbook, survey, specification or encyclopedia page, not in the original.

## Sampling and noise

**Low-discrepancy sequences.** Deterministic point sets in `[0,1)^s` that fill the cube more evenly than random points, so averages converge faster than plain Monte Carlo (quasi-Monte Carlo). The star discrepancy `D*_N = sup_B |A(B)/N − vol(B)|` over boxes anchored at the origin measures the worst mismatch between the share of points in a box and its volume, and the Koksma-Hlawka inequality bounds the integration error by `V(f)·D*_N`, with `V` the Hardy-Krause variation of `f`. Reference: Niederreiter, *Random Number Generation and Quasi-Monte Carlo Methods*, SIAM 1992 (secondary). RC traces fixed ray sets, so these matter wherever a renderer jitters probe positions or directions between frames or across probes.

**R2.** Roberts's additive recurrence `x_n = fract(0.5 + n·(1/g, 1/g²))` with `g ≈ 1.3247`, the plastic number (the real root of `g³ = g + 1`), the 2D analogue of the golden-ratio sequence. Every prefix is well spread and it costs one multiply-add. Used for probe or ray jitter in [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md), the [Split RC implementations](../sources/2026_split_rc_implementations.md), [HDDAGI](../sources/2026_godot_hddagi_dynamic_gi.md), [RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md) (EMA jitter) and the [line-sweep solvers](../sources/2026_sweep_and_short_characteristics_solvers.md).

**Halton sequence.** Coordinate `i` of point `n` is the radical inverse of `n` in the `i`-th prime base: `φ_b(n) = Σ d_k·b^(−k−1)` for `n = Σ d_k·b^k`, the base-`b` digits mirrored across the radix point. Reference: Halton, *Numerische Mathematik* 2, 84-90, 1960. Used in [GIBS](../sources/2021_seed_gibs_surfel_gi.md), [GI-1.0](../sources/2023_boisse_gi10_two_level_radiance_cache.md) and [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md).

**Hammersley set.** A fixed `N`-point set whose first coordinate is `k/N` and whose others are Halton radical inverses in the first primes: `x_k = (k/N, φ_2(k), φ_3(k), …)`. Not extensible, since `N` is fixed. Reference: Hammersley, "Monte Carlo methods for solving multivariable problems", *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 86(3), 844-874, 1960. Used in Sannikov's LegitEngine SSVGI (16 samples over 4×4 tiles, [videos](../sources/2022_sannikov_youtube_rc_videos.md)), [Brixelizer GI](../sources/2024_amd_brixelizer_gi.md) and [HDDAGI](../sources/2026_godot_hddagi_dynamic_gi.md).

**Sobol sequence and Owen scrambling.** Sobol is a base-2 digital sequence: `x_{n,i} = x_{n−1,i} XOR v_{k,i}`, with `k` the position of the lowest zero bit of `n − 1` and direction numbers `v` from published tables (Joe and Kuo for many dimensions). Owen scrambling permutes each digit by a permutation that depends on the higher digits, which keeps the stratification and removes the structured artifacts. Burley's hash-based form reverses the bits, applies a seeded Laine-Karras permutation (add the seed, then four xor-multiply rounds with 0x6c50b47c, 0xb82f1e52, 0xc7afe638, 0x8d22f6e6) and reverses them back. References: Sobol, *USSR Comput. Math. Math. Phys.* 7, 86-112, 1967 (secondary); Owen, "Randomly permuted (t,m,s)-nets and (t,s)-sequences", Springer LNS 106, 1995 (secondary); Burley, "Practical Hash-based Owen Scrambling", JCGT 9(4), 2020. Used in [light transport alternatives](../sources/2026_light_transport_alternatives.md) and [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md).

**Cranley-Patterson rotation.** Adds one uniform random vector `c` to every point of a deterministic rule modulo 1: `Q_c f = (1/N) Σ f({x_j + c})`. Averaging `q` independent shifts gives an unbiased estimate, and their spread estimates the error. Reference: Cranley and Patterson, "Randomization of number theoretic methods for multiple integration", *SIAM J. Numer. Anal.* 13, 904-914, 1976 (secondary). A per-frame or per-probe rotation of a fixed RC direction set is this shift. Used in the [Split RC implementations](../sources/2026_split_rc_implementations.md).

**Vogel spiral.** Point `n` of a disk at radius `c·√n` and angle `n·α` with the golden angle `α = π(3 − √5) ≈ 2.39996` rad (137.508°), which spreads points without radial bands. Reference: Vogel, "A better way to construct the sunflower head", *Mathematical Biosciences* 44(3-4), 179-189, 1979 (secondary).

**Spherical Fibonacci points.** `N` points on the sphere with heights `z_k = 1 − (2k + 1)/N` and azimuths `φ_k = 2πk/Φ`, `Φ = (1 + √5)/2`, which gives near-uniform coverage for any `N`. Keinert et al. add an inverse map that finds the nearest point in constant time without a table. Reference: Keinert, Innmann, Sänger and Stamminger, "Spherical Fibonacci Mapping", ACM TOG 34(6), article 193, 2015 (formula secondary, from Goebl et al., arXiv 1811.01534). These sets do not nest: the directions of `N` points are not a subset of those of `4N`, so a cascade cannot average child directions into a parent, which is why [Rulkens](../sources/2025_rulkens_rc_medical_volume_data_thesis.md) rejected them. Used for ray directions in [DDGI](../sources/2019_majercik_ddgi.md), [erhe](../sources/2026_erhe_world_space_rc.md), [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md) and the [voxel 3D repositories](../sources/2026_voxel_3d_rc_repositories.md); golden-angle spirals over the sphere appear in [BeansEngine](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#joelmartin-devbeansengine-rc-removed-from-history) and tmpvar's 3D direction comparison.

**Blue noise and void-and-cluster.** A blue-noise pattern has little low-frequency energy, so its error looks like fine, even grain. Void-and-cluster builds a blue-noise threshold array: start from a random binary pattern, score cells with a Gaussian-weighted neighbour sum on a toroidal grid, move minority pixels from the tightest cluster into the largest void until balanced, then rank every pixel. Reference: Ulichney, "The void-and-cluster method for dither array generation", Proc. SPIE 1913, 332-343, 1993 (secondary). Used for ray jitter in the [2D repositories](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#2d-variants-worth-knowing), [GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md), [volumetric RC](../sources/2026_volumetric_rc.md), [GIBS](../sources/2021_seed_gibs_surfel_gi.md), [daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md) and the [Unity and Unreal plugins](../sources/2026_unity_unreal_rc_plugins.md).

**PCG and GPU hashes.** PCG pairs a 64-bit LCG (`state·6364136223846793005 + inc`, `inc` odd) with an output permutation that hides its weak low bits; PCG32's XSH-RR output is `rotr32(((state >> 18) ^ state) >> 27, state >> 59)`. Jarzynski and Olano's survey of GPU hashes found the PCG-based hash a good default; its 32-bit form is `s = x·747796405 + 2891336453; w = ((s >> ((s >> 28) + 4)) ^ s)·277803737; return (w >> 22) ^ w`. References: O'Neill, HMC-CS-2014-0905, 2014; Jarzynski and Olano, "Hash Functions for GPU Rendering", JCGT 9(3), 21-38, 2020. Used in [Coppen's surfel RC](../sources/2025_coppen_surfel_radiance_cascades.md), [Solari](../sources/2025_jms55_bevy_solari.md), [Peters](../sources/2025_peters_jackknife_transmittance.md), the [GPU hash tables note](../sources/2026_gpu_hash_tables_and_sorting.md), [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md), [Split RC implementations](../sources/2026_split_rc_implementations.md) and [Reservoir Cascades](../sources/2026_you_reservoir_cascades.md).

**xxHash32.** A fast non-cryptographic 32-bit hash: four accumulators consume 16-byte stripes (`acc = rotl(acc + lane·P2, 13)·P1`), merge as `rotl1 + rotl7 + rotl12 + rotl18`, add the length, fold the tail, and avalanche with `xor >> 15, ·P2, xor >> 13, ·P3, xor >> 16`, with primes P1 to P5 = 0x9E3779B1, 0x85EBCA77, 0xC2B2AE3D, 0x27D4EB2F, 0x165667B1. Reference: Collet, xxHash specification. Used for surfel and probe hashing in [Coppen's surfel RC](../sources/2025_coppen_surfel_radiance_cascades.md) and [GPU hash tables](../sources/2026_gpu_hash_tables_and_sorting.md).

**Jenkins hashes.** One-at-a-time: for each byte `h += b; h += h << 10; h ^= h >> 6`, then `h += h << 3; h ^= h >> 11; h += h << 15`. lookup3 mixes three words with rotation rounds (mix: 4, 6, 8, 16, 19, 4; final: 14, 11, 25, 16, 4, 14, 24). References: Jenkins, "A Hash Function for Hash Table Lookup", *Dr. Dobb's Journal*, 1997; lookup3.c, 2006. Used in [SHaRC](../sources/2019_binder_path_space_filtering_and_sharc.md) and [Osborne's radiative transfer](../sources/2024_osborne_radiance_cascades_radiative_transfer.md).

**Interleaved gradient noise.** `IGN(x, y) = fract(52.9829189·fract(0.06711056·x + 0.00583715·y))` from pixel coordinates, no texture fetch. Designed for TAA, where its pattern behaves well across frames. Reference: Jimenez, "Next Generation Post Processing in Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare", SIGGRAPH 2014 Advances course (formula secondary). Used in [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md).

**Valve's animated dither.** A per-pixel RGB offset added before 8-bit output to hide banding: `s = dot((171, 231), p + t)`, `v = fract(s/(103, 71, 97)) − 0.5`, output `v/255·0.375`, with `p` the pixel and `t` the time. Reference: Vlachos, "Advanced VR Rendering", GDC 2015. It fixed RC banding on very dark 2D backgrounds with the least change in perceived brightness ([discussions](../sources/2026_rc_discussions.md#implementer-notes)).

**Arvo's random rotations.** A uniform random rotation from three uniforms: `θ = 2πu1`, `φ = 2πu2`, `z = 2u3`, `v = (√z·sin φ, √z·cos φ, √(2 − z))`, and `M = (v·vᵀ − I)·R_z(−θ)`, a Householder reflection composed with a rotation about z. Reference: Arvo, "Fast Random Rotation Matrices", *Graphics Gems III*, 117-120, 1992 (algorithm secondary). [DDGI](../sources/2019_majercik_ddgi.md) rotates its Fibonacci ray set this way each frame.

## Direction parameterizations

**Octahedral map.** A unit vector is divided by its L1 norm, which puts it on an octahedron; the upper half projects straight down onto the square, and the lower half folds outward over the diagonals: `p = n/(|x| + |y| + |z|)`, and if `z < 0`, `p.xy = (1 − |p.y|, 1 − |p.x|)·sign(p.xy)`. Decoding sets `z = 1 − |x| − |y|`, shifts `x, y` by `saturate(−z)` toward the center and normalizes. The map is continuous across its edges with a mirrored wrap, so a border of copied texels makes hardware bilinear filtering work. Its solid angle per unit square area is `|J| = |p|^(−3)` with `|p|² = 1 + 2·min(1 − |x| − |y|, 0) − 2|x| − 2|y| + 2|xy| + 2x² + 2y²` on `[−1, 1]²` ([RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md)); it integrates to 4π, equals 1 at the six octahedron vertices and `3√3 ≈ 5.2` at the eight face centers, so texels near face centers cover about 5.2 times the solid angle of texels at the vertices (verified numerically). An unweighted mean of octahedral texels is therefore not the mean over the sphere. References: Engelhardt and Dachsbacher, "Octahedron Environment Maps", VMV 2008; Meyer et al., "On Floating-Point Normal Vectors", CGF 29(4), 2010; Cigolle et al., JCGT 3(2), 2014 ([notes](../sources/2014_cigolle_octahedral_unit_vectors.md)); Narkowicz's encoding post, 2014. The most common direction layout in 3D RC: [tmpvar's 3D grid](../sources/2024_tmpvar_rc_3d_grid.md), [Gavras](../sources/2025_gavras_screen_probes_world_intervals_thesis.md), [Coppen's surfel RC](../sources/2025_coppen_surfel_radiance_cascades.md), [erhe](../sources/2026_erhe_world_space_rc.md), the [voxel repositories](../sources/2026_voxel_3d_rc_repositories.md), [DDGI](../sources/2019_majercik_ddgi.md), [light-field probes](../sources/2017_mcguire_light_field_probes.md) and many more.

**Equal-area octahedral map (Clarberg).** A map of the square to the sphere with constant Jacobian, so every texel covers the same solid angle and a 2×2 child block is exactly a quarter of its parent at every level ([notes](../sources/2008_clarberg_equal_area_sphere_mapping.md)). Used in [Dell'Ova](../sources/2025_dellova_rc_performance_scaling_thesis.md), [Kaméus](../sources/2025_kameus_spwi_probe_placement_thesis.md), [Split RC](../sources/2026_freeman_split_radiance_cascades.md), [Coppen's surfel RC](../sources/2025_coppen_surfel_radiance_cascades.md) and the [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md) and [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md) findings. RCPG weighs it against the plain octahedral map in [sampling and guiding](rc_for_sampling_and_guiding.md).

**HEALPix.** Equal-area, iso-latitude pixels in a hierarchy of 12·4^k cells ([notes](../sources/2005_gorski_healpix.md)); its nesting matches RC's ×4 directions per 3D cascade. Mentioned in [DexRT](../sources/2025_osborne_dexrt_ray_acceleration.md), [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md) and [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md).

**Latitude-longitude map.** Texel `(u, v)` holds `θ = π(v + 0.5)/H`, `φ = 2π(u + 0.5)/W`, covering `(2π²/(W·H))·sin θ` steradians, so rows near the poles must be weighted by `sin θ` when averaging or importance sampling. References: Blinn and Newell, "Texture and reflection in computer generated images", CACM 19(10), 542-547, 1976 (secondary); PBRT 3rd ed., section 12.6. Used by [KingTheFifth's screen probes](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#screen-probes-with-voxel-intervals-kingthefifth), [Dell'Ova](../sources/2025_dellova_rc_performance_scaling_thesis.md) and the [voxel repositories](../sources/2026_voxel_3d_rc_repositories.md).

**Cube maps and face subdivision.** Six square faces, each a 90° perspective view; a direction picks the face of its largest component and projects the other two by dividing by it. Texels near face corners cover less solid angle than those at face centers. Splitting each face into `2^c × 2^c` cells gives `6·4^c` directions that nest across levels. Reference: Greene, "Environment mapping and other applications of world projections", IEEE CG&A 6(11), 21-29, 1986 (secondary). Used in fad's original Shadertoy (cube-map storage), [Rulkens's cube mapper](../sources/2025_rulkens_rc_medical_volume_data_thesis.md), [layered distance maps](../sources/2007_umenhoffer_layered_distance_maps.md) and others. Wan and Wong's isocube is a six-face layout with near-equal solid angle per texel (GDC 2007).

**Parabolic maps.** Two maps, one per hemisphere, with front coordinates `(x/(1 + z), y/(1 + z))` and back `(x/(1 − z), y/(1 − z))`, remapped to `[0, 1]`; view-independent, no singularity, two textures. Reference: Heidrich and Seidel, "View-independent Environment Maps", Graphics Hardware 1998. Sannikov's "Radiance Cascades Rendered Directly" stores each probe as a small cube map in a parabolic projection ([videos](../sources/2022_sannikov_youtube_rc_videos.md)).

**Spherical harmonics.** Orthonormal functions on the sphere by band `l` (`2l + 1` per band). Projecting distant light onto bands 0 to 2 (9 coefficients) and convolving with the clamped cosine (band factors `π`, `2π/3`, `π/4`) gives irradiance with about 1% average error, written as `E(n) = nᵀMn` for `n = (x, y, z, 1)` with constants `c1 = 0.429043`, `c2 = 0.511664`, `c3 = 0.743125`, `c4 = 0.886227`, `c5 = 0.247708`. L1 (4 coefficients) keeps only bands 0 and 1, enough for irradiance with a dominant direction. Reference: Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan, "An Efficient Representation for Irradiance Environment Maps", SIGGRAPH 2001. RC implementations use SH to compress cascade 0 for the final gather ([mpalenque/sim](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#3d), [AliMoghimii](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#alimoghimiivulkan-ray-tracing-with-radiance-cascades-3d-world-space-rc-on-branches)) and Love and Deepspace stores luma SH plus two chroma scalars ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md)); the paper lists SH as one storage option.

**Ambient cube.** Six scalars or colours, one per axis direction, read with weights `n²` per axis toward the normal's sign. Reference: McTaggart, "Half-Life 2 / Valve Source Shading", GDC 2004 (secondary, through Valve's patent US 7,227,548). Used in [HSGI](../sources/2023_lightspeed_hsgi_hierarchical_surfel_gi.md) and [daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md).

**Spherical Gaussians.** Lobes `G(v) = a·exp(λ(μ·v − 1))` with closed-form integral `2π(a/λ)(1 − e^(−2λ))` and closed-form products, a compact basis for lighting. Reference: Wang et al., "All-Frequency Rendering of Dynamic, Spatially-Varying Reflectance", ACM TOG 28(5), 2009 (secondary); Pettineo and Hill's SG series, 2016.

**Lebedev quadrature.** Sphere quadrature with points in orbits of the octahedral group and weights chosen to integrate polynomials up to a given degree exactly; the weights sum to 4π and the smallest rule is the six axis points. Reference: Lebedev, "Quadratures on a sphere", *Zh. Vychisl. Mat. Mat. Fiz.* 16(2), 293-306, 1976 (secondary). Mentioned as a direction-set option in [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md).

**Girard's theorem.** A spherical triangle with angles `α, β, γ` on the unit sphere has area `α + β + γ − π`. Reference: Girard, *Invention nouvelle en l'algèbre*, 1629 (secondary). [Rulkens](../sources/2025_rulkens_rc_medical_volume_data_thesis.md) uses it for per-direction solid-angle weights of the tetrahedral mapper.

**Geodesic and tetrahedral subdivision.** Split each face of a Platonic solid into triangles and project the vertices onto the sphere; frequency `ν = b + c` and triangulation number `T = b² + bc + c²` classify the result. Splitting every triangle into four per level gives `4·4^c` directions from a tetrahedron or `20·4^c` from an icosahedron, both nesting across levels. Reference: Fuller, US patent 2,682,235, 1954 (icosahedral); Wenninger, *Spherical Models*, 1979 (secondary). [Rulkens](../sources/2025_rulkens_rc_medical_volume_data_thesis.md) uses the tetrahedral form.

**Lambert cylindrical equal-area projection.** `x = λ − λ0`, `y = sin φ`; area is preserved because `dy = cos φ·dφ`. Reference: Lambert, 1772 (secondary). The building block of equal-area sphere maps.

## Resampling and sampling theory

**Importance sampling.** Estimate `∫f` as `(1/n) Σ f(X_i)/p(X_i)` with `X_i ~ p`; unbiased if `p > 0` wherever `f ≠ 0`, low variance when `p` resembles `f`. Reference: PBRT 4th ed., section 2.2.2. RC traces fixed uniform ray sets and so does not importance sample ([discussions](../sources/2026_rc_discussions.md)); the guiding work uses cascades as the `p` ([sampling and guiding](rc_for_sampling_and_guiding.md)).

**Multiple importance sampling.** Combine techniques `i` with `n_i` samples each by weighting each sample with the balance heuristic `w_i = n_i·p_i/Σ_j n_j·p_j` or the power heuristic `w_i = (n_i·p_i)^β/Σ_j (n_j·p_j)^β`, usually `β = 2`. Reference: Veach and Guibas, "Optimally Combining Sampling Techniques for Monte Carlo Rendering", SIGGRAPH 1995. Used in [RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md), [Reservoir Cascades](../sources/2026_you_reservoir_cascades.md) and [Peters](../sources/2025_peters_jackknife_transmittance.md).

**Resampled importance sampling (RIS).** Draw `M` candidates from an easy `p`, weight each by `w_j = p̂(x_j)/p(x_j)` against a target `p̂`, keep one with probability `w_z/Σw_j`, and estimate `f(x_z)/p̂(x_z)·(1/M)·Σw_j`. Reference: Talbot, Cline and Egbert, "Importance Resampling for Global Illumination", EGSR 2005 (formula secondary). Used in [Reservoir Cascades](../sources/2026_you_reservoir_cascades.md), [Solari](../sources/2025_jms55_bevy_solari.md) and [HSGI](../sources/2023_lightspeed_hsgi_hierarchical_surfel_gi.md).

**Weighted reservoir sampling.** A one-item reservoir over a stream keeps item `i` with probability `w_i/Σ_{j≤i} w_j`, storing only the current pick and the running weight sum. References: Chao, *Biometrika* 69(3), 653-656, 1982; Vitter, ACM TOMS 11(1), 37-57, 1985 (both secondary). The reservoir in every ReSTIR variant.

**ReSTIR.** Each pixel streams candidate light samples through a reservoir, then merges reservoirs from neighbours (spatial reuse) and from the previous frame (temporal reuse), so the effective candidate count far exceeds the rays traced; the shading weight is `W = w_sum/(M·p̂(y))`, and the history length `M` is capped. ReSTIR GI applies it to the first indirect bounce's path; generalized RIS (ReSTIR PT) moves samples between pixels through shift mappings with their Jacobians and handles correlated samples and unknown densities. References: Bitterli et al., ACM TOG 39(4), 2020; Ouyang et al., CGF (HPG 2021); Lin et al., "Generalized Resampled Importance Sampling: Foundations of ReSTIR", ACM TOG 41(4), 2022. Its temporal reuse is what single-frame RC gives up; [Reservoir Cascades](../sources/2026_you_reservoir_cascades.md) puts reservoirs inside cascades, and [kajiya](../sources/2022_stachowiak_kajiya_gi.md), [Solari](../sources/2025_jms55_bevy_solari.md) and Love and Deepspace's second version used it ([related radiance caches](related_radiance_caches.md)).

**Permutation sampling.** kajiya's variant of temporal reservoir reuse that reads the history at a permuted pixel offset to break correlation between neighbours ([kajiya](../sources/2022_stachowiak_kajiya_gi.md)).

**Next-event estimation.** Estimate direct light by sampling a point on a light, tracing a shadow ray and converting the area density to solid angle, `(1/N) Σ f·L_d·|cos θ|/p(ω)`, and combine it with BSDF sampling by MIS so no light is counted twice. Reference: PBRT 3rd ed., section 14.3. The community wiki files vlam as RC-guided NEE, but vlam guides path directions and samples no lights; RCPG guides indirect directions with cascades and combines them with NEE by MIS ([sampling and guiding](rc_for_sampling_and_guiding.md#cascades-and-light-sampling)).

**Path guiding.** Learn an approximation of incident radiance while rendering and sample directions from it; Müller et al. store it in an SD-tree (a spatial binary tree whose leaves hold directional quadtrees), refined with unbiased samples. References: Müller, Gross and Novák, "Practical Path Guiding for Efficient Light-Transport Simulation", CGF 36(4), 2017; Vorba et al., "Path Guiding in Production", SIGGRAPH 2019 course. [Sampling and guiding](rc_for_sampling_and_guiding.md) covers RC used this way.

**Light trees and stochastic lightcuts.** A BVH over emitters whose nodes aggregate energy, bounds and orientation; one random number walks it, choosing children by estimated importance, and Yuksel's stochastic lightcuts sample the cut instead of fixing it, turning correlated error into noise. References: Conty Estevez and Kulla, "Importance Sampling of Many Lights with Adaptive Tree Splitting", HPG 2018; Yuksel, "Stochastic Lightcuts", HPG 2019. Used in [GIBS](../sources/2021_seed_gibs_surfel_gi.md) and named in [RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md).

**Stochastic texture filtering and stochastic interpolation.** Pick one texel from the filter kernel per lookup, with probability equal to its weight, instead of blending before shading; temporal accumulation or a denoiser removes the noise. Reference: Pharr, Wronski, Salvi and Fajardo, "Filtering After Shading With Stochastic Texture Filtering", PACMCGIT 7(1), 2024. kajiya used stochastic interpolation between cache entries to hide blockiness ([kajiya](../sources/2022_stachowiak_kajiya_gi.md)), Enshrouded blends overlapping cascades stochastically ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md#enshrouded-a-cascaded-probe-cache)), and 2D RC demos add noise to cascade interpolation for a painted look. A single-frame RC without a denoiser cannot use it (inference).

## Denoising, upscaling and temporal reuse

**Bilateral filter.** A weighted average whose weights fall off with both distance and value difference, `BF[I]_p = (1/W_p) Σ_q G_s(|p − q|)·G_r(|I_p − I_q|)·I_q`, so edges survive. Reference: Tomasi and Manduchi, "Bilateral Filtering for Gray and Color Images", ICCV 1998, 839-846. Joint (cross) bilateral upsampling computes the range weight on a full-resolution guide such as depth: Kopf, Cohen, Lischinski and Uyttendaele, "Joint Bilateral Upsampling", ACM TOG 26(3), 2007. Depth- and normal-weighted versions are how screen-space RC upsamples and interpolates probes ([Gavras](../sources/2025_gavras_screen_probes_world_intervals_thesis.md), [Kaméus](../sources/2025_kameus_spwi_probe_placement_thesis.md), the paper's SPWI variant); the paper argues that band-limited interval data needs only plain linear interpolation ([core theory](core_theory.md)).

**Depth-aware upscaling.** Joint bilateral upsampling with depth (and often normals) as the guide; edge-aware upscaling is the general form, with any edge signal (depth, normals, colour) as the guide. Variants: Sannikov's 4XXSWS and MXjcWK choose subsets of the bilinear footprint by depth ([Shadertoys](../sources/2023_rc_shadertoys.md#depth-aware-upscaling-and-occlusion)), pixelmager's gist weights by depth ([notes](../sources/2021_pixelmager_depth_aware_upscaling.md)); details in [3D screen space](3d_screen_space.md).

**Edge-avoiding à-trous wavelet filter.** Repeated passes of a B3-spline kernel whose tap spacing doubles each pass, multiplied by edge-stopping weights on colour, normal and depth, which approximates a large bilateral filter cheaply. Reference: Dammertz, Sewtz, Hanika and Lensch, HPG 2010 (secondary). Used in [HDDAGI](../sources/2026_godot_hddagi_dynamic_gi.md), [Phreakyx](../sources/2026_phreakyx_godot_rc_gdextension.md) and lixu0073/2DGI.

**SVGF.** Temporal accumulation with motion vectors, a luminance variance estimate from the history, and an à-trous filter steered by that variance, for 1 sample per pixel path tracing. Reference: Schied et al., "Spatiotemporal Variance-Guided Filtering", HPG 2017. Named in [GIBS](../sources/2021_seed_gibs_surfel_gi.md), [HDDAGI](../sources/2026_godot_hddagi_dynamic_gi.md) and Love and Deepspace's talk.

**BMFR.** Regresses noisy colour on noise-free feature buffers (albedo, normal, position) per image block with polynomial features, solved by a fast augmented QR factorization with stochastic regularization. Each pixel reads only its own features, which is why Love and Deepspace chose it over SVGF for its ReSTIR version ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md)). Reference: Koskela et al., "Blockwise Multi-Order Feature Regression for Real-Time Path Tracing Reconstruction", ACM TOG 38(5), 2019.

**OIDN.** Intel Open Image Denoise, an Apache-2.0 library of pretrained neural denoisers taking colour plus optional albedo and normal buffers. Used offline for references in [Split RC](../sources/2026_freeman_split_radiance_cascades.md) and [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md) findings.

**DLSS Ray Reconstruction.** NVIDIA's neural denoiser in DLSS 3.5 (2023), which replaces hand-tuned ray-tracing denoisers using temporal and spatial data; no algorithm is published. Used by [Solari](../sources/2025_jms55_bevy_solari.md).

**Pull-push.** Fill holes in a sparse image through a pyramid: the pull pass averages known texels into coarser levels weighted by coverage, and the push pass fills unknown fine texels from the level above. References: Gortler et al., "The Lumigraph", SIGGRAPH 1996 ([notes](../sources/1996_light_field_and_lumigraph.md)); Grossman and Dally, "Point Sample Rendering", 1998 (secondary). Used in Sannikov's imperfect shadow maps, bakjedev's 2D RC (1-3-3-1 weights) and [light transport alternatives](../sources/2026_light_transport_alternatives.md).

**Temporal anti-aliasing and reprojection.** Jitter each frame by a sub-pixel offset and blend into a history reprojected with motion vectors, `h_t = a·c + (1 − a)·reproject(h_{t−1})`, rejecting or clamping stale history. Reference: Karis, "High-Quality Temporal Supersampling", SIGGRAPH 2014 Advances course. Neighbourhood clamping (colour box clamping) limits the history to the min/max (or mean ± γσ, variance clipping) of the current 3×3 neighbourhood, tighter in YCoCg: Salvi, "An Excursion in Temporal Supersampling", GDC 2016 (secondary). PoE2 ships without TAA ([history](history_and_landscape.md#exilecon-2023-and-path-of-exile-2)).

**YCoCg.** `Y = R/4 + G/2 + B/4`, `Co = R/2 − B/2`, `Cg = −R/4 + G/2 − B/4`; the lifting form YCoCg-R (`Co = R − B; t = B + Co/2; Cg = G − t; Y = t + Cg/2`) is exactly invertible on integers. Reference: Malvar and Sullivan, JVT-I014r3, 2003. Used for history clamping and compression in [SHaRC](../sources/2019_binder_path_space_filtering_and_sharc.md) and [volumetric RC](../sources/2026_volumetric_rc.md).

**Hysteresis.** The weight `h` on the old value in an exponential moving average, `x_t = h·x_{t−1} + (1 − h)·s_t`. After `n` frames a change has decayed to `h^n`, so reaching 90% of a change in `n` frames needs `h ≤ 0.1^(1/n)` (derived). Reference: Majercik et al., DDGI, JCGT 8(2), 2019 ([notes](../sources/2019_majercik_ddgi.md)). Used in [light-field probes](../sources/2017_mcguire_light_field_probes.md), [GI-1.0](../sources/2023_boisse_gi10_two_level_radiance_cache.md), [erhe](../sources/2026_erhe_world_space_rc.md) and praxis's target ([history](history_and_landscape.md#adoption-shipped-tried-and-turned-down)).

**Temporal Super Resolution.** Unreal's temporal upscaler: jittered rendering at a lower resolution, motion-vector reprojection, parallax-based disocclusion detection, shading rejection, flicker analysis and history resurrection; convergence takes about `1000/(ScreenPercentage²·fps)` ms, with `r.TSR.History.SampleCount` 16 by default. Reference: Epic, "Temporal Super Resolution in Unreal Engine" documentation. Lumen leans on it ([Lumen](../sources/2022_wright_lumen_siggraph_advances.md)).

**SMAA and FXAA.** Post-process anti-aliasing from the final image: SMAA detects edges, measures their pattern with search and area lookup textures and blends neighbours (Jimenez, Echevarria, Sousa and Gutierrez, CGF 31(2), 2012); FXAA is a cheaper single-pass luma-edge filter (Lottes, NVIDIA, 2009). Single-frame GI pairs with these instead of TAA: Blackline's demos use FXAA 3 ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md#other-videos-checked)); Godot ships SMAA ([Godot GI internals](../sources/2026_godot_gi_internals.md)).

**Mip bias under upscaling.** An upscaler renders at lower resolution, so textures are sampled with a negative bias, `mipBias = log2(render/display) − 1` in AMD's FSR guidance. Reference: AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution 3.1.4 documentation. Named in [on-surface caches](../sources/2024_tatzgern_on_surface_radiance_caches.md) and [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md).

## GPU data structures

**Hi-Z and min/max depth pyramids.** A mip chain of the depth buffer where each texel keeps the min (or max, or both) of the 2×2 block below, so a ray or query can skip a whole region at once and descend only where a hit is possible. The Z pyramid comes from Greene, Kass and Miller, "Hierarchical Z-Buffer Visibility", SIGGRAPH 1993; Uludag's "Hi-Z Screen-Space Cone-Traced Reflections" (GPU Pro 5, 2014) made it the standard screen-space ray structure, and AMD's single-pass downsampler builds up to about 12 levels in one dispatch. Screen-space RC uses min/max pyramids both for tracing and to place probe pairs at the min and max depth of a tile ([3D screen space](3d_screen_space.md), [scene representation](scene_representation_and_tracing.md)).

**Morton order.** Interleave the bits of integer coordinates so points close in space get close keys: in 2D, spread 16-bit `x` with `x = (x ^ (x << 8)) & 0x00ff00ff; x = (x ^ (x << 4)) & 0x0f0f0f0f; x = (x ^ (x << 2)) & 0x33333333; x = (x ^ (x << 1)) & 0x55555555` and combine `(spread(y) << 1) | spread(x)`; in 3D use shifts 16, 8, 4, 2 with masks 0xff0000ff, 0x0300f00f, 0x030c30c3, 0x09249249. Reference: Morton, IBM Ottawa technical report, 1966 (secondary); Giesen's "Decoding Morton codes", 2009. Used for sparse storage in [DexRT](../sources/2025_osborne_dexrt_ray_acceleration.md), probe ordering in [tmpvar's 3D grid](../sources/2024_tmpvar_rc_3d_grid.md), BVH builds in [compute BVH libraries](../sources/2026_compute_bvh_libraries.md), hash keys in [GPU hash tables](../sources/2026_gpu_hash_tables_and_sorting.md) and layouts in kornelski's Bevy RC ([Bevy repositories](../sources/2024_bevy_2d_rc_repositories.md)).

**Clipmaps and toroidal addressing.** Nested fixed-size windows, one per level, centred on the viewer with doubling extent; logical texel `(u, v)` lives at `(u mod N, v mod N)`, so a moving window rewrites only the newly exposed strip. Reference: Tanner, Migdal and Jones, "The Clipmap: A Virtual Mipmap", SIGGRAPH 1998; Asirvatham and Hoppe, GPU Gems 2, ch. 2, 2005. The usual container for camera-following world-space probes and voxels: [Lumen](../sources/2022_wright_lumen_siggraph_advances.md), [HSGI](../sources/2023_lightspeed_hsgi_hierarchical_surfel_gi.md), [daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md), [kajiya](../sources/2022_stachowiak_kajiya_gi.md), Love and Deepspace, the [voxel repositories](../sources/2026_voxel_3d_rc_repositories.md) and reduz's view that open-world RC needs clipmaps.

**Froxels.** Cells of a 3D grid aligned with the view frustum (frustum voxels), small near the camera and large far away, used to inject, light and integrate participating media front to back. References: Wronski, "Volumetric Fog", SIGGRAPH 2014 Advances course; Hillaire, "Physically Based and Unified Volumetric Rendering in Frostbite", SIGGRAPH 2015 Advances course (8×8-pixel tiles, 64 depth slices by default). Used in [Lumen](../sources/2022_wright_lumen_siggraph_advances.md), [daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md) and Godot's volumetric fog ([Godot GI internals](../sources/2026_godot_gi_internals.md)).

**VDB and GVDB.** A sparse grid as a shallow, wide tree of fixed-size blocks (by default 8³ leaves under 16³ and 32³ internal nodes), with average constant-time random access; GVDB puts the layout on the GPU with a memory pool and hierarchical ray traversal. References: Museth, "VDB: High-Resolution Sparse Volumes with Dynamic Topology", ACM TOG 32(3), 2013; Hoetzlein, "GVDB", HPG 2016. DexRT's sparse storage is VDB-like ([wiki](../sources/2026_rc_community_site_and_wiki.md#dexrt)).

**Sparse voxel octrees.** Octrees that store only occupied octants and are ray cast directly. Reference: Laine and Karras, "Efficient Sparse Voxel Octrees", I3D 2010. Sannikov's 2021 SVO streaming and Crassin's GigaVoxels ([notes](../sources/2011_crassin_gigavoxels_thesis.md)) build on them.

**GPU hash tables.** Open addressing with linear probing (`slot = (h(key) + i) mod N`) places a colliding key in the next free slot; cuckoo hashing gives each key `k` candidate slots and evicts on insert, so lookups probe at most `k` slots. Bucketed cuckoo with three functions reaches load factors near 0.98. References: Alcantara et al., "Real-time parallel hashing on the GPU", SIGGRAPH Asia 2009; Awad et al., "Better GPU Hash Tables", arXiv 2108.07232, 2021. The storage of sparse 3D RC, SHaRC and GI-1.0 ([GPU hash tables](../sources/2026_gpu_hash_tables_and_sorting.md), [scene representation](scene_representation_and_tracing.md)).

**Prefix sums and decoupled look-back.** An exclusive scan gives each element the sum of those before it; Blelloch's tree form does O(n) work in O(log n) steps, and Merrill and Garland's single-pass scan has each tile publish its aggregate and look back over predecessors' status, about `2n` memory traffic. References: Blelloch, CMU-CS-90-190, 1990; Merrill and Garland, NVR-2016-002, 2016. Used for compaction and allocation in [Coppen's surfel RC](../sources/2025_coppen_surfel_radiance_cascades.md), [Solari](../sources/2025_jms55_bevy_solari.md) and [GPU hash tables](../sources/2026_gpu_hash_tables_and_sorting.md).

**Radix sort and Onesweep.** LSD radix sort orders keys digit by digit with a histogram, a scan and a scatter per pass; Onesweep folds the scan into one pass with decoupled look-back, about `2n` traffic per digit (29.4 GKeys/s for 256M 32-bit keys on an A100). Reference: Adinets and Merrill, arXiv 2206.01784, 2022. Used to sort rays, probes and BVH primitives ([GPU hash tables](../sources/2026_gpu_hash_tables_and_sorting.md), [compute BVH libraries](../sources/2026_compute_bvh_libraries.md)).

**Nanite.** Unreal Engine 5's virtualized geometry: clustered meshes in a hierarchy with per-cluster level of detail, streamed in pages, culled, rasterized and shaded through a visibility buffer. Reference: Karis, Stubbe and Wihlidal, "A Deep Dive into Nanite Virtualized Geometry", SIGGRAPH 2021 Advances course. Sannikov's cluster renderer follows the same rule ([history](history_and_landscape.md#other-work-on-sannikovs-channel)).

**Surface cache cards.** Lumen's surface parameterization: offline capture viewpoints per mesh (12 by default for static meshes, six around pre-skinned bounds for skeletal meshes) from which material properties are recorded and lighting is cached for ray hits. Reference: Wright, Narkowicz and Kelly, "Lumen", SIGGRAPH 2022 Advances course ([notes](../sources/2022_wright_lumen_siggraph_advances.md)); SDFGI's plan ([notes](../sources/2023_linietsky_godot_sdfgi_plan.md)) and [Brixelizer](../sources/2024_amd_brixelizer_gi.md) use similar ideas.

## Ray traversal and intersection

**BVH.** A tree of bounding volumes (usually axis-aligned boxes) over primitives; a ray skips any subtree whose box it misses. References: Rubin and Whitted, SIGGRAPH 1980; Kay and Kajiya, SIGGRAPH 1986 (both secondary). **SAH:** a split costs `K_T + K_I·(SA(L)/SA(V)·N_L + SA(R)/SA(V)·N_R)`, and builders pick the cheapest (Goldsmith and Salmon, IEEE CG&A 7(5), 1987; MacDonald and Booth, *The Visual Computer* 6(3), 1990). **Binned SAH** scores only the `K − 1` boundaries of `K` equal centroid bins along an axis, with `K = 16` near optimal and 4 already reasonable (Wald, IEEE Symposium on Interactive Ray Tracing 2007). **Wide BVHs** (BVH4, BVH8, MBVH) give nodes 4 or 8 children for SIMD and GPU lanes (Wald, Benthin and Boulos 2008). **CWBVH** quantizes the 8 child boxes to 8 bits per plane against a shared origin and exponent, with octant-aware child order, for 1.9 to 2.1× faster incoherent traversal at 35 to 60% of the memory (Ylitie, Karras and Laine, HPG 2017). **PLOC** builds bottom-up by sorting primitives in Morton order and merging mutually nearest clusters within a window of radius `r` (10 to 100) (Meister and Bittner, IEEE TVCG, 2018), and **H-PLOC** does the binary build in one kernel by combining LBVH's Morton ranges with PLOC merges, then collapses to a wide tree in one more (Benthin et al., HPG 2024). BVHs traced in compute are covered in [compute BVH libraries](../sources/2026_compute_bvh_libraries.md) and [scene representation](scene_representation_and_tracing.md).

**BLAS and TLAS.** In hardware ray tracing APIs a bottom-level acceleration structure holds one geometry's triangles or boxes, and a top-level structure holds instances (BLAS reference, transform, mask, hit-group offset); rays start at the TLAS and are transformed into each instance. Reference: Microsoft, DirectX Raytracing functional spec.

**Ray queries.** `VK_KHR_ray_query` (and DXR 1.1 inline ray tracing) lets any shader stage, compute included, traverse an acceleration structure with a loop of query steps and read the committed hit. Reference: Khronos, VK_KHR_ray_query, 2020. Godot's `RenderingDevice` does not expose it, which is why software traversal matters there ([Godot integration](godot_integration.md)).

**Aila and Laine traversal.** Persistent threads that fill the GPU once and pull rays from a global pool with an atomic counter, with "while-while" loops (traverse internal nodes until a leaf, then test its primitives), closing most of the gap to a simulated upper bound. Reference: Aila and Laine, "Understanding the Efficiency of Ray Traversal on GPUs", HPG 2009.

**Slab test.** A ray hits an axis-aligned box when the largest per-axis entry `t` is at most the smallest exit `t`; with `t1,2 = (min_i or max_i − O_i)·(1/D_i)` swapped so `t1 ≤ t2`, IEEE infinities from `1/0` make zero components work without branches. Reference: Williams, Barrus, Morley and Shirley, "An Efficient and Robust Ray-Box Intersection Algorithm", JGT 10(1), 2005.

**Möller-Trumbore.** Solve `O + tD = V0 + u·e1 + v·e2` by Cramer's rule: `det = e1·(D × e2)`, `u = s·(D × e2)/det`, `v = D·(s × e1)/det`, `t = e2·(s × e1)/det` with `s = O − V0`; hit if `u, v ≥ 0`, `u + v ≤ 1`, `t > 0`. Reference: Möller and Trumbore, JGT 2(1), 21-28, 1997. **Baldwin-Weber** stores a per-triangle transform to barycentric space, trading memory for a faster test (1 to 6% faster frames in PBRT): JCGT 5(3), 2016. **Watertight** (Woop, Benthin and Wald, JCGT 2(1), 2013) shears the triangle so the ray runs along +z and uses 2D edge functions, so rays never slip through shared edges.

**DDA grid traversal.** Step a ray through a uniform grid one cell at a time, always crossing the nearest boundary: per axis keep `step = ±1`, `tMax` (the `t` of the next boundary) and `tDelta` (the `t` per cell), and advance the axis with the smallest `tMax`. Reference: Amanatides and Woo, "A Fast Voxel Traversal Algorithm for Ray Tracing", Eurographics 1987. 2D RC on bitmaps ([2D implementations](2d_implementations.md)) and voxel 3D RC use it.

**Hierarchical DDA.** A DDA at each level of a VDB tree, stepping by that level's node size, so empty nodes are skipped whole. Reference: Museth, "Hierarchical Digital Differential Analyzer for Efficient Ray-Marching in OpenVDB", SIGGRAPH 2014 talk. Godot's HDDAGI is named after it ([HDDAGI](../sources/2026_godot_hddagi_dynamic_gi.md)).

**Sphere tracing.** March along the ray by the distance-field value, a step that cannot cross the surface: `t += f(o + t·d)` until `f < ε` or `t` passes a limit. It needs only a Lipschitz bound on `f`. Reference: Hart, "Sphere Tracing", *The Visual Computer* 12(10), 527-545, 1996. The tracer of most 2D RC (JFA fields) and SDF-based 3D RC.

**Bresenham's line algorithm.** Step the major axis once per pixel and the minor axis when an integer error term crosses zero (`V0 = 2Δb − Δa`; `V += 2Δb − 2Δa` on a diagonal step, `V += 2Δb` otherwise), with no multiplication or division. Reference: Bresenham, IBM Systems Journal 4(1), 25-30, 1965. Exact integer line tests in Sannikov's IntegerBlockHRC and line-space tilings in [HRC](holographic_rc.md#block-cascades) rest on the same lattice-line idea.

## Distance fields and voxelization

**Jump flooding.** Approximates a Voronoi diagram and distance transform in about `log2 n` passes: each pixel keeps the nearest of the seeds held by its 8 neighbours at step `k`, with `k` halving from `n/2` to 1. JFA+1 adds a final step-1 pass, JFA+2 adds steps 2 and 1, JFA² adds `log n` passes, and 1+JFA runs one step-1 pass first, with far fewer errors than JFA+1. References: Rong and Tan, I3D 2006 ([notes](../sources/2006_rong_tan_jump_flooding.md)); Rong and Tan, ISVD 2007. The scene representation of most 2D RC ([2D implementations](2d_implementations.md)).

**Eikonal equation and fast sweeping.** `|∇u| = f` describes distance (`f = 1`) or arrival time. Fast sweeping solves its Godunov upwind discretization `[(u − u_xmin)⁺]² + [(u − u_ymin)⁺]² = f²h²` with Gauss-Seidel sweeps in alternating orderings (4 in 2D, `2^n` in `n` dimensions), O(N) with a sweep count independent of grid size. Reference: Zhao, *Mathematics of Computation* 74(250), 603-627, 2005. **Fast marching** finalizes nodes in order of increasing arrival time from a heap: Sethian, PNAS 93(4), 1591-1595, 1996.

**GPU distance fields from meshes.** DiFi rasterizes each primitive's distance function into grid slices, culled and clamped by bounds on its Voronoi region. Reference: Sud, Otaduy and Manocha, CGF 23(3), 2004.

**Conservative rasterization.** Rasterize every pixel a triangle touches (overestimation) or only those it fully covers (underestimation); D3D12 tier 1 allows a 1/2-pixel uncertainty, tiers 2 and 3 1/256 pixel, and tier 3 adds inner coverage. Reference: Hasselgren, Akenine-Möller and Ohlsson, GPU Gems 2, ch. 42, 2005. **GPU voxelization:** project each triangle along its dominant axis, rasterize it conservatively and write fragments to a 3D image, averaging with atomics (Crassin and Green, "Octree-Based Sparse Voxelization Using the GPU Hardware Rasterizer", *OpenGL Insights*, 2012). How [voxel 3D RC](../sources/2026_voxel_3d_rc_repositories.md), [KingTheFifth](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#screen-probes-with-voxel-intervals-kingthefifth) and [HDDAGI](../sources/2026_godot_hddagi_dynamic_gi.md) build their grids.

**Draco.** Google's mesh and point-cloud compression library: position quantization bits (`-qp`, default 11), compression level (`-cl`, default 7), Edgebreaker or sequential connectivity. Used for assets in [three-rc](../sources/2025_bennett_three_rc.md).

## Visibility, shadows and screen space

How these methods relate to RC, and the RC-specific variants (visibility-bitmask SSGI, PoE1's GI, line-sweep AO, horizon-based indirect light), are in [related screen-space GI](related_screen_space_gi.md). Godot implementations of SSAO, HBAO, SAO and GTAO in compute, with their parameters, costs and bugs, are in the [compositor examples](../sources/2026_godot_compositor_effect_examples.md).

**Shadow maps, acne and bias.** A shadow map stores the depth of the nearest surface seen from the light; a point is lit if its own light-space depth is not greater. Many receiver points share one texel, and at grazing angles the stored depth sits above part of the surface, so the surface shadows itself in stripes (shadow acne). Depth bias subtracts a constant or slope-scaled offset before comparing (for example `max(0.05·(1 − n·l), 0.005)`); normal offset moves the lookup point along the normal by a multiple of the world-space texel size. Too much bias detaches shadows from their casters ("peter-panning"). Reference: Williams, "Casting curved shadows on curved surfaces", SIGGRAPH 1978 (secondary); no dated origin of the terms acne and normal offset was found. RC rays have the same problem at their start: a ray leaving a surface or a depth-buffer sample hits the surface it starts on, and the fixes are the same offsets ([three-rc](../sources/2025_bennett_three_rc.md), [Gavras](../sources/2025_gavras_screen_probes_world_intervals_thesis.md), [Kaméus](../sources/2025_kameus_spwi_probe_placement_thesis.md), [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md), [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md)).

**Variance shadow maps and the Chebyshev bound.** Store the first two moments of depth per texel, `M1 = E[z]` and `M2 = E[z²]`, which can be filtered and mipmapped linearly. For a receiver at depth `t > μ`, the one-sided Chebyshev (Cantelli) inequality bounds the fraction of the footprint that does not occlude it: `p_max = σ²/(σ² + (t − μ)²)` with `σ² = max(M2 − M1², σ²_min)`, and visibility is 1 for `t ≤ μ`. When two occluders at different depths overlap a footprint the variance is large and the bound stays high behind the nearer one, which shows as light bleeding. References: Donnelly and Lauritzen, "Variance Shadow Maps", I3D 2006 (formula secondary, from GPU Gems 3 ch. 8). The same test on stored mean and squared ray distance per probe direction is how [DDGI](../sources/2019_majercik_ddgi.md), [light-field probes](../sources/2017_mcguire_light_field_probes.md), [HSGI](../sources/2023_lightspeed_hsgi_hierarchical_surfel_gi.md), Enshrouded ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md#enshrouded-a-cascaded-probe-cache)), [GIBS](../sources/2021_seed_gibs_surfel_gi.md) and the [SDFGI plan](../sources/2023_linietsky_godot_sdfgi_plan.md) keep probes from leaking through walls, and Sannikov's PoE1 GI applied it to two screen-space depth moments ([paper](../sources/2023_sannikov_radiance_cascades_paper.md)). RC intervals need no such test inside the cascade, because each interval carries its own transmittance; the question comes back only where a probe is interpolated across a wall (inference, [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md)).

**Summed-area variance shadow maps.** Summed-area tables of `z` and `z²` give the mean and variance over any axis-aligned rectangle from four lookups, so each receiver can use its own filter size for soft shadows. Light bleeding is reduced by mapping `p_max` below a threshold to 0 and rescaling the rest. Reference: Lauritzen, "Summed-Area Variance Shadow Maps", GPU Gems 3, ch. 8, 2007. Sannikov's trapezoid coverage test for depth marching against moments ([Shadertoys](../sources/2023_rc_shadertoys.md)) is a related closed form.

**Moment shadow maps and power moments.** Store four power moments `(E[z], E[z²], E[z³], E[z⁴])` per texel (64 bits, 16-bit quantization works) and bound shadowing from the moment problem, much tighter than the two-moment bound. Reference: Peters and Klein, "Moment Shadow Mapping", I3D 2015; a solver-tolerance flaw in the original comparison was later rerun with the same conclusions. Peters's later transmittance work is in [Peters](../sources/2025_peters_jackknife_transmittance.md); Love and Deepspace blends overlapping surfel hits with four power moments of the hit points ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md)).

**Chebyshev distance.** Unrelated to the bound above: `max(|Δx|, |Δy|, |Δz|)`, the L∞ distance. Split RC sets probe LOD from the binary log of the Chebyshev distance to the camera, which gives fewer artifacts with grid-aligned probes than Euclidean distance ([Split RC](../sources/2026_freeman_split_radiance_cascades.md), [plugins](../sources/2026_unity_unreal_rc_plugins.md)); Sannikov's hemisphere map indexes texel rings by it ([Shadertoys](../sources/2023_rc_shadertoys.md)).

**Bent normals and bent cones.** A bent normal is the mean unoccluded direction over the hemisphere, used in place of the normal to look up ambient light; a bent cone adds a half-angle so the visible region is a cone around it. Klehm et al. compute both in screen space alongside SSAO; GTAO derives the cone from its horizon angles assuming visibility is the same around the bent normal. References: Landis, "Production-Ready Global Illumination", SIGGRAPH 2002 course (secondary); Klehm, Ritschel, Eisemann and Seidel, "Bent Normals and Cones in Screen-space", VMV 2011. Used in [Mayaux](../sources/2018_mayaux_horizon_based_indirect_lighting.md), [Therrien](../sources/2022_therrien_visibility_bitmask_ssgi.md) and [Lumen](../sources/2022_wright_lumen_siggraph_advances.md).

**SSAO.** Ambient occlusion from the depth buffer alone: sample depths around the pixel, count those in front of the surface and average, `AO ≈ 1 − (1/N)Σ occ_i`, with 8 to 16 samples rotated per pixel by a small noise texture and a depth-aware blur to remove the pattern. Reference: Mittring, "Finding Next Gen: CryEngine 2", SIGGRAPH 2007 course (the method credited to Kajalin at Crytek). Godot's AO multiplies indirect light only, as `min(material AO, SSAO)` ([indirect diffuse paths](../sources/2026_godot_indirect_diffuse_paths.md)).

**HBAO.** Treats the depth buffer as a heightfield: for each of several screen directions, march to find the horizon angle above the tangent plane and integrate the occluded part of the hemisphere between them, with a falloff inside radius `R`. Reference: Bavoil, Sainz and Dimitrov, "Image-space horizon-based ambient occlusion", SIGGRAPH 2008 talks (algorithm from NVIDIA's GDC 2008 deck; the talk's equations were not read).

**GTAO.** Splits the hemisphere into screen-space slices and integrates cosine-weighted visibility in closed form between the two horizon angles `h1`, `h2` of each slice: with `γ` the angle of the normal projected into the slice, `a = ¼(−cos(2h1 − γ) + cos γ + 2h1·sin γ) + ¼(−cos(2h2 − γ) + cos γ + 2h2·sin γ)` (the standard form, not checked against the memo). Spatial and temporal filtering spread the slices over pixels and frames, and a thickness heuristic handles thin occluders; 0.5 ms on PS4 at 1080p. Reference: Jimenez, Wu, Pesce and Jarabo, "Practical Realtime Strategies for Accurate Indirect Occlusion", Activision ATVI-TR-16-01, 2016. Intel's XeGTAO uses 3 slices with 3 steps per side at its High preset. GTAO's horizons are what visibility-bitmask SSGI replaces with a bitmask of sectors ([Therrien](../sources/2022_therrien_visibility_bitmask_ssgi.md)).

**SAO.** Scalable ambient obscurance keeps the math of McGuire's Alchemy AO but reads a mip pyramid of camera-space depth, so its cost no longer grows with radius or scene. Reference: McGuire, Mara and Luebke, HPG 2012 (abstract only). LSAO, which sweeps lines to get exact horizons, compares against it ([line-sweep AO](../sources/2013_timonen_line_sweep_ambient_obscurance.md)).

**Screen-space reflections.** Trace reflection rays through the depth buffer and reuse the colour at the hit, so only what is on screen can be reflected. Stochastic SSR samples GGX with Halton points, traces at reduced resolution, shares rays among neighbours weighted by BRDF over PDF, picks a prefiltered colour mip from roughness and hit distance, and accumulates over frames. Reference: Stachowiak and Uludag, "Stochastic Screen-Space Reflections", SIGGRAPH 2015 Advances course; Stachowiak later wrote [kajiya](../sources/2022_stachowiak_kajiya_gi.md). Lumen traces screen space first and falls back to its own structures ([Lumen](../sources/2022_wright_lumen_siggraph_advances.md)).

**Screen-space indirect light (SSDO, SSIL, SSGI).** SSAO extended to directional occlusion and one diffuse bounce: samples of the lit frame buffer add colour, weighted by visibility. Reference: Ritschel, Grosch and Seidel, "Approximating Dynamic Global Illumination in Image Space", I3D 2009. The modern form is visibility-bitmask SSGI ([Therrien](../sources/2022_therrien_visibility_bitmask_ssgi.md)). Godot's SSIL reads the previous frame's lit colour and is applied as `ambient·(1 − ssil.a) + ssil.rgb·albedo` ([indirect diffuse paths](../sources/2026_godot_indirect_diffuse_paths.md)); [Gerassimov](../sources/2026_gerassimov_rc_vs_unity_gi.md) compares RC against Unity's SSGI.

**Depth thickness.** A depth buffer holds one surface per pixel and no thickness, so screen-space tracers assume each pixel is a slab of thickness `z_t` behind the stored depth and count a hit when the ray's depth interval overlaps it. Too thin and rays pass through objects; too thick and rays hit phantom walls behind silhouettes. Reference: McGuire and Mara, JCGT 3(4), 2014 ([notes](../sources/2014_mcguire_mara_screen_space_ray_tracing.md)). Every screen-space RC chooses one ([screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md), [plugins](../sources/2026_unity_unreal_rc_plugins.md) with a fixed 3.0, [3D screen space](3d_screen_space.md)).

**G-buffer and deferred shading.** Per-pixel images of the visible surface's attributes (depth, normal, albedo, material) written in one geometry pass, so later passes shade or trace without rasterizing again. Reference: Saito and Takahashi, "Comprehensible Rendering of 3-D Shapes", SIGGRAPH 1990. Screen-space RC reads its probe positions and normals from it.

**Premultiplied alpha and "over".** With colour stored premultiplied by alpha, compositing A over B is `C = C_A + (1 − α_A)·C_B`, `α = α_A + (1 − α_A)·α_B`, and filtering stays linear. Reference: Porter and Duff, "Compositing Digital Images", SIGGRAPH 1984. RC's merge `(L1, β1) ∘ (L2, β2) = (L1 + β1·L2, β1·β2)` is exactly this operator with `β = 1 − α` and emitted radiance as premultiplied colour ([core theory](core_theory.md), [HRC](../sources/2025_freeman_holographic_radiance_cascades.md), [merging and ringing](../sources/2026_merging_and_ringing.md)), which is why it is associative and why scene textures store emission times opacity ([tmpvar playground](../sources/2024_tmpvar_rc_playground.md)).

## Light transport and solver math

**Radiometric quantities.** Radiance `L = d²Φ/(dω·dA⊥)` is constant along a ray in vacuum; irradiance `E = ∫ L cos θ dω` is power per area on a surface; fluence (in RC usage, after biomedical optics' fluence rate) is `∫_{4π} L dω`, the radiance integrated over all directions without a cosine. A uniform field `L` gives `E = πL` on a surface and fluence `4πL` in 3D or `2πL` in 2D. Reference: PBRT 4th ed., ch. 4. 2D RC codes disagree on whether they output fluence or mean radiance, a factor 2π in brightness ([cascade layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md)).

**Lambertian BRDF.** `f = ρ/π`, so outgoing radiance is `ρ·E/π`, and under uniform `L` it is `ρL`. Reference: Lambert, *Photometria*, 1760; PBRT 3rd ed., section 8.3. The cosine-weighted integral of cascade 0 against the normal is the diffuse gather of every RC renderer; [Rulkens](../sources/2025_rulkens_rc_medical_volume_data_thesis.md) shows the error of weighting each cone by its center cosine.

**Beer-Lambert law.** Transmittance through a medium is `T = exp(−∫σ_t ds)`. References: Bouguer 1729, Lambert 1760, Beer 1852 (secondary). The volumetric form of RC's interval transmittance `β` ([core theory](core_theory.md)).

**Opacity correction.** A sample of opacity `α` taken over step `Δx` has transmittance `1 − α`; at step `Δx'` the equivalent opacity is `α' = 1 − (1 − α)^(Δx'/Δx)`, so total absorption is unchanged. Reference: Engel et al., *Real-Time Volume Graphics*, 2006 (secondary); GPU Gems ch. 39. Needed when coarse cascades march with longer steps ([volumetric](volumetric_and_radiative_transfer.md)).

**Phase functions.** The angular distribution of scattering in a medium; Henyey-Greenstein `p(cos θ) = (1 − g²)/(4π(1 + g² − 2g·cos θ)^(3/2))` with `g` the mean cosine, isotropic at `g = 0`. Reference: Henyey and Greenstein, "Diffuse radiation in the Galaxy", ApJ 93, 70-83, 1941. Used in [Jarosz](../sources/2008_jarosz_radiance_caching_participating_media.md), [volumetric RC](../sources/2026_volumetric_rc.md) and Godot's fog ([compositor examples](../sources/2026_godot_compositor_effect_examples.md)).

**Delta tracking.** Sample collisions in a heterogeneous medium against a majorant `σ_maj ≥ σ_t`: fly `s = −ln ξ/σ_maj`, accept a real collision with probability `σ_t(x)/σ_maj`, else continue unchanged. Reference: Woodcock et al., ANL-7050, 1965 (secondary). Named in [volumetric RC](../sources/2026_volumetric_rc.md).

**Radiative transfer equation and discrete ordinates.** `Ω·∇I = j − (k_a + k_s)ρI + (k_sρ/4π)∫I dΩ`; without scattering the formal solution is `I(s) = I(s0)e^(−τ) + ∫ j·e^(−τ) dx`. Discrete ordinates replace the angular integral with `Σ_j w_j I(s_j)` over fixed directions. Reference: Chandrasekhar, *Radiative Transfer*, 1950 (secondary). RC is a hierarchical discrete-ordinates scheme in which the ordinate count grows with distance ([core theory](core_theory.md), [volumetric](volumetric_and_radiative_transfer.md)).

**Non-LTE radiative transfer.** Solves the radiation field together with atomic level populations when they depart from local thermodynamic equilibrium, iterating formal solutions of the transfer equation with statistical equilibrium, accelerated by an approximate Λ operator. References: Rybicki and Hummer, A&A 245, 1991; Osborne and Milić, "The Lightweaver Framework", arXiv 2107.00475. Osborne's RC work solves this problem ([notes](../sources/2024_osborne_radiance_cascades_radiative_transfer.md)).

**Short characteristics.** Solve the formal transfer equation at each grid point along a short segment to the nearest upstream cell boundary, interpolating the upstream intensity: `I = I_up·e^(−Δτ) + ∫_0^{Δτ} S(t)e^(−t) dt`. Reference: Kunasz and Auer, JQSRT 39, 67, 1988 (secondary). Compared with RC in [sweep solvers](../sources/2026_sweep_and_short_characteristics_solvers.md).

**Radon and Hough transforms, projection-slice theorem.** The Radon transform integrates a function along every line; the projection-slice theorem says the 1D Fourier transform of a projection is a central slice of the 2D transform; the Hough transform is its discrete voting form, `ρ = x·cos θ + y·sin θ`. References: Radon, 1917; Bracewell, *Aust. J. Phys.* 9(2), 1956; Hough, US patent 3,069,654, 1962; Duda and Hart, CACM 15(1), 1972 (all secondary). HRC's line-space structure is a discrete Radon transform ([holographic RC](holographic_rc.md#building-rays-the-acceleration-structure)).

**Jacobi iteration.** Solve `Ax = b` by `x_i^(k+1) = (b_i − Σ_{j≠i} a_ij x_j^(k))/a_ii`, every update independent and parallel; converges when the spectral radius of `D⁻¹R` is below 1. Reference: Jacobi, *Astronomische Nachrichten* 22, 1845. Bounce iteration over frames in a radiance cache is a Jacobi iteration of the rendering equation ([light transport alternatives](../sources/2026_light_transport_alternatives.md)).

**Anderson acceleration.** Speeds up a fixed-point iteration `x = f(x)` by combining the last `m` iterates with weights that minimize the combined residual, `x_{k+1} = (1 − β)Σα_i x_i + βΣα_i f(x_i)`. References: Anderson, J. ACM 12(4), 1965; Walker and Ni, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 49(4), 2011 (secondary). Proposed for multi-bounce convergence in [light transport alternatives](../sources/2026_light_transport_alternatives.md).

**Fast multipole method.** Evaluates all pairwise `1/r` interactions in O(N) for a tolerance by representing far clusters with multipole expansions translated to local expansions. Reference: Greengard and Rokhlin, J. Comput. Phys. 73, 325-348, 1987. nema-nya describes its doubling cascades as a multipole expansion ([survey](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#2d-variants-worth-knowing)); RC's distance-dependent resolution is the same far-field idea (inference).

**Hankel functions.** The outgoing 2D Helmholtz Green's function is `G = (i/4)·H0⁽¹⁾(k|x − y|)`, behaving like `−ln r/(2π)` near the source. Reference: standard (secondary). Used in Sannikov's wave-optics tests ([LegitCascades](../sources/2024_sannikov_legitcascades.md#wave-optics-tests)).

**Nyquist-Shannon sampling.** A signal with no frequencies above `B` is determined by samples at a rate above `2B` and rebuilt by sinc interpolation; below that it aliases. Reference: Shannon, "Communication in the presence of noise", Proc. IRE 37(1), 1949. The penumbra condition is a sampling-rate rule of this kind ([core theory](core_theory.md)); [merging and ringing](../sources/2026_merging_and_ringing.md) and the [lumigraph](../sources/1996_light_field_and_lumigraph.md) use it.

**FFT convolution.** Convolution is pointwise multiplication of spectra; zero-pad to length `N ≥ L + M − 1` to avoid wrap-around, O(N log N). Used in [light transport alternatives](../sources/2026_light_transport_alternatives.md) and LegitEngine's water solver.

**Laplace diffusion.** Solving `∇²u = 0` (or a diffusion step) by relaxation; an HRC-based hierarchical solver ran a 129² grid in 3 to 4 ms ([HRC](../sources/2026_holographic_rc.md)).

## Interpolation and footprints

**Multilinear interpolation.** Linear interpolation along each dimension in turn: bilinear (4 taps), trilinear (8), quadrilinear (16, trilinear in space plus linear between two mip levels, as voxel cone tracing samples a cone's radius: [VCT](../sources/2011_crassin_voxel_cone_tracing.md)) and pentalinear (32, trilinear over the 8 surrounding probes times bilinear over 4 direction texels). Pentalinear is the full continuous reading of a 3D cascade in position and direction, which [tmpvar's 3D grid](../sources/2024_tmpvar_rc_3d_grid.md) planned for its debug view. A merge that averages a parent's fixed child directions needs only the trilinear part per direction (derived, [cascade layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md)).

**Cone footprint.** A cone of half-angle `θ` from a point has radius `r(t) = t·tan θ` at distance `t`, and sampling it from a prefiltered structure uses the level whose cell size matches `2r(t)`. A pixel cone (`θ` from the pixel's angular size) sets the sphere-trace hit threshold in [tmpvar's 3D grid](../sources/2024_tmpvar_rc_3d_grid.md) (2 × cone radius at `t`). In RC, a cascade's angular spacing and interval distance define an implicit cone footprint per ray, which is the quantity the penumbra condition keeps bounded ([core theory](core_theory.md)). Cone footprints through voxel mips have no closed form ([GigaVoxels](../sources/2011_crassin_gigavoxels_thesis.md)).

**Convex offset filtering.** daGI2's fix for leaks when probes or samples sit on the border of the convex volume they interpolate in: samples are pushed off the border into the volume before filtering ([daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md)).

## Numerics and statistics

**Gauss-Radau quadrature.** A Gauss rule with one endpoint fixed as a node, exact for polynomials up to degree `2n − 2` with `n` nodes. Reference: Radau, 1880 (secondary, IMSL documentation). Osborne integrates intervals this way ([radiative transfer](../sources/2024_osborne_radiance_cascades_radiative_transfer.md), [DexRT](../sources/2025_osborne_dexrt_ray_acceleration.md)).

**expm1.** `expm1(x) = e^x − 1` without cancellation near zero, so `1 − e^(−x) = −expm1(−x)` keeps precision for thin segments. Reference: C99/C++11 `expm1`. Used in [DexRT](../sources/2025_osborne_dexrt_ray_acceleration.md).

**Index of dispersion.** `D = σ²/μ`, 1 for a Poisson process, above 1 for clustering. Used in [DexRT](../sources/2025_osborne_dexrt_ray_acceleration.md).

**Coefficient of variation.** `CV = σ/μ`, a unitless relative spread for ratio-scale data.

**AR(1).** `X_t = φX_{t−1} + ε_t`, stationary for `|φ| < 1`, with autocorrelation `φ^k` at lag `k`; models frame-to-frame correlation of a temporally reused estimate. Used in [Reservoir Cascades](../sources/2026_you_reservoir_cascades.md).

**Beurling-Selberg functions.** Band-limited functions that majorize or minorize `sgn(x)` or an interval indicator with minimal integral error. Reference: Vaaler, Bull. AMS 12, 1985 (secondary). Proposed for band-limited interval weights in [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md).

**IRLS.** Minimize `Σ|r_i|^p` by repeated weighted least squares with weights `|r_i|^(p−2)` from the last iteration, regularized near zero (`1/max(δ, |r_i|)` at `p = 1`). Used in [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md).

**2-means clustering.** Lloyd's algorithm with two centroids: assign points to the nearer, move centroids to their means, repeat; in 1D it is a threshold at the midpoint of the two means. Reference: Lloyd, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 28(2), 1982. Used to split a tile's depths into two layers in [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md) and [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md).

**White furnace test.** Under uniform unit illumination a white, energy-conserving material or renderer must reproduce the environment exactly; darker means lost energy, brighter means gained. Used to check RC normalization in [Coppen's surfel RC](../sources/2025_coppen_surfel_radiance_cascades.md), [HRC](../sources/2026_holographic_rc.md), [merging and ringing](../sources/2026_merging_and_ringing.md) and [volumetric RC](../sources/2026_volumetric_rc.md). For single-frame RC it is the cheapest end-to-end check of interval coverage, merge weights and gather normalization together (inference).

## Image metrics

**FLIP.** A full-reference metric that maps the difference a viewer perceives when flipping between two images, given viewing distance and pixel size; HDR-FLIP evaluates several exposures. References: Andersson et al., "FLIP: A Difference Evaluator for Alternating Images", HPG 2020; Andersson, Nilsson, Shirley and Akenine-Möller, "Visualizing Errors in Rendered High Dynamic Range Images", Eurographics 2021 short papers. "LIP" in some notes is FLIP with its mirrored F lost. Used in [RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md), [Gavras](../sources/2025_gavras_screen_probes_world_intervals_thesis.md), [Kaméus](../sources/2025_kameus_spwi_probe_placement_thesis.md), [on-surface caches](../sources/2024_tatzgern_on_surface_radiance_caches.md) and [Split RC implementations](../sources/2026_split_rc_implementations.md).

**SSIM.** `SSIM = (2μxμy + c1)(2σxy + c2)/((μx² + μy² + c1)(σx² + σy² + c2))` over local windows, `c1 = (0.01L)²`, `c2 = (0.03L)²`. Reference: Wang, Bovik, Sheikh and Simoncelli, IEEE TIP 13(4), 600-612, 2004. Used in [Kaméus](../sources/2025_kameus_spwi_probe_placement_thesis.md), [Gerassimov](../sources/2026_gerassimov_rc_vs_unity_gi.md), [Lambru](../sources/2021_lambru_realtime_gi_game_engines_comparison.md) and [RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md).

**MSE, RMSE, MAE, PSNR, MAPE.** `MSE = mean((I − K)²)`, `RMSE = √MSE`, `MAE = mean|I − K|`, `PSNR = 10·log10(MAX²/MSE)`, `MAPE = 100·mean|(A − F)/A|`. For HDR renders `MAX` has no natural value and must be stated, and MAPE is undefined at black pixels, so relative metrics on images need a floor. Used in [HRC](../sources/2025_freeman_holographic_radiance_cascades.md), [HSGI](../sources/2023_lightspeed_hsgi_hierarchical_surfel_gi.md), [RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md), [DDGI resampling](../sources/2021_majercik_ddgi_resampling.md) and [Reservoir Cascades](../sources/2026_you_reservoir_cascades.md).

## Formats and colour

**R11G11B10F.** Three unsigned floats in 32 bits: R and G with 5-bit exponent and 6-bit mantissa, B with 5 and 5, bias 15, maximum 65024 (R, G) and 64512 (B), no sign and no alpha. Relative precision is about 1.6% (R, G) and 3.1% (B) per step (derived from the mantissas). Reference: Microsoft, "Floating-point rules (Direct3D 10)". Common for cascades and probes ([DDGI](../sources/2019_majercik_ddgi.md), [daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md), the Osborne slides' demo, [merging and ringing](../sources/2026_merging_and_ringing.md)), but it has no room for transmittance, which must live elsewhere.

**RGB9E5.** Three 9-bit mantissas sharing one 5-bit exponent (bias 15), maximum `(511/512)·2^16 = 65408`, non-negative only, no alpha. Reference: GL_EXT_texture_shared_exponent. Godot stores GI in it ([Godot GI internals](../sources/2026_godot_gi_internals.md)); Enshrouded's E5B9G9R9 probes are the same format ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md#enshrouded-a-cascaded-probe-cache)). Small channels lose precision against a bright one sharing the exponent, a source of drift when merged values are re-stored ([artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md)).

**RGB10A2.** Three 10-bit unorm channels and a 2-bit alpha; Microsoft DXGI format 24. Used in [Mayaux](../sources/2018_mayaux_horizon_based_indirect_lighting.md), [DDGI](../sources/2019_majercik_ddgi.md) and ErwinTerpstra's Unity RC (10-bit ARGB2101010).

**sRGB.** Decode `c_l = c_s/12.92` for `c_s ≤ 0.04045`, else `((c_s + 0.055)/1.055)^2.4`; encode with the inverse, threshold 0.0031308. An sRGB texture decodes to linear before filtering, and its alpha stays linear. References: Stokes, Anderson, Chandrasekar and Motta, "A Standard Default Color Space for the Internet – sRGB", 1996; IEC 61966-2-1; EXT_texture_sRGB. Averaging encoded values darkens results, which several 2D RC codes did by mistake ([jason.today](../sources/2024_mcghee_building_realtime_gi_part2_rc.md), Suslik's McSyWm, [rc-linear](../sources/2026_rc_repository_survey.md#2d-variants-worth-knowing)).

**Tone mapping.** Reinhard's global operator `L/(1 + L)` after scaling by the key over the log-average luminance (Reinhard, Stark, Shirley and Ferwerda, SIGGRAPH 2002); Narkowicz's ACES fit `saturate(x(2.51x + 0.03)/(x(2.43x + 0.59) + 0.14))` (2016 blog post); AgX, Sobotka's log-encoded, matrix-wrapped S-curve transform from Blender. The fad and testbed Shadertoy lines use `1 − (1 + F)^−2.5` ([Shadertoys](../sources/2023_rc_shadertoys.md#parameters-across-the-2d-implementations)).

**Reversed Z.** Map the near plane to depth 1 and far to 0 in a float depth buffer, so float precision, densest near 0, balances perspective's `1/z`; in OpenGL this needs `glClipControl(…, GL_ZERO_TO_ONE)` (EXT/ARB_clip_control). Reference: Reed, "Depth Precision Visualized", 2015. Godot uses reversed Z, which matters when reading its depth in compute ([compositor examples](../sources/2026_godot_compositor_effect_examples.md)); also [three-rc](../sources/2025_bennett_three_rc.md) and [screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md).

## Shading

**GGX.** `D(h) = α²/(π((n·h)²(α² − 1) + 1)²)`, a microfacet distribution with a long tail, usually with `α = roughness²`. Reference: Walter, Marschner, Li and Torrance, "Microfacet Models for Refraction through Rough Surfaces", EGSR 2007 (after Trowbridge and Reitz, 1975). Used in [gomezzz](../sources/2026_gomezzz_godot_3d_radiance_cascades.md) and [RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md).

**Split-sum approximation.** Specular image lighting as the product of a prefiltered environment map (one mip per roughness) and a BRDF table of scale and bias indexed by `n·v` and roughness: `L ≈ prefiltered(r)·(F0·A + B)`. Reference: Karis, "Real Shading in Unreal Engine 4", SIGGRAPH 2013 course. Lazarov's and Karis's 2014 analytic fit of `(A, B)` (`EnvBRDFApprox`, the `F_AB` term of Unreal's mobile shading) replaces the table on mobile; three-rc uses it ([three-rc](../sources/2025_bennett_three_rc.md)). The paper's specular cones query RC like a prefiltered map ([core theory](core_theory.md)).

**Multiple-scattering energy compensation.** Single-scattering microfacet BRDFs lose energy at high roughness; Kulla and Conty add a lobe built from the directional albedo `E(μ)` and its average, and Filament scales specular by `1 + F0(1/r − 1)` from the DFG table. References: Kulla and Conty, "Revisiting Physically Based Shading at Imageworks", SIGGRAPH 2017 course; Fdez-Agüera, JCGT 8(1), 2019. Godot's shading applies it ([indirect diffuse paths](../sources/2026_godot_indirect_diffuse_paths.md)).

**Linearly transformed cosines.** A BRDF lobe approximated by a clamped cosine under a fitted 3×3 matrix stored per view angle and roughness, which keeps a closed-form integral over polygons. Reference: Heitz, Dupuy, Hill and Neubelt, SIGGRAPH 2016. RCPG fits LTC lobes to guide sampling ([RCPG](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md)).

**Subgroup operations.** `GL_KHR_shader_subgroup` (Vulkan 1.1) lets invocations of a subgroup share values without shared memory: basic, vote, arithmetic, ballot, shuffle, shuffle-relative, clustered and quad operations. Quad shuffles (`quadSwapX/Y` in WGSL) averaging four ray threads gained about 10% on a screen-space RC ray pass ([screen-space RC](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md)); reductions and ballots serve compaction in [sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md).

**UAV barriers and typed UAV loads.** In D3D12 a UAV barrier makes all earlier UAV accesses to a resource finish before later ones start; typed UAV loads read a UAV in a given format, with R32 float, uint and sint always allowed and more behind a capability. In Vulkan the equivalents are pipeline barriers between dispatches and storage-image formats. Each top-down merge step needs one such barrier ([cascade layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md), [Godot integration](godot_integration.md)).

**Shader execution reordering.** NVIDIA's 2022 feature that regroups ray-tracing work across threads for coherence. Not available through Godot.

**Float atomics.** `VK_EXT_shader_atomic_float` and `GL_EXT_shader_atomic_float` (2020) add atomic float add and exchange on buffers, shared memory and r32f images; without them, sums are scaled to fixed point and accumulated with integer `atomicAdd`, which is also order-independent and so reproducible (Defour and Collange, FedCSIS 2015). Godot's `RenderingDevice` shaders cannot rely on float atomics, as Lambru et al. found in OpenGL ([Lambru](../sources/2021_lambru_realtime_gi_game_engines_comparison.md)); any splat step needs integer encoding or a gather.
