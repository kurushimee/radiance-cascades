# Scene representation and tracing

Every RC design needs one query: what is the first thing hit along a segment `[t_c, t_{c+1}]`, and what light leaves it toward the probe. That query is nearly all of the cost (87 to 96% of RC time in [Dell'Ova](../sources/2025_dellova_rc_performance_scaling_thesis.md)), so the scene representation decides both speed and which artifacts appear. In Godot 4.7.2, compute shaders cannot use hardware ray queries and the ray tracing pipeline is experimental with hard limits (see [docs/rendering_device.md](../../docs/rendering_device.md)), so every option below except hardware ray tracing runs as plain compute.

## What RC asks of a tracer

- **Segments, not rays.** Each cascade traces only its own interval. Near cascades trace short segments and need full precision; far cascades trace long segments but have wide angular cells.
- **Far cascades accept coarse data.** A cascade's direction cell is wide enough that it averages a large footprint at the far end, so far intervals can read prefiltered geometry. DexRT caps cascade `n` at mip `[0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4][n]` and gets most of its 8× speedup from it ([DexRT](../sources/2025_osborne_dexrt_ray_acceleration.md)). creikey's voxel RC picks the clipmap level per sample from the distance along the ray, and Phreakyx's design cone-traces voxel mips with an aperture of `2/oct_c`.
- **Coarse data leaks if it thins walls.** Prefiltered scene input thinned 2 px walls enough to leak about 13% per crossing in the HRC samples ([2D experiments](../sources/2026_assorted_2d_rc_experiments.md)). DexRT's variance-limited mipmapping only coarsens a block whose children agree, which keeps sharp structure at full resolution.
- **A hit returns radiance and transmittance.** Radiance is emission plus direct light times albedo, plus last frame's GI at the hit for more bounces. Transmittance is 0 for an opaque hit and fractional for media.

## Options

| Representation | Projects | Off-screen | Dynamic objects | Cost found |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depth buffer | PoE2, Kung, Alpha-Renderer, Unreal bitmask RC | no | free | PoE2 about 1 ms in total |
| Distance field (jump flood, sweeps, relaxation or sparse bricks) | nearly all 2D RC; EggyStudio clipmaps; rbnelr; Enshrouded; Godot's SDFGI; Lumen; Brixelizer; daGI2 | yes | rebuild, approximate, or mark from depth | EggyStudio full GI 0.6 to 1.0 ms at 800×450; Brixelizer GI about 2 ms at 4K |
| Voxels with DDA or mips | creikey, Phreakyx, DexRT, CT volume RC, tauray (occupancy only), Godot HDDAGI, HSGI | yes | revoxelize, or inject from the screen | creikey trace 0.5 ms at 640×360; 14.3 G rays/s in a synthetic test; HDDAGI probe update 0.93 ms on a GTX 1650; HSGI full GI 3.18 ms on an iPhone 13 Pro |
| Software triangle BVH | gomezzz, wroughtinator, three-rc, Surfel RC (tinybvh), Wicked Engine (GPU build) | yes | rebuild or refit | about 3.5× slower than hardware RT; tinybvh claims 3 to 5 G rays/s on an RTX 5080 Laptop |
| Analytic primitives | gomezzz (64 boxes and spheres), X3XfRM | yes | per frame | cheap |
| Surfels in a hash grid | Love and Deepspace | yes | local-space surfels per mesh | about 2 ms total GI on an iPhone 12 |
| Hardware ray tracing | chandra, erhe, Dell'Ova, Gavras, tauray, Unreal Split RC | yes | TLAS update | erhe 0.7 ms per million rays |

### Depth buffer

- Walk the ray's screen projection with a perspective-correct DDA so each pixel is visited once, treating each depth sample as a slab of thickness `zThickness` ([McGuire and Mara](../sources/2014_mcguire_mara_screen_space_ray_tracing.md)). Stride above 1 bands; jitter hides it. 25 steps per pixel at 1080p cost 1.2 ms on a GeForce Titan.
- A min/max depth pyramid lets a ray skip empty space coarse to fine (Alpha-Renderer). A per-sample visibility bitmask handles thin objects better than one horizon ([Therrien](../sources/2022_therrien_visibility_bitmask_ssgi.md)).
- Only the front surface exists, so thickness is a guess: too small leaks behind objects, too large over-occludes. Lights and occluders off screen or hidden are missing.

### Distance fields

- **Jump flood** builds a distance field in `log2(n)` passes whatever the number of seeds; use JFA+2 (two extra passes with steps 2 and 1). Several RC repositories lose the last step-1 pass through a ping-pong parity mistake. A signed field needs a second flood with seeds and empty space swapped ([Rong and Tan](../sources/2006_rong_tan_jump_flooding.md)).
- Sphere tracing steps by the stored distance and needs a minimum step and a hit tolerance (typical values in [typegpu](../sources/2026_software_mansion_typegpu_radiance_cascades.md): `eps` 0.25 and minimum step 0.125 probe spacings, obstacles eroded by one spacing).
- **Cheaper fields:** rbnelr builds an L1-style distance per chunk by sweeping lines along x, y and z, and steps `n·(1 − ε)/(|dx| + |dy| + |dz|)`, which cannot skip a solid cell; near surfaces it falls back to a voxel DDA ([voxel repositories](../sources/2026_voxel_3d_rc_repositories.md)).
- **Clipmaps** follow the camera. EggyStudio keeps four 64³ cascades, snaps them in 8-cell steps, rebuilds at most two per frame, builds a mesh into the field only after it has stayed still for 8 frames, and approximates moving meshes as colored boxes ([design documents](../sources/2026_engine_rc_design_documents.md)). Enshrouded traces an SDF of its voxel world on all hardware.
- **Sparse bricks:** Lumen keeps mesh SDFs and a global SDF of 4 camera-centered 256³ clipmaps as sparse 8³ bricks with a half-texel border, storing only a narrow band of ±4 voxels in one byte; a quarter-resolution coarse mip, rebuilt with 5 Eikonal iterations, lets rays skip empty space ([Lumen](../sources/2022_wright_lumen_siggraph_advances.md)). Brixelizer uses 64³ cascades whose occupied voxels get 8³ bricks of `R8_UNORM` distances in one shared atlas, found through a 3-level AABB tree per cascade, and its SDK release includes the GLSL builder and tracer ([Brixelizer](../sources/2024_amd_brixelizer_gi.md)).
- **Narrow band without bricks:** daGI2 keeps 5 dense R8 clips of 128 voxels wide and 64 tall (0.15 m voxels in clip 0, about 1.1 MB each) storing only ±4 voxels. A rebuild clears a 32-bit scratch buffer, writes triangle distances with `InterlockedMin` (sign in bit 0 so an unsigned minimum orders by magnitude), relaxes once over the 26 neighbors and packs the result. Clips scroll coarsest first, at most one per frame. The march steps `max(d − 0.5 voxel, 0.25 voxel)`, and a ray leaving a clip resumes in the coarser clip one finer voxel back ([daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md)).
- **From the depth buffer:** daGI2 alternates two passes each frame. Marking writes distances from random depth pixels into 4×4×4 blocks, only lowering values. Removal walks random view rays and raises a voxel's distance when its band box lies wholly in front of the HZB. Together they track moving objects without re-rasterizing them ([daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md)).
- **Thin walls** may never cross zero between samples, giving no hit and no gradient. Lumen expands surfaces by half a voxel's diagonal at trace time, starting at zero at the ray origin and growing with distance so contact shadows survive. One-sided meshes get a closing surface 4 voxels behind, and foliage carries a coverage value that reduces the expansion and drives stochastic transparency.
- Godot's SDFGI builds 128³ SDF cascades with a jump flood, but holds them as private engine data that no script or compositor effect can reach ([Godot GI internals](../sources/2026_godot_gi_internals.md)). An RC implementation has to build its own field.

### Voxels

- creikey's clipmap of four 64³ levels (1, 4, 16, 64 m cells, 12 MiB) stores albedo, density and injected light per cell, and puts dynamic objects in a separate small clipmap rebuilt per frame with `atomicMax` to resolve overlaps. Geometry thinner than a voxel leaks.
- DexRT's hierarchical DDA steps through cells of the current block's mip level and jumps empty blocks whole, with exact path lengths per segment. Its robustness rules: push `t` forward by 0.01 cell when entering an empty region, require the next-plane `t` to exceed the current one, and use an arithmetic shift, not division, for negative coordinates.
- Godot's unmerged HDDAGI keeps a 64-bit occupancy mask per 4³ block and a flag per 8³ region, and walks them with an integer DDA in 8-bit fixed point that descends into occupied cells and climbs out of empty ones, carrying rays into coarser cascades as they leave. Cascades follow the camera by wrapping addresses and revoxelizing only the uncovered slabs, and a version counter per region tells cached probe hits when to retrace ([HDDAGI](../sources/2026_godot_hddagi_dynamic_gi.md)). Lumen found plain marching of voxel bit bricks too slow and moved to distance fields; HDDAGI's hierarchy is the answer to that cost.
- HSGI stores 4³-voxel bricks sparsely: a dense grid holds a brick ID per occupied brick and a pool indexed by ID holds the voxel data, 128 bits per voxel on PC and 64 on phones. Following GVDB, its trace reads one occupancy bit per voxel, 64 at a time. Visible geometry is updated by comparing each voxel against the depth buffer, writing the lit color at the depth and clearing voxels in front of it, so moving and destroyed objects need no revoxelization while on screen ([HSGI](../sources/2023_lightspeed_hsgi_hierarchical_surfel_gi.md)).
- tauray voxelizes every frame by conservative rasterization and uses only the occupancy mips, to find which probes are live, not to trace ([tauray](../sources/2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md)).
- Godot's `RenderingDevice` has no geometry shader stage, so tauray's single-pass dominant-axis voxelization does not port directly. SDFGI's own approach is three orthographic raster passes, one per axis, writing 3D images, which HSGI also uses per object with depth testing off and each object's own material shader; a compute pass over the triangles is the other route.
- Voxel tracing tests for RC ([sparse 3D notes](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md), [volumetric notes](../sources/2026_volumetric_rc.md)): a two-level grid (16³ cells over 4³ bricks) traced 2× faster than an H-PLOC BVH, though more slowly on AMD than on NVIDIA; a 3D DDA over 4×4×4 bit cubes traced 15M rays in about 5 ms; sparse voxel cone tracing came within about 1.8× of hardware RT. Each cascade needs the scene filtered to its probe spacing, and averaged mips need six-direction opacity, or thin walls turn half transparent at each level.

### Software BVH

- gomezzz builds a binned-SAH BVH on the CPU in GDScript (8 bins per axis, leaves of up to 4 triangles), flattens it in preorder with an escape index for stackless traversal, and stores 48-byte nodes and 80-byte triangles with constant albedo and emission ([gomezzz](../sources/2026_gomezzz_godot_3d_radiance_cascades.md)). The build took about 1.2 s for 72,012 triangles, so moving meshes need a rebuild and reset the history.
- wroughtinator's native build uses a CPU-built binned SAH BVH (16 bins, leaves of 4) and a 64-entry stack on the GPU, all in one GLSL compute source ([Split RC implementations](../sources/2026_split_rc_implementations.md)).
- tinybvh ships Vulkan GLSL compute kernels for three node layouts: a 2-wide 64-byte node with float bounds, a 4-wide 64-byte node with byte-quantized bounds, and an 8-wide 80-byte compressed wide BVH (CWBVH) with leaves of up to 3 triangles. Stacks are 32 entries per thread in shared memory, indexed depth-major so neighboring threads hit neighboring banks, or just 8 for CWBVH, which pushes one node group per level. A full stack silently drops nodes. The kernels are closest-hit only and would need segment limits and an any-hit exit for RC. Builds and refits run on the CPU in C++; unity-tinybvh builds each mesh's BLAS once and rebuilds the instance TLAS every frame ([compute BVH libraries](../sources/2026_compute_bvh_libraries.md)).
- Wicked Engine builds the whole BVH on the GPU every frame for its DDGI when hardware ray tracing is missing: Morton codes of triangle centers, a radix sort, a Karras 2012 hierarchy with one thread per node, and bounds propagated up with one atomic bit per node. Nodes are 32 bytes and triangles 48 bytes in world space; traversal uses a 32-entry stack and accepts alpha-tested triangles stochastically by opacity ([compute BVH libraries](../sources/2026_compute_bvh_libraries.md)). It needs only compute dispatches and integer atomics, so it ports to `RenderingDevice` and removes the CPU rebuild that makes gomezzz reset its history when meshes move.
- Software traversal measured 231 µs against 66 µs for hardware ray tracing on the same Split RC workload, about 3.5× slower ([discussions](../sources/2026_rc_discussions.md)). Lumen rejected software BVHs and grids as too slow for long incoherent rays.
- One optimized software ray tracer ran about 4× slower than hardware RT on Apple M3, and a binary BVH beat wide BVHs for RC's coherent rays ([sparse 3D notes](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md), [screen-space notes](../sources/2026_screen_space_rc.md)).
- Mesh data reaches GDScript through `mesh_surface_get_arrays()`; the engine's own mesh buffers are not exposed as RIDs. Skinned meshes need their own path (gomezzz rejects them).

### Surfels

Love and Deepspace generates surfels per mesh asset from its lightmap UVs (about 10 cm, four LODs), stores them in local space, keeps them in a multi-level hash grid, and tests rays against disks in each grid cell they cross. On phones this beat software triangle BVHs. Gaps between surfels leak unless each surfel has a transparent outer ring that counts only when hit several times ([talks](../sources/2026_rc_conference_talks.md)).

## Shading the hit

- **Emission only** (three-rc, Gavras, Dell'Ova, jumeder) gives a single bounce from emitters; lit surfaces bounce nothing.
- **Direct light at the hit:** shadow rays through the same structure (gomezzz for one point source, wroughtinator for the sun and a point light, erhe), light injected into voxels ahead of time (Phreakyx, SDFGI's direct-light pass, HSGI's copy of the lit screen, daGI2's voxels blended from random lit G-buffer pixels with 6 normal-facing slots per voxel), or shadow maps (tauray). daGI2 lights off-screen voxels from geometry with low weight (alpha 32/255) so screen samples soon replace them, and stops writing where its world irradiance is not yet valid after a teleport ([daGI2](../sources/2026_gaijin_dagi2_dynamic_gi.md)).
- **Bounces:** read last frame's GI at the hit (gomezzz with `bounce_feedback` 0.65, wroughtinator two LODs coarser, X3XfRM from last frame's cascade 0). Each frame adds a bounce.
- **Materials:** gomezzz uses one constant albedo and emission per surface; Phreakyx multiplies the material color by the texture's average; wroughtinator samples albedo at mip 2 because mip 0 made sub-pixel detail flicker.

## Robustness

- **Rays starting inside solids:** gomezzz returns opaque black, which darkens probes near walls; genos-engine found that such a ray must not exit through the solid's far side, which painted lit faces onto back faces; Enshrouded pushes probes out along the SDF and kills probes whose summed ray distance is under 1 mm. erhe marks a probe as inside when more than 25% of 64 rays hit back faces.
- **Back faces:** erhe stores zero with β = 0 for a back-face hit; tauray culls back faces and returns black and opaque; gomezzz treats triangles as two-sided.
- **Small emitters** seen by one or two cascade 0 directions turn into fireflies. creikey caps their injected radiance and adds an analytic near-field term; wroughtinator's browser build cuts emitters within `1.5·ds0` from the ray result and adds them back analytically; EggyStudio treats emitters no larger than twice the cascade 0 spacing as analytic lights.
- **Thin walls** thinner than one cell of whatever structure is traced will leak (creikey, SDFGI, EggyStudio). SDFGI is "mostly leak free" only for walls thicker than one voxel of the cascade that renders them. Lumen's documentation asks for walls at least 10 cm thick with software tracing.
- **Hand-offs between tracers:** each tracer should resume from the distance the previous one reached. Lumen's screen trace steps back to its last unoccluded point when it goes behind a surface or off screen, which avoids a leak at the hand-off.
