# Light transport alternatives

Methods that compete with RC or extend it beyond diffuse light: line sweeps, the sweep solvers of radiative transfer, grid visibility, cellular automata, transform solutions without occlusion, wave optics, extended gathering, the resampling view of RC, caustics and refraction, multi-bounce, wavelet point-based GI, light sampling, and other uses of the transport equation. Most of the RC-side material comes from implementer discussions and is unverified unless a public link backs it ([notes](../sources/2026_light_transport_alternatives.md)). Most of it is 2D; the parts with direct use in a 3D renderer are line sweeps for long rays, extended gathering, multi-bounce feedback without lag, light sampling and the guiding ideas.

| Method | Cost shape | Occlusion | Status for RC |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Line sweeping](#line-sweeping) | O(1) per pixel per direction | yes | measured in 2D; faster than tracing on upper cascades |
| [Sweep solvers](#sweep-solvers-for-radiative-transfer) | one wavefront per direction | through optical depth | production codes outside graphics |
| [Grid visibility](#grid-visibility) | O(N²) per point source | binary, blurred edges | a recurrence, not tried with RC |
| [Cellular automata and cone sweeping](#cellular-automata-and-cone-sweeping) | O(1) per pixel per step | leaks with few angles | test shaders only |

## Line sweeping

A sweep marches every parallel ray of one direction across the grid at once, so one march serves every pixel it passes and costs O(1) per pixel per direction, with only two columns of `N` directions in flight. Sweeps suit a uniform screen grid better than a BVH.

- **Measured:** 1000 directions at 1080p in 40 ms after removing modulo operations, which had used about 60 of 150 ALU instructions; on an M2 Pro, 1024 sweeps at 1080p took about 200 ms with modulo, 50 ms without and 27 ms at the highest GPU clock. A 13 ms budget allows about 325 directions. One thread per row ran at 105 fps against 70 for one per column. Those runs move 42 to 79 G pixel-directions per second once modulo is gone (derived).
- **With RC:** RC line sweeping was competitive with, but slightly slower than, a multi-level DDA; it was faster on higher cascades, while cascades 0 to 2 were better traced normally. Sweeps cannot use the nearest or bilinear fix because rays do not belong to probes; the parallax fix works. Skewed probe grids per direction avoid the parallax error of misaligned probes. Sannikov's `Sweeping.ls`, a library no script includes, has a transform from a sweep direction to the pixel grid (using `1/|cos θ| = √(1 + tan² θ)`) and a skewed-rectangle transform ([LegitCascades](../sources/2024_sannikov_legitcascades.md#sweepingls)).
- **Volumetrics:** a sweep cannot stop at one interval length, so finite intervals need a stack of samples (about 600 MB estimated for 2× RC) or prefix sums of additive optical depth. Volumetric sweeps handle opaque geometry poorly.
- **In 3D:** bucket fragments by world-space line and sort along it, which gives the ray-march result with no extra memory, since only the first hit matters; sparse sweeps visit only non-empty voxels.
- **Sunlight:** one sweep is faster than a second RC pass for a directional light.
- Line-sweep AO on height maps is O(1) per ray with infinite thickness, and rasterizing slices into height maps was faster than a DDA. LSAO itself is in [related screen-space GI](related_screen_space_gi.md#line-sweep-ambient-obscurance).

### Berhard's sweep framework

Constantin Berhard, "A GPU Line Sweeping Framework", master's thesis, Saarland University, 2016-12-01, 67 pages, OpenCL and C++ ([PDF](https://www.pdf.inf.usi.ch/papers/master_thesis/ConstantinBerhard.pdf); [notes](../sources/2016_berhard_gpu_line_sweeping_thesis.md)), generalizes LSAO: per direction, parallel rays cross the domain, a small per-ray state machine (the "information condenser") digests the samples it passes, and an accumulator combines per-direction results at each output position. Page numbers are printed ones.

- **Algorithm 1 (p. 16):** for each direction and each ray entering the domain, a new condenser steps `a += stepSize·d` while inside, calls `digest(a, d, f(a))`, and writes `getInterestingData()` to `output[a, d]`. Work is parallel over directions and rays; a condenser cannot allocate GPU memory.
- **Direct accumulation** uses atomics, which OpenCL has only on integers, so HDR output is impossible that way (Alg. 2, p. 17). **Two-pass accumulation** writes every (position, direction) sample to private memory without synchronization, then runs one thread per output position over all directions (Alg. 3, 4); memory grows with directions × samples, and batching directions cuts memory for time (sec. 5.8). It needs coherent ray bundles per direction so a position's samples can be found, each ray knowing its base address and offset (sec. 5.4, 5.7.2).
- Directions come from a repulsion simulation on the sphere, jittered by at most half the neighbour spacing; purely random directions bias the results (sec. 5.4.1-5.4.2).
- **LSAO in the framework (sec. 3.3):** `L(p) = ∫∫ L_i(p, ω)·(ω·n)` (eq. 3.2) becomes `L(p) = ∫ f(p, θ, n, o(p, θ)) dθ` with `o` the occluder information per direction (eq. 3.3), `f = V·W(|p − o_p|)` (eq. 3.4), `V = sin h(o) − sin t(θ, n)` from the largest occluder's elevation and the tangent angle (eq. 3.5), and `W(d) = R/(R + d²)` (eq. 3.6), which the thesis calls a slight modification of Timonen's falloff; the sweep starts at the depth map's border with Timonen and Westerholm's amortized-constant structure.
- **Diffusion inpainting (ch. 6):** instead of relaxing `∫(f_x² + f_y²)` over four neighbours, `I(p) = ∫ L(r(p, θ))·w(|p − r(p, θ)|) dθ` with `r` the nearest known pixel along the ray, so known pixels act as lights that emit and block; the condenser keeps the last non-empty value seen and the accumulator adds `brightness·w(distance)` with two-pass accumulation; light sources must sit on a regular grid.
- **Inside-outside voxelization (ch. 7):** rays from the bounding box flip an inside flag at each crossing found by brute-force Möller-Trumbore; each voxel packs two 16-bit counters (total and inside votes) in one 32-bit word through integer atomic adds of `2^16 + inside` and is inside if most votes say so. SDFs (sec. 8.5) and line-sweep photon mapping with one parallel light per direction (sec. 8.4.3) are sketched; in the photon sketch scattering works only with direct accumulation.
- **Timings** (Core 2 Q9550, Radeon HD 7850 with 2 GB, AMD APP SDK 3.0, kernel medians of three runs, sec. 3.3.4): directional occlusion on a 1024×436 depth map into a 1024×256 hemisphere map, 16 directions on a 2 px grid, 94 ms (fastest 62); 8 directions on 1.8 px, 47 ms; four batches of two directions, 78 ms. Inpainting 1155×866: 128 directions on 1 px, 3,969 ms; 32 on 2 px, 188 ms. Inside-outside at 256³ without a BVH: octahedron (8 triangles) with 2,348,486 rays 1,078 ms; box with a hole (25 triangles) with the same rays 2,109 ms; octahedron with 12,543,424 rays 7,375 ms, about 1.1 to 2.2 M rays per second (derived). Generating rays on one CPU thread took 2,250 ms for 2.37 M rays and 25.5 s for 12.5 M, under 1 s in 2D but several seconds in 3D.
- **Limits:** sweep rays miss voxel centres and leave jagged edges (Fig. 7.7; snapping as in LSAO is the proposed fix); open meshes are noisy and close crossings at shared edges count once; two-pass memory and integer-only atomics as above. Proposed: sequential writes in blocks of about 32 threads (after Timonen and Westerholm), an overhead analysis, and incremental rendering by pairing opposite directions.

### Three ways to parallelize a sweep

The sweep sources show three schemes ([notes](../sources/2026_sweep_and_short_characteristics_solvers.md)): hyperplane wavefronts from an upwind corner (Ardra, Kripke, and the anti-diagonals of grid visibility); planes swept in order with parallel work inside each plane (MURaM); and one independent thread per ray with atomic accumulation (compute.toys 1418, LSAO, Berhard). None names a 45° sweep or compares with RC. compute.toys 1418 (fadaaszhi, unlisted) is the third scheme in miniature, in WGSL: one dispatch of 256 threads each launches a line from an upwind boundary cell of a 256×256 `atomic<u32>` buffer, the edge chosen by which of four 90° sectors the angle falls in, with the major axis advancing exactly 1 per step (`d = (cos a, sin a)/max(|cos a|, |sin a|)`), adding 1 at `o + round(d·i)` for 256 steps on a torus, so the buffer counts lines per cell; the image pass shows 0 black, 1 white and 2 or more red and resets cells with `atomicExchange`. Sannikov's linked line-sweep Shadertoy XXXyWM returns Shadertoy's not-found page (checked 2026-10-19).

## Sweep solvers for radiative transfer

Radiative-transfer codes solve RC's problem with sweeps and short characteristics. The RC discussions describe 45° diamond-shaped sweep fronts with one global write per pixel, nearly free atomics when lines do other work, wasted far-field work without cascades, and wide cones that keep the direction count low. In the sources themselves, Ardra and Kripke use hyperplane wavefronts from the upwind corner, and MURaM sweeps axis-aligned planes in order; the link the discussions label "Kripke report" is the Ardra CUDA paper.

**Ardra on CUDA** (Kunen et al., LLNL-CONF-773204, M&C 2019, [OSTI 1559411](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1559411)).
- Diamond-difference zone equations (eqs. 3-7): `C = σ_t/2 + μ_d/Δx + η_d/Δy + ξ_d/Δz`, `Ψ = (1/C)·[(μ_d/Δx)·Ψ_x,in + (η_d/Δy)·Ψ_y,in + (ξ_d/Δz)·Ψ_z,in + ½Q]`, outflow `Ψ_x,out = 2Ψ − Ψ_x,in` and likewise in y and z.
- A zone-by-zone sweep has no parallelism; zones on one hyperplane depend only on the previous one, so wavefronts start at the upwind corner, with at most N² parallel zones on N³. Across MPI ranks, wavefronts from different directions can collide at a node and are handled in message-arrival order.
- Model (eqs. 8-12): `T_iter = T_rhs + T_sweep`, `T_rhs = A + B·log P`, `S = P_x + P_y + P_z − 3 + M` stages, `T_bw = G·(Z/P)^(2/3)/BW`, `T_sweep = S·(T_comp + 3(T_bw + T_lat))`, refined by the fraction `C` of rank faces that communicate.
- On a V100, register pressure limits the kernel to about 400 threads per block. Tiling x and y into 20×20 zones gives 20×20×N pencils with parallelism 400 at any N and 20 ramp stages; each thread owns an (x, y) column and sweeps z in order, z-face fluxes in registers and x and y faces through shared memory ("Flux Registers Sweep", after Rennich's CUDA Kripke); each energy group is a block. The same RAJA source runs with 1×1×N pencils on CPU.
- Benchmark: Jezebel k-eigenvalue, 48 groups, S8, P4 scattering, 50³ zones per rank, up to 4096 Sierra nodes (2 POWER9 + 4 V100) and 64 CTS-1 nodes (2× 18-core Broadwell). Latency and bandwidth: CTS-1 2.1 µs and 5.1 GB/s, Sierra 61 µs and 9.5 GB/s. Fits: CTS-1 `A` = 1.065 s, `B` = 0.237 s, `T_comp` = 0.00251 s; Sierra 0.102 s, 0.00194 s, 0.00178 s; CTS-1 matched only with bandwidth divided by 36, blamed on network contention. Single-node throughput 2.75e8 (CTS-1) and 7.32e9 (Sierra) unknowns per second per iteration, quoted as 26.1 times (the two figures give 26.6, derived). Diamond difference is bandwidth-bound, and Sierra's latency blocks the gain from 4-way spatial overloading.

**Kripke** (Kunen, Bailey and Brown, LLNL-CONF-675389, ANS M&C 2015, [OSTI 1229802](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1229802)): steady-state 3D Boltzmann transport with diamond differences, `Hψ = L⁺Σ_s Lψ + Q` (eq. 2); a sweep inverts `H`, block-diagonal in groups and directions, with one wavefront per direction from each of 8 corners. Group sets are swept in sequence and direction sets pipelined through MPI. Six data nestings (DGZ, DZG, GDZ, GZD, ZDG, ZGD) give 18 kernels; on a 16-core Sandy Bridge node (64 groups, 96 directions, P4, 12³ zones per core, 10 iterations) 252 layouts ran from 2 to 51 s, the best in about 60% of Ardra's layout time. GZD was usually best but the ranking changed with architecture and scattering order. Weak scaling reached 32,768 BG/Q cores.

**MURaM short characteristics** (NCAR, [`src/rt/rt.cc`](https://github.com/NCAR/MURaM_main/blob/main/src/rt/rt.cc), read on `main` in 2026-10; line numbers are the file's).
- The solar MHD code uses short characteristics per cell in 3D and long characteristics only along vertical columns for optical depth and the top-boundary intensity.
- Carlson direction sets of 3, 6 or 10 per octant, read from `carlson{NMU}.dat`, over all 8 octants (lines 278-296, 1145-1151). Each direction picks the base face with the smallest `aM_i = Δ_i/μ_i`; the step is `Δ_base/μ_base` (lines 417-431).
- The upwind point on the base face is interpolated bilinearly from 4 neighbours with weights `(1 − r1)(1 − r2)`, `r2(1 − r1)`, `r1(1 − r2)`, `r1·r2` from ratios of the `aM` values; density, opacity and source use the same weights (lines 433-438).
- Optical depth `dt = (ds/3)·(κ_u ρ_u + κ_0 ρ_0) + (ds/6)·(κ_0 ρ_u + κ_u ρ_0)`, exact for two linear profiles (lines 2135, 2172). Source term: `e = exp(−dt)`, `w0 = 1 − e`, `w1 = w0 − dt·e`, `η = S_0·(w0 − w1/dt) + S_u·(w1/dt)`, exact for a linear source; small `dt` uses series (`w0 = dt − dt²/2 + dt³/6`, `w1 = dt²/2 − dt³/3`) and very small `dt` passes intensity through. Update `I = I_upw·e + η`.
- Planes along the base axis run in sequence (`acc loop seq`), with gangs and vectors over the transverse indices; z sweeps use transposed copies so z is the outer loop (lines 1893-2024). The opacity tables and transposes were not read line by line. Each (octant, direction) repeats read boundary, integrate, write boundary and exchange halos until the relative boundary change is below a threshold, an MPI max; within a rank the sweep is exact for its inflow, and coupling across ranks acts like block Jacobi.
- Accumulation `J += (w/8)·I`, `F_z += (π/2)·sign·w·μ_z·I`; heating mixes the mean-intensity and flux-divergence forms with weight `exp(−10·τ)`, the first dominating where thin. OpenACC and MPI; no timings or accuracy figures.

**Short characteristics lecture** (Piskunov, [lecture 5](https://www.astro.uu.se/~piskunov/TEACHING/NUM_RT/nrt_lecture05.pdf)): long characteristics run boundary to boundary and give both directions, expensive in 2D and 3D off-grid; short characteristics cross one cell at a time, are fast, need two-direction integration for mean intensity, and suffer numerical "light defocusing" (slides 9-12). The formal solution `I(τ_{i+1}) = I(τ_i)·e^(−Δ) + ∫ S(t)·e^(−(τ_{i+1} − t)) dt` with a linear source gives `η = S_i·[1 − (1 + Δ)e^(−Δ)]/Δ + S_{i+1}·[Δ − 1 + e^(−Δ)]/Δ`, which matches MURaM's form (derived). Quadratic sources are more accurate but can go negative; Bézier sources (de la Cruz Rodríguez and Piskunov 2013) are more robust (slide 24). The Hermitian scheme (Bellot Rubio et al. 1998): `I_{i+1} = I_i + (δ/2)(I'_i + I'_{i+1}) + (δ²/12)(I''_i − I''_{i+1})` with `I' = α(I − S)` (slide 22). Errors against Runge-Kutta: Feautrier 0.45%, DELO linear 0.26%, DELO parabolic 0.16%, Hermitian 1.46% (slide 26). A formal solver must iterate when source and opacity depend on the radiation.

## Grid visibility

Goldstein's recurrence ("A Quick and Clear Look at Grid-Based Visibility", 2023-05-12, [article](https://towardsdatascience.com/a-quick-and-clear-look-at-grid-based-visibility-bf63769fbc78)) gives visibility from a corner viewpoint over a grid of 1 (free) and 0 (blocked): `V(x, y) ← V(x, y)·[x·V(x − 1, y) + y·V(x, y − 1)]/(x + y)`, in raster order, scores above 0.5 visible; an arbitrary viewpoint runs once per quadrant on four flipped sub-grids.

- The result blurs shadow edges and misclassifies cells near them; refinement sharpens it, and scores converge to exact visibility as spacing goes to zero (Kao and Tsai 2008). Obstacles must span several cells.
- Larger neighbourhoods apply the update within cones between integer vectors: 8 neighbours use [1,1] with [1,0] and [0,1]; 16 add [2,1] and [1,2].
- The score equals the number of unblocked shortest grid paths divided by the unblocked count, a binomial coefficient (Goldstein et al. 2022, JAIR 74).
- Cells read their already-updated left and upper neighbours, so each anti-diagonal is independent, the hyperplane parallelism of Ardra's sweep (derived).
- In the RC discussions, the form `T·(x·r[x−1] + y·r[y−1])/(x + y)` is an O(N²) wave from a point source without volumetrics, with partial transmittance allowed and `1/r` applied separately, where the article's own cell value `V(x, y)` plays the part of `T`; from O(N) boundary points it gives full GI in O(N³).

## Cellular automata and cone sweeping

- **Cone sweeping:** the endpoints of a 45° cone move one pixel per step, an O(1)-per-pixel cellular automaton; cascade 0 tracks one occluder per pixel, cascade 1 two, cascade 2 four. The test shaders 7c2XDw and 7f2SzV show the pieces without cascades (a scan column with 45° edge carriers, and three-tap box diffusion with a mouse-set cone), with no angular falloff or Gaussian term ([2D Shadertoys](2d_shadertoys.md#shaders-that-are-not-rc)). fld3R4, linked with them, is paniq's 2D light propagation volume, covered in [related probe GI](related_probe_gi.md). A swept-light demo took 481 ms on Iris Xe and 78 ms on an RTX A2000 ([swept-radiance.pages.dev](https://swept-radiance.pages.dev/)).
- **Diffusion automaton:** `p[x+1, y] = 0.8·p[x, y] + 0.1·p[x, y−1] + 0.1·p[x, y+1]` gives penumbras; repeated convolution converges to a Gaussian.
- Cellular-automaton GI implementations stored only 4 to 6 angles, which is not conservative and leaks where steep cone borders skip tiles. Voxel versions with 2-bit directions give 90° shadows, per the discussion; the linked video (EdBillz, "Solving Lighting Using Voxel Cellular Automata", 2025-07-10, 4:42, no captions, [wVkhP67P2sk](https://youtu.be/wVkhP67P2sk)) says only that it is cellular-automaton GI in an OpenGL and C voxel engine. xima's "Voxel Engine Dev: Solving Global Illumination with Cellular Automata" (2024-03-20, 52 s, [nNJG2GAHsQk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nNJG2GAHsQk)) shows integer automata for propagation, diffusion and bouncing, with ray-traced reflections and refractions for transparent blocks, and gives no rule, grid size or timing.

## Solutions without occlusion

- Without occlusion, 2D GI is one convolution of emission with a `1/r` kernel, exact by FFT in O(n log n); RC is also O(n log n) but not exact, so accurate occlusion is most of the GI problem.
- The same result is `DualRadon(Radon(sources))` by the projection-slice theorem ([prototype fXfXRS](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/fXfXRS)). A naive O(N³) transform ran in real time on Shadertoy; the inverse dual Radon took about 5 s in Mathematica against a 5 ms target. FFT periodicity causes lens-flare artifacts, so pad with zeros. Sinograms can hold off-screen sources.
- An FFT Poisson solve cannot take internal barriers, and occlusion is nonlinear, so no transform encodes it exactly. Analytic conformal maps give exact unoccluded fluence (a line light is `ln((z − p)/(z − q))`), but continuing it from a boundary is ill-conditioned ([7cBGDK](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/7cBGDK)).
- A line light contributes `cos θ/r`, not a sum of point lights, and a finite source falls off as `atan(d/r)/π`.
- **Limited-angle tomography** (Vahteristo, Liu and Rupp 2025, [arXiv 2502.12293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12293v1)) reconstructs binary disks of the Helsinki Tomography Challenge 2022 from sinograms of 100° down to 30° by gradient descent through a differentiable Radon transform. The loss is the L1 distance between filtered sinograms with `r_α(ω) = |(2/α)·sin(αω/2)|·[sin(αω/2)/(αω/2)]²` (ramp for small α, low-pass for larger), plus total variation and a patch autoencoder prior, with a Deep Image Prior network on a ConvNeXt backbone; 8 images train the prior and 4 pick hyperparameters. Ablations: full 3.55, without the prior network 3.07, without the filter 3.23, without TV or the patch prior 3.44; per level 2.90 at 100° to 2.17 at 30°, against 2.96 to 2.41 for the winner trained on 200,000 synthetic images. The scores exceed the [−1, 1] range of the Matthews correlation they are called, so their aggregation is unclear. A reconstruction takes about 30 s on an RTX 3060. The link to light transport is the forward model: a line-integral operator seen from few directions, which a probe or line-space store with few angles also is; the paper has no light transport.

## Wave optics

- Wave-based GI scales as N² log N in 2D with an FFT, and N³ log N is not enough in 3D, where cost should follow surface area. Sharp shadows need tiny wavelengths. A 2D point source is a Hankel function; the infinite-wavelength limit is the Poisson problem.
- **LegitCascades wave-optics tests** ask whether a field sampled on a line can be re-expanded into plane waves (an angular spectrum) and propagated, the wave analogue of storing radiance on a line ([notes](../sources/2024_sannikov_legitcascades.md#wave-optics-tests)):
  - *Holograms:* an outgoing cylindrical wave `J0 + iY0` (`H0⁽¹⁾`) of wavelength 0.5 px is sampled along a 2048-sample row, transformed by a naive O(N²) DFT, and each harmonic rebuilt as a plane wave `dir·k_lin + tangent·√(k² − k_lin²)` inside a 500 px zone; evanescent harmonics are zeroed and an optional test keeps a plane wave only between the row ends. A slit mask passes 0.35 to 0.45 and 0.5 to 0.7 of the row. The display shows the residual against the true field, but the reference pass stores zeros, so nothing is subtracted.
  - *HologramPropagation:* 1000 random-phase point sources on a 200 px segment seed row 0; each later row (8 by default, 1024-sample DFT, wavelength 2) is rebuilt from the plane waves of the row above, with a random obstacle 10% of the width per row and a window from 0.2 to 0.8. Magnitudes average over frames with re-randomized phases; with images persisting between frames, the field reaches the last row only after about as many frames as rows ([live](https://radiance-cascades.github.io/LegitScriptEditor/?gh=Raikiri/LegitCascades/HologramPropagation.ls)).
  - *HolographicCharges:* a line field is divided in Fourier space by the 2D Green function `(i/4)·H0⁽¹⁾(kr)` sampled on the line, giving "charges" that are propagated with the Green function to rebuild the field in a 100 px zone (wavelength 10, 256 samples). The division has no regularization, so near-zero kernel frequencies amplify noise without bound, and the occlusion and evanescent toggles are never read.
- **Wave Cascades**, a claimed wave-optics extension of RC for Lorenz–Mie scattering and diffraction that computed a fully spectral Mie table in real time and sampled it with a 64³ equal-angle HRC, has no paper, preprint, video, post or repository under that name, and no RC with a Mie table, that could be found on the web, GitHub or arXiv (2026-10); the HRC paper has no wave or spectral content. The claim stays unsourced.

## Extended gathering

- Hits in one direction are spread to neighbouring probe cones with a constant-width ramp, since all hits fall in a known distance interval: `L(P, d) = lerp(L(A, d_i), L(B, d_{i+1}), α)` with `A = P + d·r − d_i·r`. Each gathered direction uses its own interpolated source position, and with 4× scaling the probe count per cascade stays the same. It needs spatial resolution above angular and smears only across the propagation direction, so occlusion along the cone survives. NfVXRc gathers 200 directions per pixel from one cascade of 8-direction probes, and sfVSzd applies the same angular smoothing to each stored texel; neither merges cascades ([2D Shadertoys](2d_shadertoys.md#extended-gathering)).
- Longer intervals cut spatial error at a fixed ray count while the gather compensates angular error; interval scaling should make the two errors match.
- With analytic ray splitting, 2×2 extended gathering and 2×2 dithered jitter it acts as a ray multiplier, and a 2×2 radius keeps contact shadows. World-space probes suited it better than screen probes; in screen space, interval length should make probes project to the same radius.

## Resampling view of RC

- Extensions (N probes and M directions to N/2 probes and 2M directions) and merges (to 2N probes and M/2 directions) are both reinterpolations of a radiance field. Resampling by integrating over each target's footprint, a trapezoid in line space, beat point sampling; stripe artifacts were fixed by oversampling a cascade. Lossless reinterpolation needs probe spacing to double per cascade.
- Primal and dual RC both capture radiance on a plane and rebuild it elsewhere, and alias only when the source or receiver lies on the capture line. The primal and dual polygonal cascades and the light-field background (Gortler's lumigraph and Levoy and Hanrahan's light fields) are in [holographic RC](holographic_rc.md#background-light-fields-and-line-space).

## Caustics and refraction

- **Refractive cascades** in vanilla RC refract each interval at the boundary and keep tracing for the rest of its length, with a bilinear fix before and after refraction. Starting intervals at probe centres made bent merge lookups more sensible. Residual artifacts remained, and changing cascade 0 settings made earlier good results unphysical, which suggests luck in convergence. Merging rotates upper probes by the lower probe's deflection ([f3d3R8](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/f3d3R8)); 4 to 8 refraction bounces looked identical. NfySzt, linked as a refraction angle fix, is codyjbennett's 2D RC with hardware-bilinear upper fetches and has no refraction.
- **Equal-angle HRC** (frustums of equal width in radians) and its 2D facts are in [holographic RC](holographic_rc.md#equal-angle-ring-radial-and-axis-aligned-hrc). For refraction, crossing quadrant boundaries stayed the error source, and ring HRC was judged better suited because bent rays break frustums.
- **3D:** dense equal-angle HRC with dispersion and Fresnel reflection, compared against 4k spp spectral path tracing, was called the first 3D RC caustics. Memory is the limit: 128³ took about 9 GiB through Vulkan (WebGPU cannot allocate that much), about 4.5 KiB per probe (derived), and 32³ ran in 40 ms without accumulation on an RTX 4070 Ti Super. An adaptive topology splitting rays per cell by an error estimate, bounded to 1 to 2× splits, cleaned up a refractive sphere, but its memory was too high.
- Off-screen radiance for refraction needs adaptive, bounded allocation, and no conservative metric for lensing is known, so it does not look scalable in 3D.
- **Limits:** scattering doubles rays at every split, so cascades lose their constant size, and caustics break the angular-spatial trade that lets cascade 0 manage with 4 directions. HRC caustics in 2D were poor.
- **2D forward light tracing:** a uniform grid cut the tracing pass from 165 ms to 3.5 ms, 47 times (derived). Uniform hemisphere sampling did better than cosine-weighted for forward tracing in 2D, though metrics disagreed (FLIP mean 0.112 against 0.100). Most variance comes from rasterizing random lines, so stratifying lines matters more than better paths; Owen-scrambled Sobol cost about 8% more per path than white noise.

## Multi-bounce

The fixed-point view of bounces is in [core theory](core_theory.md#bounces).

- **Feedback:** last frame's radiance times albedo comes back as emission. Without a transmittance bound it explodes: one 256² test went from 65 to 5700 at albedo 0.9, 88 times, beyond the 10 times a closed box at albedo 0.9 can reach, so the feedback itself gained energy (derived and inference). Closed reflective boxes need decay or a reset of the temporal input; emission should count only on the first bounce; splitting floors and walls into two layers stabilized one build. Albedo 0.5 sums to twice the direct light after infinite bounces.
- **Without temporal lag:** stored ray hit distances allow `N` bounces in one frame without recasting rays, in opaque scenes. Bounces can also run as separate RC iterations, with Jacobi iteration or Anderson acceleration to converge faster ([solver math](supporting_techniques_transport_and_math.md#light-transport-and-solver-math)).
- **Energy per bounce (derived).** In a closed scene of uniform albedo `ρ`, bounce `k` carries `ρ^k` of the emitted light, so `n` lag-free bounces hold `1 − ρ^n` of the infinite-bounce indirect light: 50%, 75% and 87.5% for one to three bounces at `ρ = 0.5`, 70%, 91% and 97% at 0.3, and 20%, 36% and 49% at 0.8. Open scenes lose light to the sky at every bounce, so these shares are lower bounds. A bounce by repeated RC iteration costs a full solve; with stored hit distances it costs re-lighting the hits and a merge, with no new rays ([check](../checks/bounce_energy_share.py)).
- Path of Exile 2 shipped one extra indirect bounce for directly lit objects. One unnamed game found 3D RC too slow and used a two-bounce method instead.

## Wavelet point-based GI

Wang, Meng and Boubekeur, "Wavelet Point-Based Global Illumination", EGSR 2015 ([PDF](https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/boubek/papers/WPBGI/WPBGI_lowres.pdf); [notes](../sources/2015_wang_wavelet_point_based_gi.md)). Point-based GI stores outgoing radiance on a hierarchy of surface points and gathers it through a per-receiver microbuffer; WPBGI replaces its spherical harmonics with Haar wavelets of cube maps so glossy interreflection and caustics survive. Equations and Table 2 were read from page images. RC discussions link it as a wavelet radiance encoding ([sparse 3D RC](../sources/2026_sparse_and_3d_rc.md)).

- Outgoing radiance per point (`L_o = ∫ L_i·ρ·V·(ω_i·n) dω_i`, eq. 1) is sampled into a cube map, typically 32×32 per face, Haar-transformed, and stored in a binary bounding-sphere tree. A parent averages its children, `C_j = (C_{j−} + C_{j+})/2`, `G_j = (G_{j−} + G_{j+})/2`, and keeps the detail `H_j = (G_{j−} − G_{j+})/2`; only the root average and details are stored, details with L2 norm under 0.002 are dropped, averages are kept down to level `m = 20`, and a node is rebuilt as `C_j = G_root + Σ s_k·H_k` along its path with `s_k = ±1`.
- Each receiver starts a 16×16 quadtree microbuffer, refined by the BRDF and then by incoming light: `F(q) = F_l(q)·F_ρ(q)` with `F_l(q) = 1` when `L_i(q)/2^{Δ_q} > γ` (`γ = 1` typically). Multiple bounces split sender and receiver pairs until the solid-angle ratio falls below `ε = 0.05` (Alg. 1). Shading multiplies wavelet coefficients, `D = U·W` (4D BRDF and 2D incoming coefficients), then `L_o = T₂^{−1}(D)`.
- **Results** (Mitsuba, 8-core 2.67 GHz i7, 9 GB; 1024×768 or 1024²; 32×32 tiles; bidirectional path tracing reference):

## Light sampling and guiding

- **RC-guided NEE** was proposed in discussion: at each level pick one of the four next-cascade cones by brightness, then trace inside it, with a small uniform term. It resembles RCPG's published guiding; what sources actually do with lights and cascades is in [sampling and guiding](rc_for_sampling_and_guiding.md#cascades-and-light-sampling).
- **Light trees.** A light tree over emissive triangles built in under 0.5 ms for 10k to 100k triangles on an RTX 3090 with O(log N) sampling (the build time comes from the discussion). PBRT's sampler ([sec. 12.6.3](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Sources/Light_Sampling#BVHLightSampling)) bounds each node by its box, power `φ`, principal direction `w`, normal spread `cos θ_o`, emission spread `cos θ_e` and a two-sided flag, with infinite and distant lights sampled apart. A node's importance at `p` with normal `n` is `φ·cos θ'/d²`, with `d² = max(|p − p_c|², |diagonal|/2)` so points inside stay finite, `θ' = max(0, θ_w − θ_o − θ_b)` (`θ_w` between `w` and the direction to `p`, `θ_b` the angle the box subtends), zero when `cos θ' ≤ cos θ_e`, times `cos max(0, θ_i − θ_b)` against the surface normal. The build splits by bucketed centroids at minimum `φ·M_ω·K_r·SurfaceArea`, `M_ω` the solid-angle measure of the direction cone and `K_r = max(diagonal)/diagonal[dim]` penalizing thin boxes; leaves hold one light, nodes are 32 bytes with quantized bounds and an octahedral direction, two to a cache line. Sampling walks from the root choosing children by importance, with each light's PMF from a stored bit trail. On PBRT's Beeple scene it cut MSE 2.37 times for 5.8% more time, 2.25 times the efficiency, and scenes with millions of lights render nearly as efficiently as one.
- About 70% of Path of Exile 2's shaders are emissive effects, so screen-space emissive pixels are the simplest light source there.
- RC wants many rays and resampling methods want a few good ones, a tension in combining them.

## Other uses

- **Diffusion and Laplace solving** with HRC ([holographic RC](holographic_rc.md#other-uses)), and cascades for pathfinding connectivity: an N³ build and log N queries against N⁴ for A*.
- **Radiative heat transfer** follows the same transport equation; thermal infrared suits RC because everything emits.
- **Audio attenuation, fog of war** (one channel per faction) and **spectral rendering** with one wavelength or a batch of four per frame were prototyped.
- **Adaptive angular resolution.** A link labelled as a temporal multi-bounce transport paper resolves to Abe, Yajima, Umemura and Hoshi, "Adaptive angular resolution for time-dependent radiative transfer simulations based on local radiation field anisotropy" (JQSRT 352, 109820, 2026, [PII S0022407326000142](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022407326000142)), about light in biological tissue; only the abstract was reachable. Angular resolution varies with position by the local anisotropy of the field, measured with spherical Haar wavelet coefficients, on a polyurethane phantom; against constant resolution it is up to about 8 times faster and 3 times on average at equal accuracy, with memory down to about 20% at most, the gain growing as diffuse light spreads. It is the adaptive counterpart of RC's fixed per-cascade angular density.
- **Spectral sky with four wavelengths.** García Liñán ([fgarlin.com/blog/spectral-sky](https://fgarlin.com/blog/spectral-sky/), 2024, updated 2026) renders Rayleigh scattering and ozone absorption with four wavelengths per pixel, one SIMD lane each, converted to XYZ by one 3×4 matrix from `X = Σ_i x̄_i·L_i·w_i` (likewise Y and Z over 390 to 780 nm). The eight parameters (four wavelengths and widths) are fitted by grid search and Nelder-Mead against an offline spectral path tracer, weighting sunrise and sunset so the fit does not settle on daylight blue; the fitted values are not given, the fit holds only for that atmosphere, aerosols are left out as nearly grey, and no error figures are given. It is the quadrature a four-wavelength cascade would need, one wavelength per RGBA channel (inference); spectral RC itself is in [volumetric](volumetric_and_radiative_transfer.md#spectral-transport).
- **Quad layouts.** A link given as texture-space quad patches for surface probes ([arXiv 1905.09097](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09097)) is Viertel, Osting and Staten, "Coarse quad layouts through robust simplification of cross field separatrix partitions" (2019): CAD meshes split into four-sided regions by tracing cross-field separatrices and collapsing chords, without T-junctions on 92 of 100 surfaces. It has no texturing or lighting; a quad layout could only serve as a chart parameterization for [surface probes](3d_surface_probes.md) (inference).

## Connections

- **A sweep is RC's merge done along lines.** Both share each occluder among all receivers that see it; a sweep shares it along a line for one direction, RC shares it across a probe's footprint per cascade. Their costs agree in the far field, where sweeps beat tracing on upper cascades, and differ in the near field, where cascades 0 to 2 traced normally won (inference from the measurements above).
- **Sweep solvers are the volumetric RC of other fields.** MURaM's short-characteristics step `I = I_upw·e^(−dt) + η` is RC's interval merge with a linear source inside the interval, and its bilinear upwind interpolation is RC's spatial interpolation at the interval start; Piskunov's "light defocusing" is the angular blur RC's ringing and parallax fixes fight ([artifacts](artifacts_and_fixes.md#ringing)) (inference).
- **Hyperplane wavefronts appear in three places.** Ardra's corner wavefronts, the anti-diagonals of grid visibility and diamond sweep fronts all exploit the same dependency structure, and they map onto a compute dispatch per wavefront.
- **Transform methods bound what occlusion costs.** Unoccluded GI is an FFT; everything RC spends beyond O(n log n) buys occlusion. Limited-angle tomography shows the same operator (line integrals from few directions) cannot be inverted well without priors, which is the angular-sampling limit of a cascade read from the other side (inference).
- **Haar averages are pre-averaging.** WPBGI's average-and-detail tree is RC's pre-averaging written as a wavelet transform; dropping small details is a lossy direction compression RC does not attempt ([pre-averaging](cascade_layout_and_merging.md)) (inference).
- **Adaptive angular resolution has a precedent.** The JQSRT method and WPBGI's light-driven microbuffer refinement both raise angular resolution where the field is anisotropic, the adaptive form of what RC fixes per cascade (inference).

## Single-frame assessment

- **Sweeps are single-frame and deterministic**, and their cost is known in advance: about 325 directions fit in 13 ms at 1080p in 2D. In 3D, the line count per direction grows with area, so a full sweep is far costlier, and sorting fragments per world line is the proposed path.
- **Multi-bounce without lag exists in principle**: reusing stored hit distances gives `N` bounces in one frame for opaque scenes, and Jacobi or Anderson iterations converge a fixed number of RC iterations within one frame.
- **Cellular automata and HologramPropagation move light one cell or row per frame**, so they are temporal by construction and do not fit a single-frame goal.
- **Caustics break the constant-cost property** that single-frame RC relies on, through ray doubling at every split.
