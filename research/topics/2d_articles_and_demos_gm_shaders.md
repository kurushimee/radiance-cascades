# 2D articles and demos: GM Shaders

The two GM Shaders posts by Yaazarai and Xor and their GameMaker repository: layout, index math, pre-averaging, the direction-first layout, timings and the code. The other 2D explainers, and the connections, single-frame assessment and open questions for all of them are in [2D articles and demos](2d_articles_and_demos.md).

## GM Shaders (Yaazarai and Xor)

Two GM Shaders guest posts (Part One 2024-04-13, https://mini.gmshaders.com/p/radiance-cascades; Part Two 2024-07-13, https://mini.gmshaders.com/p/radiance-cascades2, which names no author on the page) and a GameMaker repository (date not captured) under the Unlicense (public domain, per its `LICENSE` file beside `README.md`), based on fad's Shadertoy ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md)). Layout, index math, intervals, cascade counts and merges are in [cascade layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md); the shader variants and their fixes in [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md#cheaper-fixes).

**Part One.** It gives no complete shader, only short code fragments. It presents RC as a structured alternative to random rays, defines linear resolution as the number of sample points and angular resolution as the number of ray directions per point, spaces probes evenly to fit the scene size, states the penumbra hypothesis as more probes near the light and more rays per probe farther away, and calls 4× scaling optimal for 2D, chosen by Sannikov, not a hard rule. Its example layout at 1024² has cascade 0 at 16×16 probes of 64 rays (64 px apart), then 8×8 of 256, 4×4 of 1024 and 2×2 of 4096, 16,384 rays per cascade (a 128² texture per cascade, derived), while its overview says the usual start is 4 rays. The raymarch returns radiance 0 and visibility 0 outside the scene, the scene colour and visibility 1.0 on a hit, and 0 and 0 on a miss; read with that convention its merge rule merges rays that hit, the reverse of the repository and Part Two. Its out-of-bounds test writes `raypos >= 1.0` without a component beside `raypos.x < 0.0`, so `raypos.x >= 1.0` is meant. Its merge runs from the top down: each probe finds its 4 nearest upper probes, takes the 4 rays in the same direction from each, adds them per probe (TL, TR, BL, BR) and blends the four sums bilinearly, with the exact averaging not written out (Figure 8, whose image was not captured). The output is a "cascade 0 mip", one pixel per probe summing its rays divided by the ray count, drawn scaled up with hardware interpolation; each cascade's mip can also be drawn for inspection. As extensions it names the sky integral merged into the cascade with the most directions, which leaves occlusion unchanged, and credits Mytino with volumetrics through high-viscosity fluids, skybox integrals and directional light bleeding into caves; other uses listed are Sannikov's gravity simulation and the 3D screen-space GI video "Single shot global illumination with no denoiser" (cascaded image-space GI in one frame in about 5 ms). Its cascade-count procedure stops adding cascades once the interval start passes the screen diagonal `√(width² + height²)`, since rays beyond it see nothing on screen. The author states occasional light leaks and non-linear attenuation transitions, calls the method still under active development and says the post describes one specific implementation. Reader questions on view-dependent specular GI (Sagie Levy, 2025-11-06) and on visible interference patterns (hood233, 2025-01-07, asking why light's wavelength has no part in the algorithm) have hidden replies.

**Part Two.** It lists three problems with Part One's demo (two passes; square-only math including the SDF, which the new version replaces with stricter size rules; and no cache-friendly storage, since GPUs read nearby texels faster and storage order should follow access order, with a 1024² layout called a "4K texture" and doubled density an "8K texture" without the layout behind those figures), then gives pre-averaging and the direction-first layout and walks through one merged pass. The shader takes the fragment texel position (`in_TexelCoord`), the lights and occluders (`in_RenderScene`), the scene SDF (`in_DistanceField`), the render area size (`in_RenderExtent`, vec2), the cascade surface size (`in_CascadeExtent`, vec2), the cascade count and index (`in_CascadeCount`, `in_CascadeIndex`, floats), cascade 0 spacing (`in_CascadeLinear`) and interval (`in_CascadeInterval`); cascade textures are square in the position-first version and `E_x × E_y` in the direction-first one, and spacing is `L0·2^c` per axis, so the text's "4x" per cascade matches the code only in area; each cascade is `render size / cascade0_linear` texels, constant across cascades, with powers of two: 1, 2, 4 and 8 put probes that many pixels apart, and 0.5, 0.25 and 0.125 give 2, 4 and 8 probes per pixel, which the post calls costlier. Its raymarch works in pixels, scales every SDF sample by the render diagonal `length(in_RenderExtent)`, starts each cascade's ray at the probe point plus direction times offset, converted to UV, and stops when the travelled distance reaches the range or the ray leaves `[0, 1)` in UV, returns no hit for cascades above 0 when the first sample and the travelled distance are both about zero (a start inside a volume, since 2D lights emit from their surfaces), returns the scene colour with visibility 0 on a hit and zero radiance with visibility 1 on a miss. The merge is skipped for rays that hit and in the top cascade, and skipped rays return their radiance with inverted visibility; its walk-through of the 0.25 offset writes "2,2 becomes 2.25" where its own steps give 1 + 0.25 = 1.25. `main()` takes the probe origin `(probe + 0.5)·linear`, casts 4 rays from `index·4`, merges each and accumulates with weight 0.25. Its RTX 3080 stress test (measured frame times against the 8.33 ms budget of a 120 fps target; the GameMaker version is not stated) is in [performance](performance.md). It disclaims anything beyond screen-space raymarching, calls the code under 100 lines, leaves sky and sun colours commented as pending, says in its conclusion that it has no extras this time, lists the Dolkar mask fix as planned, and has a reader comment (Zenith, Jan 5, edited, its replies hidden) that found 6 cascades at 4 rays per pixel sometimes slower than 24 random rays per pixel at equal ray operations.

**Repository.** Three projects: `RadianceCascades/` (position-first, the original paper's layout, multi-pass), `RadianceCascades-Optimized/` (direction-first, pre-averaged, one pass per cascade, several merge variants) and `CascadeVisualDemo/`, which shows probe spacing and ray mapping for both layouts. The README points to the author's plain port of fad's Shadertoy, Yaazarai/RadianceCascades, as the demonstration the repository supports; the wiki lists it as accompanying Part One ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#repository-gmshaders-radiance-cascades)).

**Stress test (Part Two, RTX 3080, 120 fps target, 4 probes per pixel, 4 cascade 0 rays).** The position-first build at 1024² showed 29.91 ms and the direction-first build at 1920×1080 25.95 ms, each beside 8.33 ms. The post calls the first a frame-time delta and the second the 120 fps budget; the repository's HUD prints measured frame time and then the budget, so both first figures read as measured frame times (derived from code). The direction-first build covers about 2.07 million pixels against 1.05 million (derived). The post's conclusion says the direction-first layout roughly doubled performance at double the area, while the overview update says it is not faster; the two statements conflict. Per pixel the timings favor the conclusion: 28.5 against 12.5 ms per megapixel, 2.3× faster for direction-first (derived), though the builds also differ in passes and pre-averaging, and both miss the 8.33 ms budget by about 3×.

Repository inconsistencies not covered elsewhere: the call site says the interval must be a multiple of 4 while `multiple_of2` rounds to 2; the original helper describes spacing as `N·4` per cascade while the code uses `2^c` per axis; the merge comment says "on hit merge" while the code merges misses; the raymarch comment's visibility convention is reversed from the code; the mip loop indexes with an unfloored position; the optimized `render_interval` comment asks for the spacing's diagonal while the code takes half of it (`multiple_ofN(0.707, 2)` = 2); the optimized setup comment says 4 rays per pixel while spacing 1 gives one probe per pixel, and the post's test used 0.25 ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#inconsistencies-in-the-repository)). Of the optimized shader variants not covered in [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md#cheaper-fixes): the vanilla `Shd_RadianceCascades` ("no fixes" in the README) linearizes samples with `pow 2.2`, encodes cascade 0 with `1/2.2`, applies the leak term on every cascade, returns zero for starts inside a volume above cascade 0, and carries a comment marking the Dolkar fix as not yet implemented; the active `BilinearFix` has no volume-start check and encodes cascade 0 with `pow 1/2.2`; `FPFixed` uses a floored half-size, indexes `mod(sector.x + sector.y·angular, count)`, and keeps the leak term and the volume-start check; `Final` clamps the fetch to `[0.5, size − 0.5]`, comments out the leak term, marches to `limit` from the start point and has no volume-start check. Neither name is explained. The README says the bilinear fix "reduces interpolation artifacts and fixes occlusion". The posts' and the codes' choices side by side:

**Earlier post: random rays per pixel.** Xor's 2023-12-08 guest post with Yaazarai traces rays from every pixel each frame, with no probes. A jump flood gives the field, with no pass offsets or count given (2-byte coordinates for precision; the post calls the field's look intentional; the direction to the seed can serve as a normal map). Each ray steps `p += dir·DF(p)` for 32 to 64 steps and on a hit returns the per-channel max of the hit texel and the texel one pixel before it; misses return black. An optional aspect correction divides `p.x` by `screen.y/screen.x` in landscape and `p.y` by `screen.x/screen.y` in portrait. Its inputs are `gm_BaseTexture` (RGB light colours), `in_DistanceField.r` (the encoded field), `in_BlueNoise.r` and `screen` (the resolution). Each pixel outside walls (`DF ≥ EPSILON`) starts from its scene colour `L` and `B = max(L.r, L.g, L.b)` and sums `R` rays at `θ_k = θ0 + 2πk/R` from a blue-noise start angle `θ0 = 2π·n` (`n` in [0, 1] from `in_BlueNoise.r`), adding each ray's colour to `L` and its max channel to `B`; the output `(L/B)·(B/R)` reduces to `L/R`, so its brightness normalization does nothing and `B = 0` gives 0/0, and its `ray < TAU` loop may run `R` or `R + 1` times (read from the text, not tested); `in_RaysPerPixel` has no default. The previous frame's output feeds back as scene light, so bounces build up over frames, with no bounce count given; the output alpha is 1. Noise options are static (`θ0 = 2π·n`), golden-ratio "3D-style" (`n′ = fract(n + φ·t)` with scene time `t`) and the recommended "2D-style" rotation (`θ = mod(2π·n + t/(2π), 2π)`, written back to the noise texture as `θ/2π` each frame), with time units not given and Calinou's open blue-noise set shown at 512×512. Its limits: unbounded rays need a step cap, non-square fields skew, static noise bands, more rays cost GPU time, denoisers band, noise hurts video encoding; the method is 2D only, light accumulates over frames, and the post gives no timings. Uses named: pseudo-wave propagation, falling-sand games such as Noita, AI traversal and planet gravity from the field's normals; it links a GMShadersGI repository and Samuel Bigos's Godot GI tutorial ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#earlier-post-2d-gi-by-random-rays-per-pixel)). The voxels post (https://mini.gmshaders.com/p/voxels, Xor, 2024-06-01), linked for raymarching background, is paywalled, as is its web.archive.org copy; the public part motivates it with how Teardown and SEUS-style Minecraft shader mods light scenes and a Teardown against SEUS Renewed image, with no method content.

**2D-QuickRayTracing-GLSL**, the source of Part One's figure 6 (LGPL-2.1, with a 9-page write-up), puts one probe at each light's centre, traces `2πR` rays one pixel apart at the light's radius in whole-pixel steps through a collision texture, stores each first-hit distance over `R` in two 8-bit channels of a square texture of side `2^ceil(log2 √(2πR))`, and draws a quad per light comparing each pixel's distance with the nearest ray by angle: hard shadows stepping at ray boundaries, about `O(R²)` samples per light per frame, `R` above 4 and at most 4096 px (the README's 65,535 px is only a loop bound). Read from the code: sub-pixel walls can be skipped, a solid centre texel is ignored, and a rounded ray count can leave an unobstructed wedge below 2π ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#links-found)). Mytino's April 2024 post shows a glowing slime simulation in Mytino's own engine, described as RC or heavily based on it, with no demo planned.
