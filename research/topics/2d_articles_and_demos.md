The tutorials and teaching demos that explain 2D RC with working code: the GM Shaders posts and repository, jason.today's two articles, the community site's demo and its twin in Osborne's slides, tmpvar's playground, and the shorter write-ups by Otavio Peixoto, Théo Le Goc, SimonDev and Kelkar et al. Interval formulas, index math, cascade counts and parameter tables shared with other codes are in [cascade layout and merging](cascade_layout_and_merging.md), merge fixes and artifacts in [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md), timings in [performance](performance.md), and who published what when in [history and landscape](history_and_landscape.md).

## GM Shaders (Yaazarai and Xor)

Two GM Shaders guest posts (Part One 2024-04-13, Part Two 2024-07-13) and a GameMaker repository under the Unlicense, based on fad's Shadertoy ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md)). Layout, index math, intervals, cascade counts and merges are in [cascade layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md); the shader variants and their fixes in [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md#cheaper-fixes).

**Part One.** It presents RC as a structured alternative to random rays, states the penumbra hypothesis as more probes near the light and more rays per probe farther away, and calls 4× scaling optimal for 2D, chosen by Sannikov, not a hard rule. Its example layout at 1024² has cascade 0 at 16×16 probes of 64 rays (64 px apart), then 8×8 of 256, 4×4 of 1024 and 2×2 of 4096, 16,384 rays per cascade (a 128² texture per cascade, derived), while its overview says the usual start is 4 rays. The raymarch returns radiance 0 and visibility 0 outside the scene, the scene colour and visibility 1.0 on a hit, and 0 and 0 on a miss; read with that convention its merge rule merges rays that hit, the reverse of the repository and Part Two. Its out-of-bounds test writes `raypos >= 1.0` without a component. Its merge runs from the top down: each probe finds its 4 nearest upper probes, takes the 4 rays in the same direction from each, adds them per probe and blends the four sums bilinearly, with the exact averaging not written out. The output is a "cascade 0 mip", one pixel per probe summing its rays divided by the ray count, drawn scaled up with hardware interpolation. As extensions it names the sky integral merged into the cascade with the most directions, which leaves occlusion unchanged, and credits Mytino with volumetrics through high-viscosity fluids, skybox integrals and directional light bleeding into caves; other uses listed are Sannikov's gravity simulation and the 3D screen-space GI video "Single shot global illumination with no denoiser" (cascaded image-space GI in one frame in about 5 ms). Its cascade-count procedure stops adding cascades once the interval start passes the screen diagonal, since rays beyond it see nothing on screen. The author states occasional light leaks and non-linear attenuation transitions, calls the method still under active development and says the post describes one specific implementation. Reader questions on view-dependent specular GI (Sagie Levy, 2025-11-06) and on visible interference patterns (hood233, 2025-01-07, asking why light's wavelength has no part in the algorithm) have hidden replies.

**Part Two.** It lists three problems with Part One's demo (two passes; square-only math including the SDF, which the new version replaces with stricter size rules; and no cache-friendly storage, since GPUs read nearby texels faster and storage order should follow access order, with a 1024² layout called a "4K texture" and doubled density an "8K texture" without the layout behind those figures), then gives pre-averaging and the direction-first layout and walks through one merged pass. The shader takes the texel position, scene, distance field, render and cascade extents, cascade count and index, cascade 0 spacing (`in_CascadeLinear`) and interval (`in_CascadeInterval`); each cascade is `render size / cascade0_linear` texels, constant across cascades, with powers of two: 1, 2, 4 and 8 put probes that many pixels apart, and 0.5, 0.25 and 0.125 give 2, 4 and 8 probes per pixel, which the post calls costlier. Its raymarch works in pixels, scales every SDF sample by the render diagonal, starts each cascade's ray at its offset and stops when the travelled distance reaches the range or the ray leaves `[0, 1)` in UV, returns no hit for cascades above 0 when the first sample and the travelled distance are both about zero (a start inside a volume, since 2D lights emit from their surfaces), returns the scene colour with visibility 0 on a hit and zero radiance with visibility 1 on a miss. The merge is skipped for rays that hit and in the top cascade, and skipped rays return their radiance with inverted visibility; its walk-through of the 0.25 offset writes "2,2 becomes 2.25" where its own steps give 1 + 0.25 = 1.25. `main()` takes the probe origin `(probe + 0.5)·linear`, casts 4 rays from `index·4`, merges each and accumulates with weight 0.25. Its RTX 3080 stress test (measured frame times against the 8.33 ms budget of a 120 fps target; the GameMaker version is not stated) is in [performance](performance.md). It disclaims anything beyond screen-space raymarching, calls the code under 100 lines, leaves sky and sun colours commented as pending, lists the Dolkar mask fix as planned, and quotes a reader (Zenith) who found 6 cascades at 4 rays per pixel sometimes slower than 24 random rays per pixel at equal ray operations.

**Repository.** Three projects: `RadianceCascades/` (position-first, multi-pass), `RadianceCascades-Optimized/` (direction-first, pre-averaged, one pass per cascade, several merge variants) and `CascadeVisualDemo/`, which shows probe spacing and ray mapping for both layouts. The README points to the author's plain port of fad's Shadertoy, Yaazarai/RadianceCascades, as the demonstration the repository supports ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#repository-gmshaders-radiance-cascades)).

**Stress test (Part Two, RTX 3080, 120 fps target, 4 probes per pixel, 4 cascade 0 rays).** The position-first build at 1024² showed 29.91 ms and the direction-first build at 1920×1080 25.95 ms, each beside 8.33 ms. The post calls the first a frame-time delta and the second the 120 fps budget; the repository's HUD prints measured frame time and then the budget, so both first figures read as measured frame times (derived from code). The direction-first build covers about 2.07 million pixels against 1.05 million (derived). The post's conclusion says the direction-first layout roughly doubled performance at double the area, while the overview update says it is not faster; the two statements conflict. Per pixel the timings favor the conclusion: 28.5 against 12.5 ms per megapixel, 2.3× faster for direction-first (derived), though the builds also differ in passes and pre-averaging, and both miss the 8.33 ms budget by about 3×.

Repository inconsistencies not covered elsewhere: the call site says the interval must be a multiple of 4 while `multiple_of2` rounds to 2; the original helper describes spacing as `N·4` per cascade while the code uses `2^c` per axis; the merge comment says "on hit merge" while the code merges misses; the raymarch comment's visibility convention is reversed from the code; the mip loop indexes with an unfloored position; the optimized `render_interval` comment asks for the spacing's diagonal while the code takes half of it (0.707, rounded to 2); the optimized setup comment says 4 rays per pixel while spacing 1 gives one probe per pixel, and the post's test used 0.25 ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#inconsistencies-in-the-repository)). Of the optimized shader variants not covered in [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md#cheaper-fixes): the vanilla `Shd_RadianceCascades` ("no fixes" in the README) linearizes samples with `pow 2.2`, encodes cascade 0 with `1/2.2`, applies the leak term on every cascade and returns zero for starts inside a volume above cascade 0; `FPFixed` uses a floored half-size, indexes `mod(sector.x + sector.y·angular, count)`, and keeps the leak term and the volume-start check; `Final` clamps the fetch to `[0.5, size − 0.5]`, comments out the leak term and marches to `limit` from the start point. **Earlier post: random rays per pixel.** Xor's 2023-12-08 guest post with Yaazarai traces rays from every pixel each frame, with no probes. A jump flood gives the field, with no pass offsets or count given (2-byte coordinates for precision; the post calls the field's look intentional; the direction to the seed can serve as a normal map). Each ray steps `p += dir·DF(p)` for 32 to 64 steps and on a hit returns the per-channel max of the hit texel and the texel one pixel before it; misses return black. An optional aspect correction divides `p.x` by `screen.y/screen.x` in landscape and `p.y` by `screen.x/screen.y` in portrait. Each pixel outside walls (`DF ≥ EPSILON`) starts from its scene colour `L` and `B = max(L.r, L.g, L.b)` and sums `R` rays at `θ_k = θ0 + 2πk/R` from a blue-noise start angle `θ0 = 2π·n` (`n` in [0, 1] from `in_BlueNoise.r`), adding each ray's colour to `L` and its max channel to `B`; the output `(L/B)·(B/R)` reduces to `L/R`, so its brightness normalization does nothing and `B = 0` gives 0/0, and its `ray < TAU` loop may run `R` or `R + 1` times; `in_RaysPerPixel` has no default. The previous frame's output feeds back as scene light, so bounces build up over frames; the output alpha is 1. Noise options are static (`θ0 = 2π·n`), golden-ratio "3D-style" (`n′ = fract(n + φ·t)` with scene time `t`) and the recommended "2D-style" rotation (`θ = mod(2π·n + t/(2π), 2π)`, written back to the noise texture as `θ/2π` each frame), with time units not given and Calinou's open blue-noise set shown at 512×512. Its limits: unbounded rays need a step cap, non-square fields skew, static noise bands, denoisers band, noise hurts video encoding. Uses named: pseudo-wave propagation, falling-sand games such as Noita, AI traversal and planet gravity from the field's normals; it links a GMShadersGI repository and Samuel Bigos's Godot GI tutorial ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#earlier-post-2d-gi-by-random-rays-per-pixel)). The voxels post is paywalled with no public method content.

**2D-QuickRayTracing-GLSL**, the source of Part One's figure 6 (LGPL-2.1, with a 9-page write-up), puts one probe at each light's centre, traces `2πR` rays one pixel apart at the light's radius in whole-pixel steps through a collision texture, stores each first-hit distance over `R` in two 8-bit channels of a square texture of side `2^ceil(log2 √(2πR))`, and draws a quad per light comparing each pixel's distance with the nearest ray by angle: hard shadows stepping at ray boundaries, about `O(R²)` samples per light per frame, `R` above 4 and at most 4096 px (the README's 65,535 px is only a loop bound). Read from the code: sub-pixel walls can be skipped, a solid centre texel is ignored, and a rounded ray count can leave an unobstructed wedge below 2π ([GM Shaders](../sources/2024_yaazarai_gmshaders_radiance_cascades.md#links-found)). Mytino's April 2024 post shows a glowing slime simulation in Mytino's own engine, described as RC or heavily based on it, with no demo planned.

## jason.today (Jason McGhee)

Two interactive three.js articles with every demo's GLSL inline in the page: part 1 (https://jason.today/gi, 2024-07-27) builds naive 2D GI on a jump-flood distance field, and part 2 (https://jason.today/rc, 2024-08-31) builds RC on top of it step by step ([part 1](../sources/2024_mcghee_building_realtime_gi_part1.md), [part 2](../sources/2024_mcghee_building_realtime_gi_part2_rc.md)). Part 2 names Yaazarai's GM Shaders post as its biggest direct influence and thanks the paper's repository; part 1's introduction links the technique document on Google Drive. Part 2's interval formulas, tile layout, merge clamp, cascade count, Reduce Demand mode and derived interval table are in [cascade layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md#intervals), its artifacts, limits and the article-against-code differences in [artifacts and fixes](artifacts_and_fixes.md#base-ray-counts-other-than-4), and its penumbra hypothesis and brute-force cost estimate in [core theory](core_theory.md#the-penumbra-hypothesis).

### Part 1: naive GI

**Drawable surface.** Strokes paint a float scene texture: rgb is the paint colour, alpha 1 where painted and 0 where empty. A GPU stroke pass copies the previous scene and, while drawing, writes `vec4(color, 1)` where the squared distance to the stroke segment is at most `radiusSquared` (25 for the default 5 px radius). The segment distance projects the pixel onto the segment, clamps the parameter to `[0, 1]` and returns the squared remainder, avoiding a square root; both endpoint y values are flipped (`height − y`) because canvas y grows downward and UV y upward. Targets are RGBA `FloatType`, nearest, clamp to edge, two per pass as a ping-pong pair, cleared with `renderer.clearColor()` with no clear colour set.

**Naive raymarch.** Each empty pixel casts `N` rays at `TAU/N·(i + noise)` with `noise = rand(uv)`, directions `(cos, −sin)/size` so a step moves one pixel, and averages the colour of the first painted pixel each ray reaches, stopping at the canvas edge; painted pixels (alpha above 0.1) return their own colour and cannot receive light. The script differs from the article: noise is the constant 0.1 when the noise toggle is off; step `k` samples `uv + direction·k` from `initialStep` to `maxSteps − 1`, where `initialStep` is 0 with "accumulate radiance" on and `maxSteps − 1` with it off, so with it off each ray tests one texel and the rays show as separate dots; the hit test is alpha above 0.5 while the early return stays at 0.1; there is no sun or sky. The demo uses 8 rays and 128 steps by default (slider 0 to 512), both toggles on; the author says light spreads at most 256 px, the loop repeats overlapping work that neighbouring pixels cannot share (the motivation for the jump flood), the core loop is nearly the same as far more efficient methods, and the random offset avoids aligned rays and repeating patterns, with any cheap noise working and the goal being to use none.

**Jump flood.** The seed pass writes `vec4(uv·a, uv·(1 − a))`, so painted pixels hold their UV and empty ones `(0, 0)`; only rg is read later. `P = ceil(log2(max(W, H)))` passes, 9 at 300×300 and 300×400, use offsets `2^(P−1−i)` px (256 down to 1); each samples the 3×3 grid at `uv + (dx, dy)·o_i/(W, H)` including the centre, skips samples outside `[0, 1]²` and samples whose rg is `(0, 0)`, keeps the seed nearest to the current UV by squared distance, and writes rg with ba 0, or `(0, 0)` when nothing qualifies. There is no early exit, because "closer" depends on the stored seed, not on the jump length, and two targets ping-pong because a pass cannot read and write one target. The JFA demo's slider runs 0 to 9 passes (5 by default) with offsets still taken from `P`, so a partial run uses the largest offsets first; "No Passes" shows the UV map through a `skip` path. The distance pass writes `d = clamp(distance(uv, JFA.rg), 0, 1)` in UV units as `vec4(d, d, d, 1)`, and the DF demo always runs all `P` passes.

**GI with the field.** Each empty pixel casts 32 rays (`rayCount = 32.0`, a value only in the code) at `angle_i = stepAngle·(i + offset) + sunAngle`, with `offset = rand(uv + coef)` when noise is on and 0 when it is off (`coef` is the frame time with temporal accumulation, else 0) and `stepAngle = TAU/N`, to which an optional "sand grain" mode adds `offset·TAU`. Directions are unit vectors in UV, not divided by size. From `p = uv`, for steps 1 to `maxSteps − 1`, the march reads `d = DF(p)` with nearest sampling, moves `p += dir·d`, stops with no hit outside `[0, 1]²`, and on `d < EPS` (0.001) adds the scene colour at the moved `p` and stops, so the distance is read before the move and the colour after. Misses add `sunColor·smoothstep(1, 0, angleToSun) + skyColor` when the sun is on, with `angleToSun = mod(angle − sunAngle, TAU)`, sun (0.95, 0.95, 0.9) and sky (0.02, 0.08, 0.2); the sky term is constant and the sun depends only on the ray angle, a model the author made up after finding others complicated or copied. Painted pixels are not marched: they emit their own colour if `length(rgb) ≥ 0.1` and are black occluders otherwise (only #000000 in the palette). Output `max(scene.rgb, radiance/N)` with alpha 1. With the field, max steps "can be way smaller" (32 against 256) and set quality and scale with canvas size; at the defaults a pixel reads the field at most 992 times plus one scene read per hit (derived).

**Temporal accumulation**, "basically a hack to deal with noise" that RC "does not need", adds the frame count to the noise seed (`rand(uv + time)`) and mixes `mix(new, previous, 0.9)` while `time < 10` (`accumAmt`); at the limit the next render resets time, skips the blend and stops the animation loop. It pauses while drawing (each stroke resets time), while a slider is pressed (`drawingExample`) and while toggled off; two ping-pong GI targets hold the history, and the screen renders a second time after the history pass, at the cost of a target switch and an extra lookup per frame.

**Article against code.** The article's seed is `vec4(vUv·alpha, 0, 1)` and the script's `vec4(vUv·alpha, vUv·(1 − alpha))`; the article initializes the flood with `vec4(−2.0)` and copies the full vector, the script with `vec4(0.0)` and copies `.xy`; the naive hit threshold is 0.1 in the article and 0.5 in the script; the article starts one step out while the script samples from step 0 (the empty pixel itself); the article always uses `rand(uv)`; the article mixes accumulation every frame while the script mixes only when `time > 0` and not drawing or sliding, and renders the GI pass twice. Read from the code: on the 300×400 canvas the GI rays and field are in UV units, so ray spacing and distances are not uniform in pixels (`oneOverSize` is declared and unused, while the naive shader divides by size); an unseeded pixel measures its distance to the UV corner, clamped to 1, and partial floods can leave pixels unseeded; no gamma or colour-space conversion appears anywhere.

**Stated limits and cost.** No timings, hardware or frame rates; the naive method is "wildly inefficient" and the canvas cannot grow much and stay smooth even on strong machines; part 2 promises the same result at much higher resolution. Only painted surfaces are handled (reflective, transparent and subsurface materials need more), the sky is one colour and the sun one falloff, noise is needed against patterns and accumulation works around it. Ringing, leaks, aliasing, banding and flicker are not discussed. A Markdown gist of the post (`e78bc628…`) has the same demo constants but is an earlier text: the sentences about 8 rays reaching 256 px and "like 256 steps" appear only on the page.

### Part 2: building RC

**Motivation.** Part 1's stop test is an epsilon the author calls hand-wavy, and its quality came from 32 rays, style and accumulation. Raising the ray count mid-march is a branch the author calls not GPU friendly, and the distance field already skips steps, so lowering resolution with distance is not obvious; the answer is separate passes, a high-resolution one with few rays and a low-resolution one with many. **Packing directions into pixels.** For base 16 the high-ray pass runs on a 4 px probe grid, one probe per 16 pixels: `spacing = 1` for the base pass and `√base` otherwise, `size = floor(resolution/spacing)` probes per axis, `probeRelativePosition = mod(coord, size)`, `rayPos = floor(coord/size)` picks the group of rays a texel holds, `baseIndex = baseRayCount·(rayPos.x + spacing·rayPos.y)`, ray `index = baseIndex + i` at angle `(index + 0.5)·TAU/rayCount`, probe centre `(probeRelativePosition + 0.5)·spacing`. Sixteen directions sit in 16 quadrants of a 4×4 grid, each quadrant one upper tile, numbered from the bottom left rightward and then up with a clockwise rotation. The merge decodes `upperPosition = (mod(index, √base), floor(index/√base))·upperSize` and samples at `(upperPosition + (probeRelativePosition + 0.5)/√base)/resolution`. Each output pixel then costs 2 × 16 = 32 raymarches, the same as the 32-ray naive method, while the penumbra hypothesis predicts better accuracy from 256 directions against 32. One sample per pixel group leaves a grid pattern, and linear filtering on the target gives bilinear upscaling "for nearly free".

**Tuning the split.** The ideal split is close to zero but not zero; about 0.02 UV (about 8 px) looks a little long but reasonable; the author's rule of thumb is half the probe width, 8 px for 16 px probes, probably too long. Shadows shift in proportion to the upper interval's offset and look reasonable with a lower split. With 16 px probes and 256 rays the upper interval would need about 128 px, which needs another layer, and that leads to the general form: `N_k = base^(k+1)` rays, spacing `√base^k`, start `base^(k−1)/shortestSide` (0 at cascade 0), length `base^k/shortestSide`, a lowest cascade covering 1 px from the probe centre, and `scale = shortestSide/resolution` per axis to keep rays on a unit circle on non-square canvases. The cascade count `ceil(log(diagonal)/log(base)) + 1` comes from 300×400: the longest ray is 500 px, `log4(500)` is about 4.5, so at least 5 cascades, and the `+1` was added when a top-left light did not reach the far edge ([layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md#cascade-count)).

**sRGB and ringing.** Scene reads are linearized with gamma 2.2 and the output encoded with 1/2.2, since WebGL works in sRGB and light should add in linear space (the page links Shadertoy McSyWm); the author says this "produces a much brighter (accurate) result" and exposes vanilla RC's ringing, seen best with a single dot. Ringing is "still an active area of research", with known fixes that "incur a fair amount of overhead (as in doubling the frame time) or cause other artifacts"; the penumbra appendix shows subtle ringing in the shadow. The final page carries a hidden "Ringing Fix" checkbox (`display: none`) read into `this.ringingFix` and never used.

**Final demo: sizes.** Query parameters `width` and `height` (512 on desktop, 300×400 on mobile), `pixelRatio` (2, `dp` in the code), `rcScale` (equal to it) and `autosize=true`, which scales the CSS size when the canvas is wider than the window minus 80 px. The simulation size (drawing, flood, field and RC targets) is `pixelRatio·width/rcScale`, unrounded, 512 at the defaults; the backing store is 1024, the CSS size 512, the high-resolution drawing and overlay targets 1024, the probe extent `floor(W_s/P)`. The RC targets are allocated at the simulation size, not the extent, so texels beyond the extent repeat. The brush radius is `4·pixelRatio` = 8 drawing px, divided by `rcScale` in the simulation pass. The RC reads the low-resolution scene and the overlay the high-resolution one.

**Final demo: pipeline.** Each frame draws the stroke into the simulation and high-resolution targets with a thin white cursor outline, seeds `(vUv·round(alpha), 0, 1)`, runs `ceil(log2(max(W_s, H_s)))` flood passes (9 at 512) over the 3×3 neighbourhood with out-of-range samples skipped, writes `clamp(|vUv − nearest|, 0, 1)` to red from `JFA(floor(vUv·resolution))`, renders the cascades from the top, and overlays. `angularSize = √(W_s² + H_s²)` = 724.08 gives `ceil(log4(724.08)) + 1` = 6 cascades with a literal base 4, so the Base Ray Count slider does not change the count; the Pixels Between Base Probes slider re-runs `initializeParameters(true)`, which resets the count uniform. The `animate()` loop keeps rendering about 1 s after the last mouse event. Uniforms: `cascadeInterval` 1.0, `rayInterval` 2.0, `intervalOverlap` 0.04, `baseRayCount` 4, `sunAngle` 4.2, `srgb` 1.0 or 2.2, `firstCascadeIndex` from Layer to Render; `lastCascadeIndex` is declared and never set or used, and `time` is set to 0.1 in JavaScript and not declared in the shader. Constants: `SQRT_2 = 1.41` (rounded), `PI = 3.14159265`, `sunDistance = 1.0`, sky `(0.2, 0.24, 0.35)·6`, sun `(0.95, 0.9, 0.8)·4`; the march stops after at most 100 iterations.

**Final demo: shader.** Cascade 0 returns painted pixels unchanged. A ray starts at `start·b^(k−1)/R` along its direction, returns 0 if that point is outside `[0, 1)²`, steps by the field value scaled by `min(R)` in pixels, and hits when the field is at most half a texel; a hit texel with alpha below 0.99 returns black, otherwise its colour raised to `srgb`. The merge returns the ray unchanged on a hit or at the top, and otherwise adds one `textureLod` of the upper tile `idx` at `clamp((probe + 0.5)/√b, 1, upSize − 1)`, at mip `log2(P)` when `P > 1`; the targets use `LinearMipmapLinearFilter`, `LinearFilter` for magnification and generated mipmaps, though the article states linear filtering only for the V2 demo. With the sun on, every top-cascade ray takes `max(sun(angle), m.rgb)` with no hit test, where `sun = mix(sunColor·max(0, cos t)^4, skyColor, 0.3)` for `t = mod(angle − sunAngle, TAU)`; with Naive GI Noise on, the old `smoothstep(1, 0, t)` sun with part 1's colours applies instead. Noise is `rand(uv·(k + 1))`, up to one ray step. Output is `acc.rgb` above the displayed cascade and `pow(acc.rgb, 1/srgb)` at it, alpha kept. Paint: alpha 1 texels occlude and emit (linearized when `srgb` is 2.2); alpha 0.5 to 0.99 occlude with zero radiance; erased texels are not seeds and do not occlude. The overlay, at `rcScale` times the simulation size with linear filtering, shows the high-resolution paint where `highRes.a > 0` only when Layer to Render is 0, and the cascade output otherwise; the display plane draws the brush indicator on top.

**Final demo: precision and cost.** Targets default to half float: a `#full-precision` checkbox referenced in the code is missing from the page, so the half-float path is always taken, except flood targets above 1024 px on non-mobile. No timing is measured: every demo constructs `GPUTimer(renderer, true)`, which disables the timer extension, and no hardware is named. The page's ways to lower cost are 2 or more pixels between probes, `rcScale` above 1, `pixelRatio` 1 and Reduce Demand. Full mode casts `b·E²` rays per cascade, 1,048,576 at the defaults and about 6.3 M per frame over six cascades (derived). The article's claims, all unmeasured: 16 RC rays cost about as much as 16 naive rays and give no noise, the two-pass design costs 32 raymarches per pixel, the linear-filter upscale is nearly free, and some ringing fixes double frame time.

**Article against code.** Part 2's text and final code differ in ten places. The interval modifier is `base < 16 ? 1.0 : √base` in the article, `base < 16 ? P : √base` in the final code and `base < 16 ? 1 : 4` in the multipass demo. The merge clamp is `[0.5, upperSize − 0.5]` in the article, `[1, upperSize − 1]` in the code and absent in V2. Cascade 0 starts at 0 in the article and at `1·base^−1` (0.25 px at base 4) in the code. The article's snippet encodes the output when `cascadeIndex > firstCascadeIndex`, the code when `cascadeIndex ≤ firstCascadeIndex` (the display cascade), opposite conditions. The snippet sets alpha to 1, the final `rc-fragment` keeps `acc.a` and the multipass demo forces 1. The final code applies the sun to every top-cascade ray with no non-opaque test, the multipass demo only to non-opaque rays. The article loops from `cascadeCount − 1` and the multipass demo from `cascadeCount`, whose extra top pass is never merged because the merge condition is false for both. The final code counts cascades in log base 4 whatever the base ray count, the multipass demo in the chosen base, with the shorter side for the slider limit and the diagonal in `rcPass`. The penumbra text says the low-resolution pass starts at `partial` where the code runs the high-ray pass from it. The article does not describe Reduce Demand.

**Gists.** The Markdown gist `0c72d185…` is an earlier version without "Packing Direction in Pixels", "Nailing the Details" and "Generalizing to Many Cascades", the overlap slider and term, and the 0.99 threshold, with the other values matching (32-ray naive demo, base slider `4^value`, the 512-ray example at 1024²). The gist `f71708c1…` (`.4k-rc.md`) has every section but still predates the overlap term and its 0.04 default, the 0.99 threshold and `SQRT_2 = 1.41`.

## radiance-cascades.com demo and Osborne's slide demo

The site's `public/js/rc.js` (2,237 lines) is one WebGL2 2D demo ([demo](../sources/2026_rc_community_site_and_wiki.md#demo-publicjsrcjs)). Its intervals, index math and merge are in [layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md#intervals).

Every sampler bound to a texture triggers `gl.generateMipmap` in `setUniform`, so mips regenerate on every render.

**Distance field.** Seeds store the UV where drawing alpha is above 0. `P = ceil(log2(max(W, H))) + 1` passes, 10 at 512² with offsets 512 down to 1, each reading the pixel and its 8 neighbours and keeping the non-empty seed nearest by squared distance; a value counts as empty when both components are at most 0, so a seed exactly at `(0, 0)` is empty, and a pixel with no seed stores `(−1, −1)`. The field is `min(1, |UV − seed|)` in UV units, 1 for `(−1, −1)`; the source says nothing on the approximation's accuracy.

**Probes and intervals.** Cascade 0 has `E = floor(W/p) × floor(H/p)` probes for `p` = `basePixelsBetweenProbes` = `2^e` (slider `e` from 0 to 4), and interval lengths scale by `M = modifierHack·rI·cI`, with `rI` the Interval Length slider (default 1.0) and `cI` fixed at 1.

**Ray march.** Sphere tracing with steps in pixels (`df·min(W, H)`), a hit at half a texel (`0.5·min(1/W, 1/H)`) returning `scene.rgb^srgb` with the scene alpha, and a miss `(0, 0, 0, 0)` once the travelled distance reaches the segment length or the position leaves `[0, 1]²`; there is no iteration cap.

**Sun, sky and sRGB.** Only with the sun button, only on the top cascade: `max(sky(θ), radiance)` per channel, hits included. The default sky is `mix(sunColor·I, skyColor, 0.3)` = `0.7·sunColor·I + 0.3·skyColor` with `I = max(0, cos φ)^4`, `φ = mod(θ − sunAngle, 2π)`, sun `(0.95, 0.9, 0.8)·3`, sky `(0.2, 0.24, 0.35)·4`; with Naive GI Noise on, the old `smoothstep(1.0, 0.0, φ)` sun with reversed edges and part 1's colours. The sun angle is in radians, inferred from the modulo by `TAU`. Hits are decoded with `rgb^srgb`, the cascade at Layer to Render is encoded with `1/srgb` and the ones above stay linear; with the checkbox off nothing converts, and the overlay never converts.

**Other bases** (derived at 512², overlap 0.1; base 4 is in [layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md#intervals)):

- **Resources.** `worldTexture` rgba16float (rgb premultiplied emission, alpha opacity); `worldAndBrushPreviewTexture` rgba16float with 10 mips, which the march reads, so the brush preview lights the scene unless disabled; `probeBuffer` of vec4f sized `2·maxLevel0Rays·16` bytes with `maxLevel0Rays = 2^6·(1024/2)²` = 16,777,216, so 512 MiB allocated at startup from the slider limits; `fluenceTexture` rgba8unorm, so the final image clamps at 1. rgba16float is chosen because multiplier-scaled radiance is averaged down the mip chain. A per-level uniform block holds 16 int32 fields (total rays, probe radius, ray count, level, level count, width, height, `maxLevel0Rays`, interval start and end radii, branching factor and five debug values) at a dynamic offset per level, with 10 levels reserved and a TODO noting it assumes an alignment of at most 256 bytes.
- **Index math.** `D_l = D0·2^l`, `W_l = 1024/D_l` probes per axis, `R_l = R0·2^(B·l)` rays; probe `p = row·W_l + col`, decoded as `col = p mod W_l`, `row = p div W_l`; ray slot `base_l + p·R_l + k` with `base_l = (l mod 2)·maxLevel0Rays`, so levels alternate between the buffer's halves; angle `2π(k + 0.5)/R_l` with direction `(cos, sin)` (the intro demos use `(sin, cos)` for the same angles); centre `(col·D_l + D_l/2, row·D_l + D_l/2)`; 256 threads per group, `id = gid.x + gid.y·256`, probe `id/R_l`, ray `id % R_l`.
- **Frame order.** Nothing runs unless a control changed, the mouse moved, clear was pressed or timing is on. A mouse drag paints a capsule into `worldTexture` (y flipped); the world is copied to the preview, the brush disc painted on it, and mips 1 to 9 built by 2×2 box averages; `ProbeAtlasRaymarch` runs once per level from the top down, each level its own compute pass; `BuildFluenceTexture` reads level 0 and `DebugWorldBlit` draws it.
- **Shaders.** `DebugWorldBlit` (full-screen triangle), `ProbeAtlasRaymarch` (compute, 256×1×1, with `DDACursorInit`, `RayMarchDDA`, `RayMarchFixedSize`, `AccumulateSample`, `SampleUpperProbe`, `SampleUpperProbes`, `ComputeMain`), `BuildFluenceTexture`, `WorldClear`, `WorldPaint` (a capsule via `SDFSegment`, used for world and preview) and `GenerateMipmapsBoxFilter`, all 16×16 except the march.
- **Per ray.** March `[S_l, E_l]` from `(0, 0, 0, 1)` to get `Lower`, read `Upper`, store `rgb = Lower.rgb + Lower.a·Upper.rgb`, `a = Lower.a·Upper.a`, with `S_0 = 0`, `E_l = I·2^(B·l)` and `S_l = E_(l−1)`. The top level's upper term is `(0, 0, 0, 1)`, with a TODO for sky or environment light.
- **Merge.** Lower ray `k` maps to upper rays `k·N … k·N + N − 1`, which fall inside its angular sector (derived from the angle formula). Upper probe index `f = P/D_(l+1) − 0.5`, base `floor(f)`, four probes each clamped to `[0, W_(l+1) − 1]`, which the source calls an intentional reuse of edge probes, with a ring of probes outside the scene as the planned fix; each probe value is the mean of its `N` rays at slots `base_(l+1) + p′·R_(l+1) + k·N + m` (`m < N`, `p′ = x + y·W_(l+1)`), and the four blend bilinearly with `w = fract(f)`.
- **March.** Fixed steps of `2^l·m` on mip `l` (or `m` on mip 0 without mips) from `P + d·S_l`, sampled at position/1024, until the distance from `P` passes `E_l` or the ray leaves the texture; or a DDA after the lodev.org raycasting tutorial in mip-level pixels that samples each visited cell at its corner, not its centre. Accumulation is `acc.rgb += acc.a·s.rgb` with `acc.a *= 1 − s.a`, or `acc.a *= exp(−s.a·decay)`; the step size is passed in and unused, so opacity does not scale with step length.
- **Fluence.** Each pixel takes the mean of its level 0 probe's rays, with the probe cell `floor(c/1024·W_0)` and no interpolation between probes; the direction debug view keeps only rays whose line passes within about a pixel, tested with `sdSegment` from a cited Shadertoy.
- **Read from the code.** The dispatch runs levels 0 to `levelCount` while the merge runs only for `l + 1 < levelCount`, so level `levelCount − 1` sees no upper term and level `levelCount` is computed and never read; the ray id ignores the x-group count once groups split into y, which matters past 65,535·256 = 16,776,960 rays, below the 16,777,216 maximum at level 0; the guard uses `>` instead of `>=`, processing one extra id; JavaScript's `<<` truncates the interval, so the label shows 6.76 while the ends are 6, 24, 96, 384, 1536 and 6144; with `B = 3` rays per level double, and at the maximum level 0 settings level 1 needs 33,554,432 slots in a half that holds 16,777,216, overrunning the buffer; the fluence bounds check uses the width for y, correct only on a square canvas. At the defaults the grids are 512 down to 16 per side with 4 to 4096 rays, 1,048,576 rays per level (derived).
- **Stated in the source.** Mapping the staging buffer for timings may take more than one frame; the debug UI prints per-pass milliseconds, but the page reports no timings and names no hardware.

## Otavio Peixoto, OPEngine

A 2D RC renderer inside the author's own C++/OpenGL engine, with a portfolio page (https://otaviopeixoto1.github.io/portfolio/2DRadianceCascades/) and code in `src/render/RadianceCascades/` and `data/shaders/RadianceCascades/2D/` of https://github.com/Otaviopeixoto1/OPEngine (repository from 2023-10, RC commits 2024-06-01 to 06-05, no license) ([Peixoto](../sources/2024_peixoto_opengine_2d_rc.md)). Light comes from mouse strokes; each cascade is a full-size square texture, rays sphere-trace a distance field, and cascades merge top-down with bilinear weights over four upper probes and an average of four upper rays. No timings, hardware or tested resolution are given; profiler scopes (March Cascades, Merge Cascades, Capture Mouse) exist with no recorded numbers.

**The page** says each cascade has 4× the rays and 4× fewer probes, with spacing doubling per axis, and that rays reach twice as far per cascade, while the code uses 4×. Probes must sit uniformly; merging takes the 4 nearest upper probes and the 4 upper rays sharing the ray's direction; the final pass sums cascade radiance per pixel; sharp shadows come out poorly. Its memory figure draws 4 cascades and its march figure 5. The README credits tmpvar's playground, fad's and Suslik's Shadertoys and the paper.

**Parameters and storage.** `MAX_CASCADES` 10, `c0Angular` 4 rays, `c0Interval` 4 px, `c0Spacing` 2 px. The region is square with side `R = max(W, H)`, storage side `S = floor(R/2)·√4`, equal to `R` for even `R`. The count `N = min(floor(log2(4·√2·R)/2) − 1, max(ceil(log2(S/2)), MAX_CASCADES))` uses `max` where a cap needs `min`, making 10 a floor of the second term, so the first term decides: `N = 5` at 1920×1080, 4 at 512². `N + 1` square RGBA16F textures with `GL_LINEAR`, one per cascade plus a ping-pong buffer, take 6 × 1920² × 8 B = 176.9 MB at 1920×1080, and two viewport-sized RGBA16F distance textures 33.2 MB.

**Layout.** Position-first: cascade `i` has `4^(i+1)` rays (4, 16, 64, 256, 1024) in a `2^(i+1)`-texel block per probe, probe `floor(texel/probeSize)`, ray index `ty·probeSize + tx` row-major, spacing `2^(i+1)` px, so 960, 480, 240, 120 and 60 probes per axis at 1920 and `S²` texels in every cascade. Ray angle `θ = 2π(rayIndex + 0.5)/rayCount`, direction `(cos θ, −sin θ)`; probe UV `(probePos + 0.5)·spacing/(W, H)`; rays start at `probeUV + dir·minimum/R`.

**Intervals.** Raw `minimum_i = 4·(4^i − 1)/3` and `range_i = 4·4^i`: `[0, 4], [4, 20], [20, 84], [84, 340], [340, 1364]` px. An overlap adjustment `minimum −= (range_i/4)·sign(i − 1)` then gives `[1, 4], [4, 20], [4, 84], [20, 340], [84, 1364]`: cascade 0 starts 1 px out, and from cascade 2 up each cascade starts where the one below starts, re-tracing the lower interval from coarser probe positions. Since the merge runs only where the lower ray missed, a hit inside that re-traced range, seen from the upper probe, can reach the result after the lower ray missed it (derived). The top reaches 1364 px, short of the 2716 px diagonal at 1920×1080.

**Tracing.** Sphere tracing in UV: `rt = range/R`, each step adds the field value, the sample is `start + dir·min(rd, rt)`; a miss is `rd ≥ rt` or leaving `[0, 1)²`, a hit `d < 1e-4` returning the emission with alpha 1. The loop cap `ii < range` gives 3, 16, 80, 320 and 1280 iterations for cascades 0 to 4, sized as if each step covered a pixel; sphere-tracing steps shrink near surfaces, so a ray can stop before its interval ends.

**Merge.** The loop runs `i = N − 1` down to 1 with cascade `i` as the upper input (the raw top, then the previous step's buffer); the uniform `cascadeIndex` is `i − 1`, the cascade written, though the renderer's comment calls it the upper index. Hits (alpha 1) are skipped. Upper base index `floor((p − 1)/2)` with weight `0.25 + 0.5·(p − (2·base + 1))`, 0.25 or 0.75, the correct bilinear weights for upper centres at `(2j + 1)·spacing`. For each of 4 upper probes the shader averages upper rays `4·rayIndex + k`, `k = 0..3`, blends the four bilinearly, adds the result and sets alpha 1: 16 upper reads per texel, with no pre-averaging pass.

**Final pass.** `probePos = sceneCoord/2 − 0.5`; the 4 cascade 0 rays of each of the 4 nearest probes are averaged with equal weights and the probes blended bilinearly. There is no cosine term and no 2π, so the output is mean radiance.

**Distance field.** Not a jump flood: each stroke is a quadratic Bézier from mid(A, B) through B to mid(B, C) with distance `sdBezier(uv) − brushRadius`, updated as `sd = min(d, sd)` where `d < max(0, sd)`, the emission taking the brush colour. Both buffers clear to `1e20`, which RGBA16F (maximum 65504) stores as +inf, and the tracer relies on inf acting as a far distance. Distances are in UV, not pixels.

**Read from the code.** `FetchUpperCascade` and `CascadeFetch` turn integer texel coordinates into UV without the +0.5 texel-centre offset, and the y term `rayIndex/probeSize` has no floor, so with `GL_LINEAR` the reads should land between texels and blend neighbouring rays and probes (a capture would confirm it). Probe positions divide by `(W, H)` but lengths by `max(W, H)`, so at 1920×1080 a UV unit is 1920 px in x and 1080 px in y and lengths are right only on square viewports. At 1920×1080, 960 × 540 of cascade 0's 960 × 960 probes are on screen, so 43.75% of every cascade is off screen, which a comment says is intended.

## Théo Le Goc (tlegoc), Vulkan

A blog post (https://tlegoc.github.io/blogs/radiance-cascade/, 2024-10-22) and C++/Slang code (https://github.com/tlegoc/RadianceCascadesGIVulkan, last commit 2024-11, no license) for a 2D Vulkan compute version on a screen-space SDF in RGBA32F, colour plus distance ([blog posts](../sources/2024_rc_blog_posts_and_slides.md#tlegoc-vulkan-blog-post)). The author first tried 3D with screen-space marching in Unity and moved to 2D to learn the method. The cascade count and the top cascade's vertical probe count are fixed and the texture width stretches to the screen, keeping the layout symmetric at any aspect ratio with extra probes on the sides. All cascades trace in parallel, then merge top-down one dispatch at a time, each waiting on the one before; the merge reads the 4 nearest rays of each nearby upper probe, averages them and interpolates bilinearly across probes. It crashes when rays are too long or the march step too small and shows artifacts at low resolution. Read from the code: three of four bilinear corners get the wrong weights, `output += final·output.a` also changes alpha, skipped edge taps still count toward the divisor of 4 (darkening edges), and a sign-keeping `%` wraps `index − 1` at index 0 into the previous probe block ([assorted 2D](../sources/2026_assorted_2d_rc_experiments.md#bugs-worth-recognizing)).

## SimonDev's demo

The code behind SimonDev's video (https://github.com/simondevyoutube/Shaders_RadianceCascades, JS and GLSL, MIT, 2024-07), which the video does not link; the video's own content is in [history](history_and_landscape.md) ([assorted 2D](../sources/2026_assorted_2d_rc_experiments.md#simondev-demo)). Plain RC with 7 levels, 4 directions per cascade 0 probe ×4 per level, intervals `(4^L − 1)/3` to `(4^(L+1) − 1)/3` scene pixels, and every level in one target of the same size, so rays per level are constant.

- **Scene.** A lazy-brush pen paints a 1920×1080 distance field whose texels hold colour and distance; the march sphere-traces up to 64 steps, hits below 0.1 px, and returns alpha 0 for a hit and 1 for an escape.
- **Sizes.** The cascade target is `1024·16/9` = 1820.44 texels wide, a non-integer size, by 1024. The level count comes from a loop that stops at the first level whose interval end passes the screen diagonal (5461 > 2203 px gives 7), overriding an earlier formula.
- **Layout.** Probe `P`, angle `a` sits at texel `(dims·Px + a mod dims, dims·Py + floor(a/dims))` with `dims = 2^(n+1)`, and the angle is `a·2π/dims² − π/dims²`, half a step back from the bin centre.
- **Merge.** The upper lookup averages four angular taps at `index − 1 … index + 2`, centred half a bin off the child range `4k … 4k + 3`; several helpers are marked "fixme". The bilinear probe set is chosen from `pixel − dims/2` while its weights are measured from centres at `dims·k + (dims − 1)/2`, a half-texel mismatch that wraps the fraction in a narrow band at probe boundaries (found by reading). Probes outside the texture return `(0, 0, 0, 0)`, which reads as opaque and stops the merge; `index − 1` at index 0 wraps into the previous probe block. A 13-tap merge option calls a function that does not exist.
- **Output.** The radiance field averages cascade 0's 4 directions per probe with no cosine term; the composite multiplies a background mixed in Oklab by the radiance and applies ACES with a 1/2.2 power.

## Connections

- **One lineage, three rewrites.** GM Shaders' Part One follows fad's Shadertoy; jason.today names GM Shaders as its main influence; the community demo and Osborne's slide demo are both a port of jason.today's final demo with a new interval formula; Sohojoe's Godot branch `mix-Yaazarai-and-jason` merges two of them ([2D implementations](2d_implementations.md#godot)). The teaching codes form one chain, so they share choices: one pass per cascade from the top, direction-first tiles, a single hardware-filtered merge fetch, sRGB handled by a 2.2 power, sky on the top cascade only (inference, from the sections above).
- **Shared hacks travel with the code.** `modifierHack` appears in jason.today (`1`, then `P`, against `√B` above base 16), the multipass demo (a literal 4) and rc.js; the reversed-smoothstep old sun appears in all three; the `+1` cascade from jason.today's top-left light is in rc.js and Osborne's demo. Each was a local fix for one artifact, kept without explanation ([artifacts](artifacts_and_fixes.md#base-ray-counts-other-than-4)).
- **Article and code disagree in every series.** GM Shaders' posts and repository disagree on the visibility convention, the leak term and comments; jason.today's text and code differ in ten listed places in part 2 and nine in part 1; Peixoto's page says ×2 intervals where the code uses ×4. Reading the code, not the prose, gives the method each demo actually runs.
- **Off-by-one levels recur.** jason.today's multipass loop starts one pass above the top, tmpvar's dispatch computes one level that is never read, and Peixoto's overlap adjustment re-traces a whole lower interval. Each wastes a full cascade of work without changing the image, which is why none was noticed (inference).
- **Normalization varies.** jason.today and rc.js average rays (mean radiance), Peixoto and SimonDev average without a cosine, tmpvar averages one probe's rays without interpolation, and GM Shaders divides by the ray count; none multiplies by 2π, so their brightness is comparable to each other and about 2π below fluence outputs ([layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md#final-gather)).
- **Opacity per sample.** tmpvar's playground ignores the step size, the same flaw as wfyyDz and knowdawg ([2D implementations](2d_implementations.md#scene-representations)).

## Single-frame assessment

For single-frame 3D RC (inference, from the sections above):

- Every teaching demo computes its cascades from scratch each frame; the temporal parts (part 1's accumulation, GM Shaders' bounce feedback, jason.today's Reduce Demand) are optional, and jason.today's Reduce Demand never even reads its first frame. The method needs no history in 2D.
- The demos' cost claims are unmeasured: jason.today disables its GPU timer and tmpvar prints timings without recording any. Their value for a 3D design is the pipeline structure, not numbers; GM Shaders' stress test is the only measurement among them ([performance](performance.md)).
- jason.today's two-pass step shows the core economy in its simplest form: 32 raymarches per pixel reach 256 directions. In 3D the same split is what keeps per-pixel cost bounded while upper cascades resolve distant lights.
- The tile-and-single-fetch merge that all these demos share is the cheapest merge, but tmpvar's bilinear-of-means and Peixoto's 16-read merge show the explicit forms a 3D merge would need where hardware filtering cannot cross tile boundaries ([layout](cascade_layout_and_merging.md#merge-kernels)).
- Edge handling matters more in 3D, where the share of off-screen or out-of-volume probes grows: Peixoto keeps 43.75% of every cascade off screen, and tmpvar clamps edge probes and plans an outside ring.
