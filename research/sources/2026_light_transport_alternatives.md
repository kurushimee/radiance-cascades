This note covers methods that compete with RC or extend it beyond diffuse light: line sweeping, short characteristics, cellular automata and cone sweeping, transform-based solutions without occlusion, wave optics, extended gathering, caustics and refraction, multi-bounce, and RC used for path guiding. Most of it is 2D research; the parts with direct use in a 3D renderer are extended gathering, multi-bounce feedback and the guiding ideas.

## Line sweeping

- A sweep marches all parallel rays of one direction across the grid at once, so one march serves every pixel it passes and costs O(1) per pixel per direction. It needs only two columns of `N` directions in flight. Sweeps suit a uniform screen grid better than a BVH.
- **Measured:** 1000 directions at 1080p in 40 ms after removing modulo operations, which had used about 60 of 150 ALU instructions; on an M2 Pro, 1024 sweeps at 1080p took about 200 ms with modulo, 50 ms without and 27 ms at the highest GPU clock. A 13 ms budget allows about 325 directions. Dispatching one thread per row ran at 105 fps against 70 for columns.
- **With RC:** RC line sweeping was competitive with but slightly slower than a multi-level DDA. It was faster on higher cascades, while cascades 0 to 2 were better traced normally. Sweeps cannot use the nearest or bilinear fix because rays are not per probe; the parallax fix works. Skewed probe grids per direction avoid the parallax error of misaligned probes.
- **Volumetrics:** a sweep cannot stop at one interval length, so finite intervals need a stack of samples (about 600 MB estimated for 2× RC) or prefix sums of additive optical depth. Volumetric sweeps handle opaque geometry poorly.
- **In 3D:** bucket fragments by world-space line and sort along it, which gives the ray-march result with no extra memory; only the first hit matters. Sparse sweeps visit only non-empty voxels.
- **Sunlight:** a single sweep is faster than a second RC pass for a directional light.
- **Grid visibility recurrence** for a point source without volumetrics: `T·(x·r[x−1] + y·r[y−1])/(x + y)`, an O(N²) wave from the source with partial transmittance allowed and `1/r` applied separately ([article](https://towardsdatascience.com/a-quick-and-clear-look-at-grid-based-visibility-bf63769fbc78), [notes](2026_sweep_and_short_characteristics_solvers.md#grid-based-visibility-goldstein)). From O(N) boundary points this gives full GI in O(N³). In the article the cell's own value plays the part of `T`: `V(x, y) ← V(x, y)·[x·V(x − 1, y) + y·V(x, y − 1)]/(x + y)` on a grid of 0 (blocked) and 1 (free), which converges to exact visibility as the grid is refined and equals a count of unblocked shortest grid paths.
- Line-sweep ambient occlusion on height maps is O(1) per ray with infinite thickness ([LSAO](http://wili.cc/research/lsao/lsao.pdf)), and rasterizing slices into height maps was faster than a DDA. LSAO itself sweeps the screen-space depth buffer, costs O(K) per pixel for K directions with unlimited radius, and ran 1920×1080 in 1.7 to 5.2 ms on a GTX 480 ([notes](2013_timonen_line_sweep_ambient_obscurance.md)).

## Short characteristics

Radiative-transfer codes solve the same problem with 45° diamond-shaped sweep fronts, one global write per pixel ([Kripke report](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1559411), [MURaM solver](https://github.com/NCAR/MURaM_main/blob/main/src/rt/rt.cc), [lecture](https://www.astro.uu.se/~piskunov/TEACHING/NUM_RT/nrt_lecture05.pdf)). In the linked sources the fronts are hyperplane wavefronts from the upwind corner (diagonal in 2D) in Ardra and Kripke, while MURaM sweeps axis-aligned planes in order with parallel work inside each plane; the OSTI link is the Ardra CUDA paper, not Kripke's ([notes](2026_sweep_and_short_characteristics_solvers.md)). Atomics are nearly free in a sweep when lines do enough other work. An uncascaded sweep wastes far-field work, and wide cones keep the direction count low.

## Cellular automata and cone sweeping

- **Cone sweeping:** the endpoints of a 45° cone move one pixel per step, which makes it an O(1) per pixel cellular automaton. Cascade 0 tracks one occluder per pixel, cascade 1 two, cascade 2 four. Two test shaders show the pieces without cascades: [7c2XDw](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/7c2XDw) sweeps a scan column across the scene with two 45° edge carriers per source, and [7f2SzV](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/7f2SzV) diffuses light one column per frame with a three-tap box kernel whose cone half-angle the mouse sets; neither has angular falloff or a Gaussian term ([code notes](2023_rc_shadertoys.md#7c2xdw-and-7f2szv-suslik-sweeping-and-diffusion-tests)). [fld3R4](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/fld3R4), linked with them, is paniq's 2D light propagation volume with circular harmonics, not a sweep ([code notes](2023_rc_shadertoys.md#fld3r4-paniq-2d-light-propagation-volume)). A swept-light demo took 481 ms on Iris Xe and 78 ms on an RTX A2000 ([demo](https://swept-radiance.pages.dev/)).
- **Diffusion automaton:** `p[x+1, y] = 0.8·p[x, y] + 0.1·p[x, y−1] + 0.1·p[x, y+1]` gives penumbras; repeated convolution converges to a Gaussian.
- Cellular-automaton GI implementations checked stored only 4 to 6 angles, which is not conservative and leaks where steep cone borders skip tiles. Voxel versions with 2-bit directions give 90° shadows ([video](https://youtu.be/wVkhP67P2sk?si=lU0rayMB7kyutOd8)). The linked video is EdBillz, "Solving Lighting Using Voxel Cellular Automata" (2025-07-10, 4:42, no captions), whose description says only that it is GI by cellular automata in a voxel engine written in OpenGL and C; the 2-bit directions and 90° shadows come from the discussion, not the video's text.

## Solutions without occlusion

- Without occlusion, 2D GI is one convolution of the emission with a `1/r` kernel, exact with an FFT in O(n log n); RC is also O(n log n) but not exact. Accurate AO is therefore most of the GI problem.
- The same result is `DualRadon(Radon(sources))` by the projection-slice theorem ([prototype](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/fXfXRS)). A naive O(N³) transform ran in real time on Shadertoy; the inverse dual Radon took about 5 s in Mathematica against a 5 ms target. FFT periodicity causes lens-flare artifacts, so pad with zeros. Sinograms can hold off-screen sources.
- An FFT Poisson solve cannot take internal barriers, and occlusion is nonlinear, so no transform encodes it exactly. Analytic conformal maps give exact unoccluded fluence (a line light is `ln((z − p)/(z − q))`), but continuing it from a boundary is ill-conditioned ([demo](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/7cBGDK)).
- A line light contributes `cos θ/r`, not a sum of point lights, and a finite source falls off as `atan(d/r)/π`.

## Wave optics

- Wave-based GI scales as N² log N in 2D with an FFT, and N³ log N is not enough in 3D, where cost should follow surface area. Sharp shadows need tiny wavelengths. A 2D point source is a Hankel function; the infinite-wavelength limit is the Poisson problem.
- **Wave Cascades**, a wave-optics extension of RC for Lorenz–Mie scattering and diffraction, computed a fully spectral Mie lookup table in real time and sampled it with a 64³ equal-angle HRC. No paper, preprint, video, post or repository under that name, and no RC with a Mie table, could be found on the web, GitHub or arXiv (2026-10); the HRC paper has no wave-optics or spectral content. The claim stays unsourced.

## Extended gathering

- Hits in one direction are spread to neighboring probe cones with a constant-width ramp, since all hits fall in a known distance interval: `L(P, d) = lerp(L(A, d_i), L(B, d_{i+1}), α)` with `A = P + d·r − d_i·r`. Each gathered direction uses its own interpolated source position, and with 4× scaling the probe count per cascade stays the same. It needs spatial resolution above angular resolution and smears only across the propagation direction, so occlusion along the cone survives ([NfVXRc](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/NfVXRc) gathers 200 directions per pixel from a single cascade of 8-direction probes; [sfVSzd](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/sfVSzd) applies the same angular smoothing to each stored texel; neither merges cascades, see the [code notes](2023_rc_shadertoys.md#nfvxrc-and-sfvszd-suslik-extended-gathering)).
- Longer intervals cut spatial error at a fixed ray count while the gather compensates angular error; pick interval scaling so the two errors match.
- With analytic ray splitting, 2×2 extended gathering and 2×2 dithered jitter, it acts as a ray multiplier, and a 2×2 radius keeps contact shadows. World-space probes suited it better than screen probes; in screen space, interval length should make probes project to the same radius.

## Resampling view of RC

- Extensions (N probes and M directions to N/2 probes and 2M directions) and merges (to 2N probes and M/2 directions) are both reinterpolations of a radiance field. Resampling by integrating over each target's footprint, a trapezoid in line space, beat point sampling; stripe artifacts were fixed by oversampling a cascade. Lossless reinterpolation needs probe spacing to double per cascade.
- Primal and dual RC both capture radiance on a plane and rebuild it elsewhere; they alias only when the source or the receiver lies on the capture line.

## Caustics and refraction

- **Refractive cascades** in vanilla RC refract each interval at the boundary and continue tracing for the rest of its length, with a bilinear fix before and after refraction. Starting intervals at probe centers made bent merge lookups more sensible. Residual artifacts remained, and changing cascade 0 settings made earlier good results unphysical, which suggests luck in convergence. Merging rotates the upper probes by the lower probe's deflection ([Shadertoy port](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/f3d3R8)); 4 to 8 refraction bounces looked identical.
- **Equal-angle HRC** gives each frustum equal width in radians instead of equal slope spacing, which removes HRC crosses; one 2D build ran in 1.05 ms on an RTX 5090. Refraction across quadrant boundaries remained the error source, and ring HRC was judged better suited because bent rays break frustums.
- **3D:** dense equal-angle HRC with dispersion and Fresnel reflection, compared against 4k spp spectral path tracing, was called the first 3D RC caustics. Memory is the limit: 128³ took about 9 GiB through Vulkan, since WebGPU cannot allocate that much, and 32³ ran in 40 ms without accumulation on an RTX 4070 Ti Super. An adaptive topology that splits rays per cell by an error estimate, bounded to 1 to 2× splits, cleaned up a refractive sphere, but its memory was too high.
- Off-screen radiance for refraction needs adaptive, bounded allocation, and no conservative metric for lensing is known, so it does not look scalable in 3D.
- **Limits:** scattering doubles rays at every split, so cascades lose their constant size; caustics also break the angular-spatial trade-off that lets cascade 0 manage with 4 directions. HRC caustics in 2D were poor.
- **2D forward light tracing:** a uniform grid cut the ray-tracing pass from 165 ms to 3.5 ms. Uniform hemisphere sampling did better than cosine-weighted sampling for forward tracing in 2D, though metrics disagreed (FLIP mean 0.112 against 0.100). Most variance comes from rasterizing random lines, so stratifying lines matters more than better paths; Owen-scrambled Sobol cost about 8% more per path than white noise.

## Multi-bounce

- **Feedback:** feed last frame's radiance times albedo back as emission. Without a transmittance bound it explodes: one 256² test went from 65 to 5700 at albedo 0.9. Closed reflective boxes need decay or a reset of the temporal input. Emission should count only on the first bounce, and splitting floors and walls into two layers stabilized one build. Albedo 0.5 sums to twice the direct light after infinite bounces.
- **Without temporal lag:** stored ray hit distances allow `N` bounces in one frame without recasting rays, for opaque scenes. Bounces can also run as separate RC iterations, with Jacobi iteration or Anderson acceleration to converge faster.
- Path of Exile 2 shipped one extra indirect bounce for directly lit objects. One game found 3D RC too slow and used a two-bounce method instead.

## Guiding and next-event estimation

- **RC-guided NEE:** at each level, pick one of the four next-cascade cones by brightness, then trace inside the chosen cone, with a small uniform term. This resembles the published RC path guiding (see [2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md](2026_tauray_rcpg_path_guiding.md)).
- A light tree over emissive triangles built in under 0.5 ms for 10k to 100k triangles on an RTX 3090 with O(log N) sampling ([PBRT light BVH](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Sources/Light_Sampling#BVHLightSampling)). The build time comes from the discussion, not from PBRT. PBRT's sampler (sec. 12.6.3) bounds each node by its box, power `φ`, principal direction `w`, normal spread `cos θ_o`, emission spread `cos θ_e` and a two-sided flag, with infinite and distant lights sampled apart. A node's importance at point `p` with normal `n` is `φ·cos θ'/d²`, where `d² = max(|p − p_c|², |diagonal|/2)` keeps points inside the box finite, `θ' = max(0, θ_w − θ_o − θ_b)` with `θ_w` the angle between `w` and the direction to `p` and `θ_b` the angle the box subtends, zero when `cos θ' ≤ cos θ_e`, and times `cos max(0, θ_i − θ_b)` against the surface normal. The build splits by bucketed centroids at minimum cost `φ·M_ω·K_r·SurfaceArea`, with `M_ω` the solid-angle measure of the direction cone and `K_r = max(diagonal)/diagonal[dim]` penalizing thin boxes; leaves hold one light, nodes are 32 bytes with quantized bounds and an octahedral direction, two to a cache line. Sampling walks from the root choosing a child in proportion to its importance; each light's PMF comes from a stored bit trail of the children taken. On PBRT's Beeple scene it cut MSE by 2.37× for 5.8 % more time, a 2.25× efficiency gain, and the book says scenes with millions of lights render nearly as efficiently as one. About 70% of Path of Exile 2 shaders are emissive effects, so screen-space emissive pixels are the simplest light source there.
- RC wants many rays, while resampling methods want a few good ones, which is a tension in combining them.

## Other uses

- **Diffusion and Laplace solving** with HRC (see [2026_holographic_rc.md](2026_holographic_rc.md)), and cascades for pathfinding connectivity: an N³ build and log N queries against N⁴ for A*.
- **Radiative heat transfer** follows the same transport equation; thermal infrared suits RC because everything emits.
- **Audio attenuation**, **fog of war** (one channel per faction) and **spectral rendering** with one wavelength or a batch of four per frame were prototyped.

Vahteristo, Liu and Rupp (2025) reconstruct binary disks of the Helsinki Tomography Challenge 2022 from sinograms covering 100° down to 30°, by gradient descent through a differentiable Radon transform. The loss is the L1 distance between filtered sinograms, with filter `r_α(ω) = |(2/α)·sin(αω/2)|·[sin(αω/2)/(αω/2)]²` (a ramp filter for small α, low-pass for larger α), plus total variation and a patch autoencoder prior, and the image comes from a Deep Image Prior network with a ConvNeXt backbone; 8 images train the prior and 4 select hyperparameters. Ablation scores: full 3.55, without the prior network 3.07, without the filter 3.23, without TV or the patch prior 3.44. Per-level scores run from 2.90 at 100° to 2.17 at 30°, against 2.96 to 2.41 for the challenge winner, which trained on 200,000 synthetic images; the scores exceed the usual [−1, 1] range of the Matthews correlation they are said to be, so their aggregation is unclear. A reconstruction takes about 30 s on an RTX 3060. The link to light transport is the forward model: a line-integral operator observed from few directions, which is what a probe or line-space radiance store with few angles also is; the paper itself has no light transport.

The PII is Abe, Yajima, Umemura and Hoshi, "Adaptive angular resolution for time-dependent radiative transfer simulations based on local radiation field anisotropy" (JQSRT 352, 109820, 2026), about light in biological tissue, not multi-bounce rendering. Only the abstract was reachable: angular resolution varies with position according to the local anisotropy of the radiation field, measured with spherical Haar wavelet coefficients, on a polyurethane phantom; against constant angular resolution it is up to about 8× faster and 3× on average at the same accuracy, with memory down to about 20 % at most, and the gain grows as diffuse light spreads. Position-dependent angular resolution chosen from the field itself is the adaptive counterpart of RC's fixed per-cascade angular density.
- Radiance caching for participating media: https://cs.dartmouth.edu/~wjarosz/publications/jarosz08radiance.pdf (records with gradients, radius from the log-gradient, log-space extrapolation; [notes](2008_jarosz_radiance_caching_participating_media.md))
- Spectral sky with a SIMD trick: https://fgarlin.com/blog/spectral-sky/. García Liñán (2024, updated 2026) renders Rayleigh scattering and ozone absorption with four wavelengths per pixel, one SIMD lane each, and converts them to XYZ with one 3×4 matrix built from the Riemann sum `X = Σ_i x̄_i·L_i·w_i` (likewise Y and Z, over 390 to 780 nm). The four representative wavelengths and widths, eight parameters, are fitted by grid search and Nelder-Mead to an offline spectral path tracer with the mean squared error over view and sun angles, weighting sunrise and sunset so the fit does not settle on daylight blue; the fitted values are not given. The fit holds only for that atmosphere; aerosols are left out as nearly grey but position-dependent. The post claims a cost comparable to RGB and gives no error figures. For RC this is the quadrature a four-wavelength cascade would need, one wavelength per channel of an RGBA texture (inference).
- Linked as texture-space quad patches for surface probes: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09097. The paper is Viertel, Osting and Staten, "Coarse quad layouts through robust simplification of cross field separatrix partitions" (2019): it partitions CAD triangle meshes into four-sided regions by tracing cross-field separatrices and collapsing chords, reaching layouts without T-junctions on 92 of 100 surfaces. It has no texturing or lighting; a quad layout could only serve as a chart parameterization for surface probes (inference).
