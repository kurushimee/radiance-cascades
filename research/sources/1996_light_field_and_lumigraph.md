# Light field rendering and the Lumigraph

| Source | Authors | Date | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Lumigraph | Steven J. Gortler, Radek Grzeszczuk, Richard Szeliski, Michael F. Cohen | SIGGRAPH 1996, pp. 43-54 | https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~ravir/6160/papers/p43-gortler.pdf |
| Light Field Rendering | Marc Levoy, Pat Hanrahan | SIGGRAPH 1996 (corrected version, July 1996) | https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/light/ |

Both papers store radiance in free space as a 4D function of lines, each line given by its crossings of two parallel planes, and reconstruct images by interpolating that function. RC discussions cite them as the discretization behind holographic RC ([light transport alternatives](2026_light_transport_alternatives.md), [HRC](2026_holographic_rc.md)): an HRC frustum stores radiance per pair of points on two planes, the same two-plane line parameterization. Section numbers are the papers' own.

The plane names are swapped between the papers: the Lumigraph calls the camera plane `st` and the image plane `uv`, while Levoy and Hanrahan call the first plane `uv` and the second `st`.

## The Lumigraph (Gortler et al.)

- **Representation (sec. 2.1-2.2):** with transparent air, radiance is constant along empty rays, so the plenoptic function reduces to 4D on the surface of a convex hull. A cube encloses the object, and each face is a pair of parallel planes: `st` at `z = 1`, `uv` at `z = 0` with the origin at its center. A ray is the tuple `(s, t, u, v)` of its two crossings, and the full Lumigraph uses six pairs with normals along ±x, ±y and ±z. A straight eye path along `st` moves the projection of a fixed surface point along straight lines in `uv`, the optical flow that depth correction uses.
- **Discretization (sec. 2.3):** `M` subdivisions in `s` and `t` and `N` in `u` and `v`; `L̃(s, t, u, v) = Σ x_ijpq·B_ijpq(s, t, u, v)` over `i, j = 0..M` and `p, q = 0..N`. The chosen basis is quadralinear: 1 at its grid point and 0 at its neighbours, so each sample comes from the 16 grid points of its hypercube. It is C0 but not band-limited to Nyquist, so the grid is slightly visible (sec. 2.3.1).
- **Projection (eq. 1):** in the L2 metric `x_ijpq = ⟨L, B̃_ijpq⟩`, the inner product with the dual basis, which equals point sampling after low-pass filtering with `B̃`. For the box basis `B = B̃`; for the quadralinear basis the dual is approximated by the basis itself.
- **Resolution (sec. 2.3.3):** `N` near the final image resolution (128 to 512 tested), `M` much lower (16 to 64), because the surface seen in `uv` changes little across `st`. Radiance needs fine spatial resolution on the image plane and only coarse sampling of the viewpoint, which is the same kind of asymmetry that lets RC trade positions against directions (inference).
- **Depth correction (eq. 2, sec. 2.3.4):** with a proxy depth `z` (0 at `uv`, 1 at `st`), `u' = u + (s − s_i)·z/(1 − z)` and `v' = v + (t − t_j)·z/(1 − z)`, and `B'_ijpq(s, t, u, v) = B_ijpq(s, t, u', v')`; the weights still sum to 1. The paper's pseudocode loops over the four `(s_i, t_j)`, corrects `(u, v)` per corner, then weights the four `(u_p, v_q)` by `(h_uv − |u_p − u'|)(h_uv − |v_q − v'|)/h_uv²` and the corner by `(h_st − |s_i − s|)(h_st − |t_j − t|)/h_st²`. The equation is garbled in text extraction; the pseudocode and a similar-triangle derivation agree on `z/(1 − z)`. This is a near-field correction: a coarse depth proxy shifts the lookup so interpolation between viewpoints lands on the same surface point, the problem parallax-corrected RC merges also solve (inference).
- **Capture (sec. 3.1-3.3):** synthetic data from a pinhole camera at each `(s_i, t_j)` with a skewed frustum onto `uv`; real data from a hand-held camera with Tsai calibration against a 30-marker stage (about 0.5 s per frame on an SGI Indy) and blue-screen silhouettes. An octree visual hull (typically 4 subdivisions) with Taubin smoothing gives the depth proxy.
- **Rebinning (sec. 3.4):** scattered samples become grid coefficients in three phases. Splat (eq. 4): `w_i⁰ = Σ_k B̃_i(s_k)` and `x_i⁰ = (1/w_i⁰)·Σ_k B̃_i(s_k)·L(s_k)`, undefined where `w = 0`. Pull (eq. 5): coarser levels with `h̃ = {1/2, 1, 1/2}`, `w^{r+1}_i = Σ_k h̃_{k−2i}·w^r_k` and `x^{r+1}_i = (1/w^{r+1}_i)·Σ_k h̃_{k−2i}·w^r_k·x^r_k`, with `min(w^r_k, 1)` in both sums after Mitchell, so dense regions do not dominate. Push: each coarser level fills the gaps of the finer one, keeping fine coefficients with weight at least 1 and blending lower weights with the coarse estimate. This is the pull-push scheme, usually credited to this paper, for filling gaps between sparse samples.
- **Compression (sec. 3.5):** a face of 32×32 `(s, t)` and 256×256 `(u, v)` at 24 bpp over six faces is `32²·256²·6·3` bytes, 1.125 GB. Per-image JPEG gives about 20:1, and inter-image prediction was expected to give about 200:1 (under 6 MB); the results are preliminary.
- **Rendering (sec. 3.6):** by ray, compute `(s, t, u, v)` per pixel and interpolate 16 grid points, with depth from a proxy render whose vertex colours are positions. By texture mapping, each `st` grid point owns a texture `tex_ij(u_p, v_q) = x_ijpq`: the constant basis draws at most `M²` textured squares, and the quadralinear basis draws at most `6M²` alpha-blended triangles that approximate the bilinear `st` weights with a hexagonal pyramid summing to one (Fig. 15c). Depth correction is exact at triangle vertices and approximate inside; triangles subdivide adaptively by screen size or depth variation.
- **Results (sec. 4):** capture under an hour with an analog camera; processing under a day on an SGI Indy; ray-by-ray rendering a few seconds per 450×450 frame; texture-mapped rendering several frames per second on an SGI Reality Engine, nearly independent of output resolution, limited by texture-cache transfers. Only one cube face was captured, and compression and error reduction are future work.

## Light Field Rendering (Levoy and Hanrahan)

- **Definition (sec. 2):** the light field is radiance as a function of position and direction, 4D in free space. A light slab parameterizes lines by a point `(u, v)` on a first plane and `(s, t)` on a second, all in [0, 1]. The map from `(u, v)` to its plane, and from pixel `(x, y)` to `(u, v, s, t)`, is projective (a 3×3 matrix, with `u = u_w/w`). One plane can sit at infinity, giving point-plus-direction lines.
- **Tiling line space (sec. 2, Figs. 2-4):** one slab cannot hold every line through a convex hull, so several slabs tile line space, and sampling density in line space should be uniform. Planes meeting at a corner sample poorly near the singularity, so parallel planes or a plane at infinity are preferred. The paper measures density in `(r, θ)` line coordinates, where equal areas mean equal density. RC's per-cascade direction sets face the same question of uniform line-space sampling, and HRC's frustums are slabs that tile the directions in four parts (inference).
- **Creation (sec. 3.1, Fig. 5):** each `uv` sample is an image rendered with a sheared perspective so its pixels align with `st`.
- **Prefiltering (sec. 3.1, Figs. 7-8):** the ideal sampling filter is a 4D box in line space: a pixel filter in `st` and an aperture filter in `uv`, which a camera focused on `st` with an aperture equal to the `uv` spacing provides. That aperture turns aliasing (images jumping by the stereo disparity) into blur whose circle of confusion equals the disparity; too small or too large an aperture leaves gaps or overlaps in line space. This is the light-field form of the ringing trade-off in RC: undersampled directions either alias or, when prefiltered, blur (inference).
- **Acquisition (sec. 3.2, Table II):** a four-axis gantry (x, y, pan, tilt), a 10-120 mm zoom locked at 10 mm, f/8, four 90° slabs for 360°. Camera spacing was 3.125 cm against a 1.25 mm aperture, so the aperture problem is acknowledged, not solved. About 3 s per image and 4 h for the lion.
- **Compression (sec. 4, Table III):** fixed-rate vector quantization of 2D (12-dimensional) or 4D (48-dimensional) tiles at rate `k·l/log₂N`, which for `k = 48`, `l = 8`, `N = 16384` is 384/14 ≈ 27:1, or 24:1 with 2-byte indices; the codebook is trained on a subset and adds under 20 %. Gzip on the indices gives about 5:1 more. Decoding is random access (tile index, then codebook lookup) and took about 25 % of CPU time. Buddha (50.3 MB raw) reached 45:1 at 36 dB PSNR (15 min training, 1 min encoding); lion (402.7 MB, 2×2×2×2 tiles, 16384 codewords) 118:1 at 27 dB (4 h training, 8 min encoding). Artifacts become objectionable only above about 200:1 (sec. 6). The paper notes that a plain 4D block code compresses well because the scene's 3D structure is already in the coherence.
- **Display (sec. 5):** compute line parameters by the projective map, then resample with quadralinear interpolation and no band-pass prefilter; 4D mipmapping is suggested for small images. Fig. 13 compares nearest, `uv`-linear and `uvst`-linear.
- **Performance (Table IV):** lion at 192×192 on a 250 MHz MIPS R4400 in software: 30 ms without interpolation, 75 ms with `uv`-linear and 230 ms with `uvst`-linear. Playback cost depends on image size, not light field size.
- **Datasets (Table I):** buddha 1 slab, 16×16 images of 256², 50 MB; kidney 1 slab, 64×64 images of 128², 201 MB; hallway 4 slabs, 8192 images of 256², 1608 MB; lion 4 slabs, 2048 images of 256², 402 MB.
- **Limitations (sec. 7):** sampling must be dense; the observer must stay in free space (stitching convex regions or adding depth are suggested); lighting is fixed (storing normals and materials is suggested); interreflection could come from superposing light fields lit by basis illuminations.

## For RC

- Both papers sample radiance per line between two planes and interpolate quadralinearly, which is HRC's storage and, in 2D, its bilinear interpolation between plane points. Neither builds a hierarchy over line-space resolution; that hierarchy is what RC adds (inference).
- The Lumigraph's coarse `st` and fine `uv` sampling, and the depth correction that keeps a coarse viewpoint grid usable, carry the same idea as the penumbra condition and parallax-corrected merges: angular sampling can be coarse where a depth estimate fixes the parallax (inference).
- The memory figures (hundreds of MB to over 1 GB per object at 1996 resolutions) show why a full 4D radiance store cannot be dense, and why RC keeps its constant per-cascade size by trading resolution between space and angle (inference).
