## Love and Deepspace: surfel RC on mobile

The only shipped 3D RC found. The game (Unity 2019 with a custom pipeline, over 80 million players) lets players place furniture and follows real-world time of day, so GI must be dynamic, need no baking and run on phones without hardware ray tracing. It went through three versions:

1. SH-warped irradiance volumes with baked probes: cheap, but lost small-angle detail and needed baked material data.
2. Simplified ReSTIR with 8 steps of screen-space marching plus marching through probe data for offscreen hits, denoised with BMFR (chosen over SVGF because each pixel reads only its own features). It looked much better but cost 3.2 ms on an iPhone 12, ghosted after big changes, and still needed hand-placed probes.
3. Surfel radiance cascades, shipped.

### Scene representation

- Each mesh asset gets surfels (position, normal, radius about 10 cm, four LOD levels) when it loads. They are generated by rendering the mesh into its lightmap UVs and spawning one surfel per texel, then thinning and respawning until coverage converges. Positions are stored in local space.
- Surfel IDs live in a multi-level hash grid keyed by cell position and LOD (prime table size 51,481, about 19 surfels per cell). Inserting or removing a surfel only checks its own cell.
- Rays march the hash grid, then test ray-disk intersections in each cell they cross. Ray-disk tests are cheaper than ray-triangle tests, and software BVH traversal of triangles was too slow on mobile.
- A coarse surfel BVH is rebuilt on the GPU every 4 frames, used for an early out on rays from probes that sit on the ground or high in the air.

### Cascades and probes

- World-space probes in a three-level camera-following clipmap, 32×32×16 probes per level, about 1 m apart at level 0, doubling per level, about 64 m reach.
- Each probe's radiance is split by distance into cascades: probe count ÷8 per cascade (spacing ×2 per axis), directions ×4. Total storage is about twice cascade 0.
- Direction-first memory so neighboring probes for one direction sit together and hardware filtering works when probes are not occluded. Pre-averaging over 4 directions cuts memory above cascade 0 to about a quarter.
- Irradiance is stored as luma in SH and two chroma scalars, 16 bits per channel, as in Assassin's Creed Shadows.
- Ray tracing is spread across frames, and each ray's hit distance is cached after its path completes so the next trace starts at the right hash grid. The cache scrolls with the camera. The talk says this nearly removes the ray tracing cost.

### Artifacts and fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Light leaks through gaps between surfels | Each surfel gets a transparent outer ring. A hit counts if it lands on an inner disk once or on outer rings several times; overlapping hits blend albedo using four power moments of the hit points. |
| Indoor pixels read outdoor probes through trilinear filtering | Probes are tagged by indoor volume (one probe may have several tags) and the gather rejects mismatched types. Planned: movable occlusion planes on wall assets. |
| Cascade probes do not line up | Lower cascades overlap the higher ones. |
| All higher cascades occluded at a point | The lower cascade's ray length is raised temporarily. Rare in practice. |

### Gather and timing

- Each pixel reads 8 probes with trilinear weights and type rejection. At full resolution this took 2.8 ms on an iPhone 12, so it runs at about 70% resolution (about half the time and bandwidth) and is upscaled.
- Each bounce converges in 28 frames, or 5 frames in a fast mode used on scene entry and light switches.
- Total GI is about 2 ms on an iPhone 12. Low-end devices use baked probes with six time-of-day keyframes instead.

## GPC 2024 student showcase

A short explainer of 2D RC and a first look at 3D "screen probes, world intervals" in Vulkan compute (the same split as [Gavras](2025_gavras_screen_probes_world_intervals_thesis.md)). It compares branching: probe spacing ×2 with rays ×2 per cascade makes memory approach twice cascade 0, while rays ×4 keeps every cascade the same size. The 2D demo adds noise to the cascade interpolation for a painted look. No numbers.

## Enshrouded: a cascaded probe cache

Not RC, but a shipped world-space probe system with cascaded grids, SDF tracing and a fixed ray budget, built to run on a Steam Deck. Its practical tricks carry over to world-space RC probes.

- A "spatial cascaded cache": a 32³ world-aligned grid around the player, 12 cascades, 2 m spacing at cascade 0, doubling per cascade, with at most 16,000 active cells activated from the depth buffer and garbage-collected lazily. A 16³ ambient grid with 8 cascades covers distance; its last cascade is centered on the world as a fallback.
- Rays trace an SDF of the voxel world, the same on all hardware. Hits are shaded with emission, sun with a shadow ray, point lights from a light list per cell, and probe GI for further bounces.
- Probes that land in a wall are pushed out to the nearest SDF surface, then offset toward the camera and along the normal. A probe whose summed ray distance is under 1 mm is dead and skipped.
- A new probe starts with 512 rays and drops to 16 per frame, which only checks for change; activity raises it again. The whole budget is fixed per platform: 100k, 250k or 500k rays per frame.
- Rays are importance sampled from each probe's own radiance, with uniform sampling every 5 frames so the guide cannot starve directions. Uniform sampling alone flickered at 16 to 32 rays.
- Rays are sorted by position (8³ bins) and octahedral direction (4×4 bins), and hits are shaded by separate sky, terrain and building shaders, because mixed rays diverged badly.
- Storage in E5B9G9R9 (32 bits) to save bandwidth; occlusion moments in RGBA16F. Visibility uses a Chebyshev test on stored mean and squared distance, as in DDGI.
- Overlapping cascades blend stochastically: in the overlap zone the coarser cascade is picked with a probability that rises from 0 to 1.
- A probe on a surface that sees ground on one side and sky on the other queries the other side through its stored distances and moves its ray start.
- Known hole: trees are not in the SDF, so characters under trees look too bright.

- Blackline Interactive, "Real-Time Radiance Cascades Global Illumination 3D" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TTI1QoHlCHE) and "Radiance Cascades Global Illumination - without TAA, SSGI, DDGI..." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-fBe0ZIu39Q), 2026-10-03 (17:32) and 2026-09-12 (20:00): custom-engine demos without captions. The descriptions call the work real-time GI on Sannikov's RC approach with the author's own 3D architecture, experimental and not game-ready, aiming for zero light leaks and stable performance on older GPUs, and claim no TAA, SSGI, DDGI, voxels, lightmaps or denoising, "0 ms temporal latency" with no temporal accumulation or ghosting, FXAA 3, native resolution with no upscaling, and near path-traced quality. Scenes are Sponza (also with 4K textures and about 5.7M triangles), Amazon Bistro and a Cornell box; hardware is a 2019 16-inch MacBook Pro with a Radeon Pro 5500M (8 GB, 50 W) at Full HD and 2K, on Metal, with C++ and OpenGL in the hashtags. The code is unreleased, and no timings in ms, probe layout or engine name are given. These are the author's claims; nothing shown verifies them.
- Daniel Wright's SIGGRAPH 2021 radiance caching talk and SEED's surfel GI talk are not RC.
